Is it spring training yet? No — the pennants are being decided, and spring must wait its turn.

THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 101 · Saturday, the Seventeenth of October, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

No contests this day; the pennant races rest.

0 days until the fourth game of the American League's pennant round — the Rays at the Guardians, eight o'clock this very evening by the lake (Saturday, the 17th of October, 2026).

News Around the League

THE TARPAULIN DESK: THE SKY HOLDS ITS VETO OVER THE LAKE AND THE FOURTH GAME NEVER THROWS A PITCH — THE AMERICAN ROUND MOVES WHOLESALE TO TONIGHT AT EIGHT, SUNDAY SURRENDERS ITS DAY OF REST, AND THE DOME STANDS BY FOR MONDAY

The washout this paper feared only yesterday arrived on schedule and stayed past all bargaining. Rain settled over Progressive Field through Friday afternoon and declined every invitation to leave; the grounds crew swept standing water from the infield tarpaulin and chased the pools collecting on the warning track; a party of Tampa Bay pitchers emerged to play catch near the appointed hour and promptly thought better of it; and late in the evening the league called the thing off. "It never cleared out the way they were hoping it would," said Kevin Cash, who allowed that the league and both clubs "did everything they could to try to get it in." His opposite number took the longer view of local meteorology. "This is baseball. This is Cleveland," said Stephen Vogt. "We're more than used to these kinds of days."3

The week is accordingly redrawn, precisely as this paper warned it might be. The fourth game goes tonight at eight o'clock by the lake; the fifth follows Sunday at five o'clock, the scheduled day of rest stricken from the calendar; and should the argument require a sixth and a seventh, they wait at the Gulf Coast dome Monday and Tuesday nights.36 The standing of the quarrel is untouched by weather: the Lake Erie men lead it two games to one and may yet take the pennant without packing a trunk, while the Tampa men must win three of the four that remain. Even the ticket desk is shuffled with the games — Friday's stubs are good for Sunday, and Saturday's stand exactly where they are. The forecast, for its part, has repented: nothing heavier than ordinary October is projected tonight, the thermometer bound for 40 degrees by the late innings.3

THE CHESSBOARD DESK: A FREE DAY TO RESHUFFLE AND NEITHER MANAGER TOUCHES A PIECE — MARTINEZ KEEPS THE BALL FOR TAMPA BAY, ARMSTRONG STILL OPENS FOR CLEVELAND, AND THE TRUE MANEUVERING IS SAVED FOR SUNDAY

A postponement hands each side the rarest October commodity — choice — and both clubs promptly declined it. Nick Martinez, 15-6 with a 3.10 mark on the year, takes the ball tonight for the Gulf Coast club exactly as he would have Friday, and Cash would commit to nothing beyond him: "We'll go day to day from there." The logic wants little defending. Martinez has thrown 13 scoreless innings against these same Guardians over two regular-season meetings; his 2.38 figure on the road stood fourth in the American League — behind a list that includes, pointedly, Cleveland's own Parker Messick — and the Rays have gone 23-9 when he starts. "Nick's been a leader for us all year. He's been the bridge that's held us together," says Chandler Simpson. "When he's on the mound, we know our leader's on the mound."4

The deferred riches come later. Drew Rasmussen — worked hard of late, with a career-high 114 pitches in his final regular-season turn and better than 90 in each of two October starts since — now lines up for Sunday's fifth game on the five days' rest he kept nearly all season, and Freddy Peralta, warm off his Game 2 gem, stands ready for a sixth game should the series reach the dome.4 Cleveland's answer is the bullpen day it had long pencilled in for this spot: Shawn Armstrong, 2-1 with a 4.58 mark, opens — "Armstrong's going to start for us tomorrow," said Vogt on Friday night, and then said it again, in the manner of a man closing a subject — with Foster Griffin rested and unnamed but conspicuously available for the bulk innings behind him, and Tanner Bibee, Joey Cantillo and Slade Cecconi all in reserve. The rookie Messick, who started the second game, could move up to Sunday, when a pennant may sit on the table.5 And the idle Friday fattened both bullpens — no small mercy for the Lake Erie men, who have ridden Cade Smith and Hunter Gaddis hard through the month.3

THE HOMEFRONT DESK: THE CREAM CITY REARRANGES ITSELF AROUND FRIDAY — THE BASKETBALL CLUB MOVES ITS OPENING NIGHT TO MID-AFTERNOON FOR GAME ONE OF THE 122ND WORLD'S SERIES, AND THE FOOTBALL COACH TURNS UP IN A BREWERS CAP

Milwaukee's first World's Series since 1982 opens Friday next, the 23rd of October, at American Family Field — the first game of the 122nd Fall Classic, the National League's champion hosting whichever club survives the week by the lake — and the city has begun clearing its calendar accordingly.62 The Bucks, the town's basketball concern, were booked to raise the curtain on their own season at the Fiserv Forum that very evening; the club and its league have moved the tip-off from seven o'clock to three, "in recognition of this historic occasion," so that no Milwaukeean need choose between openings.7

"The Brewers' return to the World Series for the first time in 44 years is a special moment for Milwaukee and sports fans across Wisconsin," the basketball men declared, promising to cheer the baseball club from down the street. And up the road in Green Bay, Matt LaFleur, who coaches the football eleven, took Friday's press conference in a Brewers cap. "I want to support the Brewers," he said. "That was pretty cool to watch last night."7 The Suds men themselves, swept to the pennant and idle until their opponent is named, can only watch the lake with the rest of us.

THE TWO-WAY DESK: 'YES, DEFINITELY' — OHTANI, SILENT ON THE MOUND SINCE THE THIRD OF JULY, VOWS TO PITCH IN 2027, THOUGH WHETHER THE WINTER INCLUDES THE SURGEONS IS A QUESTION HE LEAVES OPEN

Shohei Ohtani's season ended Thursday night in the on-deck circle, bat in hand, and his first public business of the offseason was to promise both halves of himself back. Asked after the Angelenos' season-closing 6-4 defeat whether he means to pitch next year, the four-time Most Valuable answered through his interpreter without a comma's hesitation: "Yes, definitely. And looking forward to having a better season next year." Whether the road back runs through an operating theater is not yet settled — "hard to tell at this point," he allowed; it is "something that's going to be analyzed, discussed over the offseason."8

The mound half of him was, while it lasted, superb: a 1.79 earned-run average and a .179 mark against across 85 2/3 innings. But the left knee began complaining on June 11, the final four starts leaked 12 earned runs in 24 2/3 innings where all the work before had allowed 5 in 61, and after the third of July he did not pitch again — a September fortnight on the injured list for the right biceps, his first such visit in nearly three years, closing the ledger. This paper carried his insistence Wednesday that he could help with the bat regardless, and he did, the three-run homer in Thursday's finale ending a drought of two months. The career figures explain the stubbornness: among starters with 500 innings pitched since 1969, only Jim Palmer, Jacob deGrom and Clayton Kershaw own a better earned-run average than his 2.83.8

THE LEATHER DESK: THE SHAKIEST GLOVE OF 2025 IS A FINALIST FOR THE GOLD OF 2026 — SIMPSON LEARNS OF HIS NOMINATION FROM HIS OWN TELEPHONE IN A CLEVELAND HOTEL, AND THE COACH WHO REBUILT HIM SHARES THE CREDIT

The Rawlings nominations this paper recorded Thursday carried one story worth telling on its own. Chandler Simpson, the Rays' left fielder, woke in his Cleveland hotel Wednesday, took up his telephone for the morning's idle scrolling, and found himself tagged in congratulations: a Gold Glove finalist, alongside Cody Bellinger of the Yankees and Wyatt Langford of the Rangers, the verdicts sealed until the first of November. "Oh, wow," the 25-year-old reports thinking. "OK, cool."9

The arithmetic of the conversion is the story. A year ago the league's accounting had Simpson at minus-5 outs above average, the 11th percentile of all defenders; this season he stands at plus-8, the 93rd, and the runs-saved ledger swung from minus-9 to plus-8. The credit is shared, by every account including the manager's, with Corey Dickerson, the first-base coach and former outfielder whose daily instruction taught the fastest man in the league to read the ball off the bat and stand where it was going. "I think that is maybe one of the coolest stories of our season, where he was defensively last year, where he's come to this year," said Cash.9 Nor is the glove the whole of the tale: when Friday's rained-out lineup was posted, Simpson stood third in the batting order — contact and speed deployed where sluggers ordinarily reside — and the assignment is expected to survive to tonight.3

October has now emptied three Fridays running — twice by the bracket's own design, and last night by the sky's veto. What remains is simply told: the American question resumes tonight at eight by the lake, continues Sunday at five with no day of rest between, and removes to the Gulf Coast dome Monday and Tuesday only if Tampa Bay makes it necessary; Milwaukee waits on the far side with Friday's curtain already hung.36 A pennant may be had by Sunday evening, or the argument may run the whole week — either way the Herald will be at its post with the full account. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
