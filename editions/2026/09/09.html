Volume I · No. 63 · Wednesday, the Ninth of September, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

ONE STRIKE FROM DEFEAT, CHOURIO LEVELS IT WITH A SWING OF THUNDER AND THE BOY LARA ENDS IT WITH A SINGLE — THE YEAR'S FIRST NINETY-WIN CLUB: BREWERS 4, CUBS 3, IN TEN INNINGS

Misiorowski opens the evening by striking out Crow-Armstrong at 103.7 miles to the hour and passes his thousandth pitch of the season at one hundred or better — a feat without precedent in the tracked annals — yet the crown of the night is the rally: Yelich's pinch single with the account one strike from closing, Chourio's tying two-run blast, and the rookie Luis Lara's first career walk-off knock, Milwaukee's fortieth victory retrieved from behind

The first ninety-win club of the season spent a moment of Tuesday night as a beaten one. The Cubs — asked by this paper only yesterday morning to answer at once, and against Misiorowski no less — had pried two runs from the closer Megill in the top of the ninth to stand ahead 3-1, and the count had run to its final strike when the veteran Christian Yelich, batting for another man, lined a single to keep the books open. Whereupon Jackson Chourio turned on an offering from the reliever Webb and sent it over the fence with a man aboard, his two-and-twentieth of the year, and the account stood level at three. In the tenth, against the left-hander Thielbar, the rookie Luis Lara roped a single into left field with the winning run at third, and American Family Field emptied its lungs. "I think the only thing repeating in my head over and over again was, 'Wow, wow, wow,'" testified the young man, author now of his first walk-off blow in the major leagues. "That's what you love to be part of," ruled the manager Murphy, "trusting your team and believing. And they'll keep coming back."

For the game's first two-thirds the story had been the young thunderbolt. Jacob Misiorowski began his evening by striking out Pete Crow-Armstrong with a fastball registered at 103.7 miles to the hour, touched 104.5 against Suzuki moments later, and somewhere in the third inning threw his thousandth pitch of the season at one hundred or better — a figure, the wires certify, without precedent in the tracked annals, which open in 2008. A 25-pitch first inning cost him a run; five and a third scoreless frames followed, nine strikeouts in all, and the ledger he left behind reads 1.95 and 236 strikeouts, both the National's best — a strikeout count that on Milwaukee's single-season roll trails only Sheets' 264, Burnes' 243, and Higuera's 240. "That's how I throw. I don't know what to tell you," the pitcher shrugged of the milestone. "Right now, that's what I do. I throw hard."

The freight of the verdict is considerable. Milwaukee is the first club in either league to reach ninety victories, and the winning blow was its fortieth retrieved from a losing position this year — the full portrait of the small-market colossus in one line of type. The wires allow that a postseason berth, the eighth in nine years, could be sealed as soon as the weekend's set with Cincinnati, whose arrival Friday the schedule confirms. The Central's lead stretches to nine full games over Chicago, the season's series now banked at eight games to four; the visitors have dropped three running, though the wild-card row's first chair stays within their half-game reach of Philadelphia, both pretenders having lost on the night. And the desk notes the pursuit it has chronicled all week did advance in the wreckage: Crow-Armstrong pilfered his six-and-thirtieth base, leaving the forty-and-forty ledger wanting four thefts in sixteen games. The clubs conclude the set this evening, Gausman opposing Henderson and his 2.48.

News Around the League

THE RESTORATION DESK, PAID IN FULL: BOTH OF YESTERDAY'S PROMISES ARE KEPT — JUDGE STANDS IN RIGHT FIELD AFTER A HUNDRED DAYS, AND BURNES THROWS THREE SPOTLESS INNINGS AFTER FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR

This paper closed two files Tuesday that it has carried through the summer entire. In the Bronx, Aaron Judge was written into the third spot and stationed in right field precisely as yesterday's columns forecast, one hundred days after the invalid list claimed him with the fractured rib. The captain went hitless in three visits with two strikeouts and patrolled the grass for six innings of a victory sung in the card below, and the Stadium rose to him all the same. "I felt great; I just haven't been in a live game or seen live pitching in three months, so it's kind of like the start of the year," the great man allowed. "But I got work to do." The manager Boone had put the matter plainly beforehand: "His presence is very real."

And in Kansas City the desert's cavalry arrived on schedule. Corbin Burnes, four hundred and sixty-four days removed from his last start and the surgeon's table between, took a major-league mound for Arizona and delivered three scoreless innings on seven-and-fifty pitches — two hits, two walks, and a finishing flourish, Salvador Perez struck out on a 93.5-mile cutter with a runner at second before the manager Lovullo called it an evening. A ten-pitch duel with Pasquantino in the third, the pitcher mourned, was what kept him from a fourth inning. "Now we can stop talking about how long it has been and just get back to playing baseball," said Burnes, and his club made the return a winning one in eleven innings, as the card relates.

THE MILESTONE DESK: OKAMOTO'S THIRTIETH COMPLETES A COMPANY THE GAME HAS NEVER ONCE ASSEMBLED — THREE FRESHMEN AT THIRTY HOMERS IN A SINGLE SEASON

When Kazuma Okamoto deposited a Perkins offering over the left-field fence in the second inning Tuesday — back-to-back with Josh Smith, and the difference in a Toronto victory the card below records — the blow was his thirtieth of the season, and it closed a circuit no season before this one has ever completed: three rookies at thirty home runs apiece. Cincinnati's Sal Stewart, at one-and-thirty homers and the National's leading count of one hundred seven runs driven in, and the White Sox's Munetaka Murakami, whose own thirtieth these pages recorded on Sunday, had already crossed the line; the annals held ten pairs of thirty-homer freshmen — García and Mountcastle in 2021 the latest — but never a trio, until now. Okamoto's blast was also the tenth thirty-homer season struck by a man born in Japan.

The author of the record was characteristically unmoved. "Definitely, it's great to get to 30 in my first year, but we've still got a few games to go here," Okamoto said through his interpreter, pointing to a September in which he is batting .333 with sixteen contests remaining and a wild-card chair one game distant. As for a fourth member of the company, Kansas City's Carter Jensen stands at two-and-twenty homers with sixteen games to play — a long road, the desk allows, but this rookie class has made a habit of embarrassing the arithmetic.

THE PRODIGY DESK: MONTES STRIKES THE LONGEST BALL EVER MEASURED IN SEATTLE — FOUR HUNDRED SEVENTY-FOUR FEET INTO A COUNTRY NO BALL HAD VISITED

The measuring instruments at T-Mobile Park were given their busiest evening in years by a man three weeks into his major-league life. Lazaro Montes, the Mariners' one-and-twenty-year-old colossus of six feet five and two hundred fifty pounds, the fifth-ranked prospect in the organization's books, met a Quantrill fastball in the fourth inning and drove it into the right-center-field concourse, a blow the trackers certified at four hundred seventy-four feet: the farthest ball ever measured in that yard since the instruments were installed in 2015, surpassing Zunino's 470-foot drive of 2018. The ball left his bat at 116.1 miles to the hour, the hardest-struck baseball by any Mariner in three years.

"I thought that home run by Laz was spectacular," said the manager Wilson. "We talk about home runs here at this ballpark, and I don't think I've seen one that far." It was but the second homer of a major-league life one week old, begun with the September call-ups, and it arrived, the desk must report, in a losing cause — the full accounting of Texas's ten-run evening stands in the card below. The Herald has sung a great many débutantes this season; it suspects this one's name will require larger type before long.

A RECORD NO CLUB EVER SOUGHT: WASHINGTON'S EIGHT-AND-THIRTIETH BLOWN SAVE PASSES A MARK THAT HAD STOOD SINCE 2024, AND A ROOKIE'S PERFECT EVENING GOES FOR NAUGHT

The saddest line of Tuesday's agate belongs to the capital. When San Diego's four-run eighth overturned a 4-1 Washington lead — the full mechanics are in the card below — the failure was entered in the books as the Nationals' eight-and-thirtieth blown save of the season, and no club in the annals of the statistic, kept since 1969, has ever recorded more. The 2024 White Sox, at seven-and-thirty, are relieved of the distinction. The cruelty of the evening was in its beginning: the rookie Riley Cornelio had opened with three perfect innings, the finest work of his young life, before the late innings came apart — three straight walks from the rookie Simpson on eighteen pitches loading the bases for the collapse.

The clubhouse met the record with a workingman's dignity this paper is bound to respect. "We show up every day and we work really hard," said Cornelio. "The results obviously have been frustrating, but it's not from a lack of trying. I'll tell you that." The manager Butera reached for the schoolmaster's register: "Failures in life, we can either look at and put our head down and accept it, or we can face it head on and take something from it and learn from it." Washington has now dropped six running; the record, at least, can only be extended, not set twice.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: THE ORIOLES LIMP THROUGH A DEFEAT THEY COULD NOT SPARE, CARROLL'S BACK SENDS HIM TO THE EXAMINERS, AND CROCHET'S SECOND SIDE SESSION RAISES OCTOBER HOPES IN BOSTON

The ward's fullest wing this morning is Baltimore's. In the loss to Cleveland the card below records, Gunnar Henderson fouled a ball off his left knee in the eighth, worked an eight-pitch walk on the aching joint, and then departed when it locked up on the bases; Blaze Alexander left in the ninth with a back spasm; and the young Samuel Basallo winced through his swings all evening, the soreness in the right shoulder lingering still. Both infielders are listed day-to-day, which is more than a club that has dropped seven of eight and stands four back of the last chair can comfortably afford. In the desert's camp, Corbin Carroll was scratched late from the Kansas City lineup with discomfort in the lower back — Tawa took his post in right field to considerable profit, as the card relates — and the manager Lovullo says the outfielder, who leads both leagues with sixteen triples, will be examined by imaging upon the club's return to Phoenix.

The brighter page is Boston's. Garrett Crochet threw his second side session in five days — some twenty pitches, and this time the full wardrobe of offerings, where the first session was fastballs alone — and the interim manager Tracy pronounced it "looked good and felt good. Good step in the right direction." The left-hander has been off a mound for the better part of three months with the ailing shoulder, and the club now openly entertains the thought of his return in relief for the season's last days or for October itself. "It's Garrett Crochet," said Tracy. "If Garrett Crochet looks like Garrett Crochet and he's healthy... yeah, that helps any team." Swift knitting to every name above.

The Rest of the Card

THE SUMMIT OF THE TWO EASTS IS NO CONTEST AT ALL — FOUR BALLS LEAVE THE YARD AND SIMPSON STEALS HIS FORTIETH: SUNSHINE NINE 7, BRAVES 1

The bill this paper hung its lantern on — the leaders of the two Easts, five-and-eighty wins apiece calling — was settled early and emphatically by the visitors. Tampa Bay struck four home runs at Truist Park: Díaz's two-and-twentieth, Junior Caminero's nine-and-thirtieth — the American's leading count — Hicks's eighteenth, and Palacios's ninth, while Peralta gave six innings of one-run ball and three relievers finished without a blemish. Chandler Simpson pilfered his fortieth base besides, drawing level with the Yankee Chisholm atop the American's theft ledger. Atlanta managed a run in the first and nothing after, the young Smith-Shawver lasting but three and a third and two errors compounding the evening; the Braves have dropped two running, their lead in the National's East holding at four over Philadelphia, while the Rays' own margin in the American's East stands at four over New York. The clubs go again this evening at 7:15 and conclude at Thursday's matinee.

BAZZANA CAPS A NINE-PITCH ARGUMENT WITH THREE-RUN THUNDER, AND THE LAST CHAIR'S TIEBREAKER IS BANKED AT THE HARBOR: CLEVELAND NINE 9, BALTIMORES 5

The visitors built a 6-0 lead on the strength of a four-run second — Travis Bazzana's three-run blast, his eleventh, closing a nine-pitch at-bat against Young — watched all of it nearly vanish, and answered like a club with October designs. Baltimore's five unanswered runs, four of them in a fifth inning crowned by Pete Alonso's four-and-thirtieth homer, chased the starter Bibee; whereupon Cleveland stacked three in the seventh, Brayan Rocchio's two-run single the decisive freight, and four relievers carried the last four and two-thirds innings without permitting a hit. "Huge, huge team win all around tonight," ruled the manager Vogt, and the tables agree: the Cleveland nine draw within two of the Central's chair and hold the American's last wild-card seat, with the season's series against Baltimore — the tiebreaker, should it be wanted — now secured. The Orioles, whose walking wounded fill the ward above, stand four back with sixteen to play; Alonso's two runs driven raised his American-leading count to seven-and-ninety. The rubber game goes tonight, Griffin and his fifteen victories opposing the hard-luck Baz.

THE BOY WHO SHAGGED FLIES AT THE BANK RETURNS TO ROB IT — VARSHO'S BLAST DECIDES A CONTEST OF HEAVYWEIGHTS: HOUSTON NINE 6, QUAKERS 5

Daulton Varsho passed his boyhood summers chasing batting-practice flies at Citizens Bank Park, where his father coached the Phillies and whose great catcher supplied his very name; on Tuesday he returned to torment the household, collecting three hits, scoring twice, and striking the homer — his eleventh — that proved the difference. Houston's four-run second, built around Walker's five-and-twentieth blast and three runs driven, did the heavy lifting against the beaten Painter, and the club then held its breath: Philadelphia answered with three in the sixth behind Trea Turner's eighteenth homer, another run in the seventh, and one more in the ninth off Hader, who closed the door regardless for his four-and-twentieth save. "I like hitting here," Varsho allowed. "It's a place that always brings back good memories." The victory holds the West's chair by two over surging Texas, no small matter for a Houston nine in the teeth of thirteen straight engagements against clubs in the October picture; the Quakers' first wild-card chair survives the defeat at half a game.

NOTHING-TO-NOTHING INTO THE EIGHTH, THEN TWO BLOWN LEADS, AND TAWA — THE SCRATCHED MAN'S UNDERSTUDY — SETTLES IT IN THE ELEVENTH: PHOENIX NINE 5, ROYAL BLUES 3, IN ELEVEN INNINGS

For the second consecutive evening at Kauffman Stadium the clubs required extra chapters, and for the second consecutive evening the desert men wrote the last one. Burnes's three spotless innings — the restoration column above gives the homecoming its due — opened a duel that stood scoreless into the eighth, Wacha matching zeros with six innings of his own; thereafter the game blew two leads with abandon. Arizona edged ahead in the eighth and ninth, John Rave's two-run homer off Morillo levelled it at two; each club scored once in the tenth, Walston surrendering the tying run unearned; and in the eleventh Tim Tawa — stationed in right field only because Carroll's back sent him to the ward above — struck a two-run blast, his tenth, to end the argument. Gurriel's first triple of the year and Perdomo's fiftieth run driven furnished the middle freight. The Phoenix nine have won four running and hold the National's last chair by half a game over San Diego, the two clubs level in the losses column — and the cavalry, as the ward and restoration desks both attest, is only beginning to arrive.

THE SCOREBOARD TELLS THEM ARIZONA HAS WON, AND THE EIGHTH INNING ANSWERS — FOUR ACROSS AND THE CHASE HOLDS AT HALF A GAME: MISSION NINE 5, WASHINGTONS 4

"Whoever tells you they're not looking — they're lying," confessed Manny Machado, and Petco Park's scoreboard had indeed announced Arizona's victory while the home nine trailed by three. The answer was a four-run eighth of relentless small arithmetic: the record-setting Washington collapse is chronicled in the news columns above, but the ledger here credits Hays, Machado with two runs driven, Merrill, and Campusano across the late scoring, before Morejon sealed the ninth for his third save. Mize's five and two-thirds innings of seven strikeouts kept the evening within reach through the early Washington scoring — Chaparro's tenth homer among it — and Dylan Crews doubled twice in a losing cause. The Mission nine have won three running and hold their deficit for the National's last chair at half a game, level with Arizona in the losses column; Washington's sixth consecutive defeat carries the melancholy distinction the news desk records.

TEN STRIKEOUTS, NOT A SINGLE PASS, AND A FOUR-AND-THIRTIETH WINNING SEASON SEALED ON THE CAPTAIN'S FIRST NIGHT BACK: PINSTRIPES 5, MOUNTAIN MEN 3

The evening the Bronx had circled — the captain's return commands the restoration column above — was underwritten by the young man who has quietly become the American's best pitcher. Cam Schlittler carried seven innings of three hits and a single run, struck out ten without walking a soul, and lowered his league-leading figure to 2.01; the victory, his thirteenth, was the club's two-and-eightieth of the season, sealing a four-and-thirtieth consecutive winning campaign for the franchise — a river of success that began before most of Tuesday's crowd drew breath. Ramos supplied the cushion with a three-run blast in the eighth, his eleventh, and after a noisy ninth — Amador's homer among it — Bednar recorded the final out for his two-and-thirtieth save. For Colorado, Hunter Goodman's seven-and-thirtieth homer — level with Olson for third in the National — was the chief consolation of an evening that left the beaten Hughes at seven defeats against no victories. New York holds the East's second chair, four behind Tampa Bay; the clubs go again at 7:05, Sugano opposing Warren.

BACK-TO-BACK IN THE SECOND, SEVEN STRONG FROM SORIANO, AND TORONTO CLIMBS BACK TO ITS WATERLINE WITH THE CHAIR ONE GAME OFF: CANADIANS 4, ATHLETICS 2

The seesaw this paper has ridden all week — Toronto above the waterline Saturday, below it by Monday midnight — tipped upward again in West Sacramento. Smith and Okamoto opened the second inning with consecutive homers off Perkins, the latter blast the historic thirtieth sung at proper length in the milestone columns above, and José Soriano made the early thunder stand with seven innings of two runs, his twelfth victory; Miles carried the last two frames spotless for his second save. Cortes and Walton supplied the home nine's homers before a September gathering of 7,632. The Canadians stand level once more at three-and-seventy wins apiece, one game behind Cleveland's chair with the tiebreaker banked — an arithmetic this paper has recited so often it could set it to music — and Okamoto, batting .333 for the month, shows no inclination to let the season end quietly.

A FIVE-RUN FIFTH BURIES THE SOUND, JANSEN DRIVES IN FOUR, AND THE FANTASY DRAFT GETS THE CREDIT: TEXAS NINE 10, MARINERS 5

Texas passed Monday's idle day conducting its annual fantasy-football draft, and the manager Schumaker convened his veterans besides; whatever was said, the bats arrived in Seattle fully briefed. A five-run fifth broke the evening open, Danny Jansen drove in four runs with his sixth homer among the freight, Seager and Langford added blasts of their own — the latter stealing a bag for good measure — and Quantrill carried six innings of three runs for his eighth victory. The home nine's answer was chiefly the rookie Montes's four-hundred-seventy-four-foot thunderclap, a ball struck into country no Mariner had ever reached, whose full measurements the prodigy desk above records. The Texas nine have won two running and stand two behind Houston in the West and a game and a half from the last wild-card chair — the season-defining errand through Seattle and Phoenix, as their manager frames it, begun in the best of order.

THE DEADLINE PURCHASES DELIVER PRECISELY AS ADVERTISED — KREMER'S FIVE STRONG, FOUR SCORELESS FROM THE PURCHASED BULLPEN: MINNESOTANS 3, BENGALS 2

Five weeks ago Minnesota's front office spent its July capital on pitching, and Tuesday at Comerica Park was the catalogue illustration: Dean Kremer, a deadline acquisition, gave five innings of two runs; Minter, another, threw a perfect sixth; Hoffman, a third, carried the eighth; and the home-grown Morris and Gómez filled the seams, the latter sealing his two-and-twentieth save. "Good victory all around," pronounced the manager Shelton, economically. Kody Clemens supplied the decisive blows with his five-and-twentieth homer and two runs driven, while Detroit's answer was the rookie McGonigle's sixteenth blast — the Bengals, who won Monday's opener behind Greene as these pages recorded, thus surrender the middle game, and the set will be decided at this afternoon's matinee, Matthews opposing Montero at 1:10.

DETMERS PERMITS ONE HIT IN SIX AND STRIKES OUT NINE, AND BOSTON'S FIVE-GAME PARADE IS HALTED BY THE LEAGUE'S LAST-PLACE SPOILERS: SERAPHS 6, HUB NINE 1

The Anaheim club, ninety times beaten and long past table arithmetic, reserves the right to inconvenience its betters, and Reid Detmers exercised it in full: six innings, a single hit, nine strikeouts — his season's count now one-and-ninety, fourth in the American — while his mates stacked fifteen hits at Fenway Park. Zach Neto did the heaviest lifting with his four-and-twentieth homer, his nine-and-twentieth double, and two runs driven; the ancient Trout doubled; d'Arnaud doubled twice; and the beaten Sandoval absorbed ten hits across six innings yet kept the margin within reason, the final pair of runs arriving late against the relief. Boston's five-game winning parade thus ends at a nondescript 6-1 — the day's real Fenway news was thrown on a side mound hours earlier, as the ward above relates — and the Hub nine hold the wild-card row's second chair in comfort regardless, six games clear.

A FIVE-RUN FIRST SETTLES MATTERS BEFORE THE PALE HOSE HAVE BATTED, AND THE CENTRAL'S CUSHION THINS TO TWO: SMOKY CITY NINE 9, PALE HOSE 3

Pittsburgh required but one inning at Rate Field: five runs crossed before Chicago swung in anger, seven had scored by the second's end, and the evening's remaining labor belonged to Bubba Chandler, who struck out eight across six innings for his seventh victory. The visitors stacked eighteen hits in all — Bryan Reynolds doubling twice among his two runs driven, Cruz two more, the rookie Horwitz three — while Burke, the beaten starter, was excused after recording four outs. The rookie Teel's fourth homer led a modest reply. The consequence travels upward: the Pale Hose watch their Central lead thin to two games over the Cleveland nine, whose own victory stands higher in this card; the Smoky City men, winners of two running and seven of their last ten, continue a September of honest mischief five and a half games from the National's last chair.

SKUBAL'S LONGEST EVENING IN HIS NEW LIVERY CARRIES THE CHAMPIONS TO A SIXTH STRAIGHT: DODGERS 3, REDLEGS 2

The left-hander acquired at the deadline continues to repay the ransom by installments. Tarik Skubal worked seven and a third innings of two runs — his longest outing since donning Dodger flannels — struck out five, and left with the lead his mates never surrendered, Scott sealing the two-and-twentieth save. The champions' scoring was patient work: Edman and Smith drove in the early runs, the latter's seventh homer, and Teoscar Hernández legged out a triple with a run driven besides. Cincinnati's whole account belonged to Elly De La Cruz, whose five-and-twentieth homer and six-and-twentieth double produced both runs. Los Angeles has won six running, matching its best run of the season, and each of Skubal's last four turns; the West is held at ten and a half. The night owls are directed to this evening's rematch at 10:10, Yamamoto and his 2.67 opposing Lowder — and the desk observes the Redlegs' Stewart, whose historic thirtieth-homer company is convened in the milestone columns above, still leads the National with one hundred seven runs driven.

ROUPP WALKS FOUR AND STRANDS THEM ALL, TWO HOMERS SUFFICE, AND THE SPOILERS OF THE BAY TAKE ANOTHER: JINTS 2, REDBIRDS 1

San Francisco's evening was an exercise in economy: two runs in the first two innings — the young giant Bryce Eldridge's sixteenth homer and the unheralded Koss's fourth, both off the otherwise-creditable Mathews — and nothing more, defended to the last out. Landen Roupp walked four across six innings and permitted none of them to score, scattering two hits for his ninth victory; three relievers finished, Kuhnel recording the last four outs for his seventh save and surviving Torres's ninth-inning run driven in. St. Louis, twice beaten running and six games from the National's last chair, continues to find September unkind; the Jints, winners of two straight and long past their own table arithmetic, play on for pride and for the education of the young Eldridge, whose runs-driven count stands now at forty-and-nine. The set concludes at 3:45 this afternoon, Pallante opposing Tidwell.

SEVEN RUNS INSIDE FIVE INNINGS CHASE ALCANTARA, THREE BALLS LEAVE THE YARD, AND THE STREAK RUNS TO THREE: METS 7, FLORIDIANS 5

New York's bats gave Alcantara the roughest evening of his September: seven runs inside four and a third innings, three of them aboard homers from Lindor, Soto — three runs driven in his case, with his four-and-twentieth blast — and the young Baty, whose tenth homer and two runs driven continued a happy stretch. Miami's four-run sixth, entered entire against Manaea's account, made a contest of it — Lopez, Sanoja, and Marsee among the runs driven — but the New York relief restored order, Fulton's three hitless innings for the home nine going unrewarded before Senga sealed the ninth for his seventh save. The Mets have won three running — their brightest stretch in a month — while the Floridians drop a second straight; both clubs play out a string the pennant left behind some weeks ago, which renders the baseball no less honest. The set continues at 6:40 this evening.

The tables this Wednesday morning read thus. In the American: Tampa Bay holds the East by four over New York, Boston seven astern; the Central tightens to two, the Pale Hose over Cleveland; the Astros keep the West by two over Texas. The wild-card row: New York and Boston in comfort, the last chair Cleveland's by a single game over Toronto — tiebreaker to the chair-holder — with Texas a game and a half back and battered Baltimore four. In the National: Atlanta by four over Philadelphia; Milwaukee, first of all clubs to ninety victories, by nine over Chicago; Los Angeles by ten and a half over Arizona. The wild-card ledger: Philadelphia's first chair by half a game over the Cubs, and the last chair Arizona's by half a game over San Diego, the two level in the losses column with Pittsburgh five and a half back. Tonight's bill is again a full board of fifteen engagements, all verified against the schedule, and the desk commends five to the reader's lamp. At 6:35 the rubber game at the harbor, Cleveland's Griffin and his fifteen victories against the hard-used Baz; at 6:40 in Philadelphia the Quakers hand the ball to Sánchez, he of the sixteen victories and the 2.58, with the West's leaders visiting; at 7:05 in the Bronx the captain's second evening, Sugano opposing Warren; at 7:15 in Atlanta the Easts' summit resumes, the Braves wanting urgent amends; and at 7:40 in Milwaukee the Cubs make their last stand of the set, Gausman against Henderson and his 2.48. The night owls are commended once more to Chavez Ravine at 10:10, Yamamoto against Lowder. The almanac notes Roberto Clemente Day upon the fifteenth; eighteen mornings remain to the season's final page on the seven-and-twentieth, and the Wild Card rounds open two days after. Until the next dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
