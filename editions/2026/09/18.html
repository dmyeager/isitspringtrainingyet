The Game of the Day

THE CORONATION, POSTPONED A NIGHT, ARRIVES BY BULLPEN PARADE — A THIRTEENTH WEST FLAG IN FOURTEEN YEARS IS SEWN ON AT CINCINNATI: DODGERS 8, REDS 2

Tucker strikes four blows and finishes a triple shy of the club's first cycle since 2017; Knack, summoned from Oklahoma City that very morning, banks four scoreless innings for the victory; and the champions' fourteenth consecutive October now aims itself at a prize no club has taken thrice running since the century turned

The champagne had waited a night in its ice, and the champions elected not to make it wait a second. Denied on Wednesday when Snell's groin gave way after an inning — as this paper recorded yesterday, coronation postponed — the Dodgers came to Thursday afternoon at Cincinnati with a patchwork bullpen day where a starter ought to have been, and turned it into a procession. Justin Wrobleski opened with three perfect innings; Landon Knack, recalled from Oklahoma City only that morning, gave four scoreless in the middle for the victory; and by the time Halvorsen surrendered a courtesy run in the ninth the score stood 8-2, the West stood clinched, and the visiting clubhouse stood ankle-deep in its second champagne of the week — the berth toasted Monday, the flag properly celebrated now.

The day's largest bat belonged to Kyle Tucker: four hits in five tries, a two-run homer in the first, doubles in the second and fourth, three runs driven, and a ninth-inning at-bat that came up one triple shy of the club's first cycle since 2017. Freeman, Muncy, De Paula, and Rojas all drove in runs behind him — Muncy's a two-run double in the three-run fourth, Rojas' a solo homer in the ninth — while Cincinnati's answer was confined to Stewart's two-and-thirtieth home run, struck off Treinen in the fourth. Singer absorbed the loss, seven runs in three and a third.

The ledger of the dynasty asks a moment's awe. This is the thirteenth division title in fourteen years — only the 2021 Giants ever interrupted — the fifth in succession, and the fourteenth consecutive October engagement; since divisional play began in 1969 no club has gathered more such flags than Los Angeles' four-and-twenty, one clear of Atlanta. "You get so mired in each year, it's hard to have any perspective," said the president Friedman. "But the important thing is how we finish and how we go into October." The manager Roberts stated the house terms plainly: "If you don't win the World Series, you fail. But that's kind of part of what you signed up with the Dodgers." What they chase now is the game's first three-peat since the 1998-2000 Yankees — pursued, it must be said, at less than full strength, with Ohtani and Pages both on the invalid list, Snell's groin under watch, and the six-man rotation of summer reduced to four stretched arms. "We've had guys go down. We've had guys step up in their place," shrugged Muncy. "We've been able to survive a lot." Betts put the creed shortest: "Getting here, you can't really get too focused on how, as long as you get there."

News Around the League

THE INFIRMARY WARD: A TERRIFYING MINUTE AT FLUSHING — JARED YOUNG CARTED OFF AFTER A COLLISION AT FIRST BASE, AND THE HOSPITAL'S FIRST WORD IS GUARDEDLY GOOD; BOSTON'S CATCHER AND CLOSER BOTH DAY-TO-DAY

The ward's gravest entry stopped a ballgame cold. In the sixth inning at Citi Field, Philadelphia's Crawford tapped a slow roller toward first; the Mets' Jared Young and his pitcher McLean converged upon it, and McLean's knee struck Young's head at a full run. Young lay motionless while the trainers worked, then was carted from the field — a thumbs-up to the crowd as he went, consciousness regained — and taken to hospital, where the scan of his head came back negative. He showed concussion symptoms through the evening but full use of his limbs, and the interim manager Green pronounced the club optimistic he has "avoided the scariest outcomes." "That was a terrifying moment on a baseball field," Green said; the shortstop Lindor spoke for the room: "Life is bigger than this game, for sure." McLean, shaken, finished the inning — Green confessed afterward he regretted allowing even that — and asked to be excused from the pressmen. The Herald sets down its scorecard a moment and wishes the man a full and unhurried recovery.

The lesser cots are Boston's, and they matter to a pennant race. Willson Contreras took a pitch on the right hand in the eighth at Arlington and left the game; the X-rays came back negative, the diagnosis a bruise with swelling, the prognosis day-to-day. Aroldis Chapman, the closer, was unavailable altogether with tightness of the lat and is likewise day-to-day — "We're going to be conservative with it," said the interim manager Tracy. Both absences hang over a weekend at Tampa Bay that could seat Boston at October's table, as the desk below relates.

THE MOST-VALUABLE DESK: FORTY FOR ALVAREZ, ONE-AND-FORTY FOR CAMINERO — THE AMERICAN'S GREAT ARGUMENT SHARPENS, AND ITS BATTING RACE COMES DOWN TO SEVEN TEN-THOUSANDTHS

The American League's autumn argument gained a chapter on each coast of the Gulf. At Houston, Yordan Alvarez struck his fortieth home run — one of five separate Astro thunderclaps, as the card below records — and the wires promptly asked whether the Most Valuable canvass has become a two-man affair between Alvarez and Tampa Bay's Junior Caminero, who stands at one-and-forty homers with an .895 figure of on-base-and-slugging. "Not so fast," came the answer from Alvarez's own manager Espada and his teammates, who would widen the field; the Herald reminds the reader in any case that the writers' ballot is cast after the season and announced in November, and until then the argument is an argument, not an award.

Caminero, for his part, spent Thursday demonstrating that a man may carry a pennant race lightly. Chasing a foul ball to the Athletics' bullpen down the left-field line, he was met by the opposing reliever Medina bearing a cup of water; the third baseman drank it, tossed the cup, and jogged back to his station. "Hydration," he explained. "It's a long walk. That's my game right there — getting funny."

And beneath the home-run question runs a finer one: the batting race. Houston's Alvarez holds the American's lead at .3114; Tampa Bay's Chandler Simpson — two hits Thursday, his five-and-fiftieth multi-hit game, batting .356 across three months — sits at .3107, seven ten-thousandths behind, with his four-and-fortieth stolen base banked besides. "I know he hates to talk about numbers; I'll talk about them for him," said his teammate Palacios. Nine days remain for the decimals to argue.

THE DEALINGS DESK: A WAIVER PRIZE CROSSES THE LEAGUE LINE — URQUIDY TO PHILADELPHIA, AND WASHINGTON OPENS ITS PURSE TOWARD ITS ACE

Philadelphia, arranging its October pantry, claimed the right-hander José Urquidy off waivers from the White Sox on Thursday — a veteran arm with a champion's pedigree from his Houston years, acquired for the price of a claim. To clear the room, the Phillies moved the right-hander Kilian to the sixty-day shelf with his oblique strain. The Quaker City's rotation ledger explains the appetite: Luzardo on the shelf, as this paper recorded Wednesday, and every contender in the National hunting insurance arms for the rounds ahead.

In Washington, meanwhile, the wires report the Nationals in contract-extension talks with their ace, the right-hander Cade Cavalli — per a source, the club not yet confirming. The case for haste writes itself: eight-and-twenty years old, twelve victories against five defeats with an earned-run figure of 3.12, under contract until 2030 but pitching this season like a man whose price rises by the start. He takes the ball this very night at St. Louis; the purse and the pitcher will presumably keep talking.

THE OCTOBER DESK: ARLINGTON STAGES THE WEEKEND'S HEAVYWEIGHT BILL — TWO CLUBS DEAD LEVEL AT SIX-AND-SEVENTY WINS, WITH THE WEST AND THE LAST CHAIRS ALIKE IN THE BALANCE

With nine days left to the season's final page, the schedule-makers have delivered their best card to Texas: the Blue Jays and Rangers, each at six-and-seventy wins and seven-and-seventy defeats, open three games at Arlington tonight with consequences running in every direction — the American's West, where Thursday's defeat dropped Texas a game behind Houston, and the wild-card row, where both clubs sit two games from the last chair with the tiebreakers and the arithmetic all still in motion. Toronto sends its ace Cease, he of the 2.38 earned-run figure and the American's strikeout lead, against Rocker in the opener; the loser of the series may find the mathematics turned suddenly cruel.

The weekend's other appointments carry their own October freight. Boston, holder of the second wild-card chair, calls at Tampa Bay with a chance to seat itself formally at the table — the Red Sox can clinch a postseason berth at the Trop this very weekend, catcher and closer permitting, as the ward above frets. Atlanta, the East's leader, opens at Houston, where the newly throned West leaders wait; and the crowned Brewers call at Baltimore still hunting victory six-and-ninety. The desk commends the whole card to the reader's lamp-oil.

The Rest of the Card

DOWN THREE BY THE SECOND, AND ONE SWING REWRITES THE EVENING — STORY'S FIRST THUNDER SINCE MAY ARRIVES THREE RUNS DEEP, AND A 72-AND-1 LEDGER TAKES ITS SECOND BLOT: BOSTONS 4, LONE STAR NINE 3

Texas entered the night two-and-seventy wins against a single defeat when leading in the eighth inning or later; it left with the second. Down 3-0 from the second inning — Jung's homer and a squeeze among the damage — Boston scratched one back in the seventh, then put two aboard in the eighth on Abreu's walk and the hit batsman that sent Contreras to the ward above before Trevor Story turned on an eighty-mile curveball from Latz and lined it to left-center: a three-run blow, his first home run since the thirteenth of May and fourth of the year, on the far side of surgery and three lost months. "That was the point of getting the surgery — to come back and try to be the player that I know I can be," said Story, riding an eight-game hitting streak. Gray gave six and a third of three-run ball on a season-high one hundred two pitches, Whitlock sealed the fourth save, and Boston — its sweep averted, its second wild-card chair held five clear — set off for Tampa with clinching on its mind. Texas fell a game behind Houston in the West, its wounds catalogued in the desk above.

FIVE SEPARATE THUNDERCLAPS, THE FORTIETH AMONG THEM — THE HOUSTON NINE TAKE THE WEST'S THRONE ALONE: HOUSTON NINE 6, ROYAL BLUES 2

A day after sharing the summit, Houston claimed it outright by the simplest means the game allows: five home runs from five different men. Peña's twentieth answered Kansas City's early run in the first, Varsho's twelfth followed in the second, and the fifth inning brought Wade Jr.'s fourth and then Yordan Alvarez's fortieth — the blow that feeds the Most Valuable argument above — before Trammell, activated from the paternity list only that morning, added the eighth of his year in the eighth inning. Lugo surrendered four of the five and the loss; the rookie Pecko gave five and two-thirds of one-run relief behind the opener. Kansas City managed three hits, two of them Jensen's. The Houston nine lead Texas by a game with nine to play, and Atlanta arrives tonight.

MURAKAMI'S THREE-RUN STATEMENT STANDS ALL NIGHT, AND THE PROPHECY OF YESTERDAY'S COLUMNS IS KEPT — A SHARE OF THE THRONE RETAKEN WITHOUT CLEVELAND THROWING A PITCH: SOUTH SIDERS 3, DETROITS 1

This paper observed only yesterday that Chicago might retake a share of the Central without Cleveland lifting a finger, the new leaders being idle; the South Siders required three batters to set about it. Munetaka Murakami struck a three-run homer in the first — his two-and-thirtieth, with Meidroth and Grichuk aboard — later added a triple, and the three runs stood untouched to the end. Fedde carved six strikeouts into the fifth, Hudson took the decision, Taylor banked a seven-out save, and the third baseman Vargas twice robbed Detroit with the glove at moments the wires called crucial. The ledger this morning: Chicago and Cleveland level at eight-and-seventy wins apiece, the tiebreaker Chicago's, banked Monday, as these columns have thrice recorded — meaning the Cleveland nine must still finish clear. Detroit has dropped two straight since its six-game surge.

A TWO-OUT WALK, THEN SEVEN STRAIGHT CORSAIRS ABOARD — THE AUDITION ENDS IN THE SECOND INNING, AND MILWAUKEE'S SIX-AND-NINETIETH MUST WAIT: CORSAIRS 7, MILWAUKEES 4

The audition this paper flagged yesterday came to order at PNC Park and adjourned inside two innings. Kyle Harrison, auditioning for an October role, walked Simon with two out in the second and watched seven consecutive Pirates reach — Griffin's two-run double, Cruz's two-run single, and Lowe's hit-by-pitch with the bases full among the wreckage — before departing at five runs, his figure across the last four outings now a grim 21.00. "I'm not going to give in, not going to give up," the left-hander vowed, and his manager Murphy kept a door ajar: "We're not giving up hope that he's going to be really effective for us soon." Davis' two-run homer in the eighth answered Naylor's review-upheld blow for Milwaukee, and Montgomery sealed the fourteenth save. The crowned Milwaukees, stalled at five-and-ninety wins, carry the hundred-win arithmetic to Baltimore tonight.

SIX IN THE FOURTH, A SIXTEENTH SWEEP, AND THE RETURNING SHORTSTOP BUNTS HIS WAY INTO THE LEDGER: GULF COAST CLUB 10, MACKMEN 1

The sweep this paper's Thursday columns saw the Gulf Coast club attempting is banked — a sixteenth of the season, extending the franchise record, the most by any club since the 2022 Dodgers — on the strength of a six-run fourth: Simpson's two-run single, Palacios' two-run double with Vilade scampering home on the loose throw, and Taylor Walls, activated from the shelf that very morning, dropping a perfect bunt single along the third-base line for the sixth run of the frame. The bunt was the club's seven-and-fortieth hit of the kind this year, the second-most of any team since such things were tracked. Rasmussen coasted five scoreless for his fifteenth victory — the Rays have now won eleven straight games he has started, tying the franchise record set across Matt Moore's 2012-13 turns — and the winning streak stands at six, the East's lead at five, with Boston arriving tonight. Butler's homer spared the Mackmen the shutout.

A SEVEN-RUN SECOND, THREE TRIPLES IN ONE AFTERNOON, AND BOGAERTS COMES A SINGLE SHY OF THE CYCLE — THE MISSION NINE BANK A TENTH IN ELEVEN: MISSION NINE 9, COLORADANS 2

Xander Bogaerts did the hard parts first — a homer leading off the second, a triple to cap the seven-run rally that followed as San Diego batted around, a double off the left-center wall in the fourth — and then chased the humble single in vain to the last. "I wanted it bad," he admitted. The Mission nine struck fifteen hits in all, three of them triples (Merrill and Cronenworth joining the fun), Harris driving in three; King gave six innings of two for his twelfth victory. The arithmetic travels well: ten victories in eleven games, a road trip closed at six wins and one defeat, and the third wild-card chair now held by four full games over Arizona with the season's final series against those very Snakes still waiting at Petco. Denver's misery deepens at six-and-fifty wins.

SEVEN SPOTLESS INNINGS IN THE OLD STYLE — NOLA BLANKS THE METROPOLITANS ON A NIGHT THE GAME ITSELF STOOD STILL: BROTHERLY LOVE NINE 3, METROPOLITANS 0

Aaron Nola threw seven scoreless innings of three hits — vintage stuff, the wires calling this run his best since 2018 — and Philadelphia manufactured its three runs the artisan's way: Harper's challenge-overturned single in the fifth, then sacrifice flies from Arraez and Crawford in the seventh and eighth. Harper collected three hits, Duran sealed his two-and-thirtieth save, and the Brotherly Love nine opened at Flushing with a second straight victory to stay level with the Cubs atop the wild-card row, four behind Atlanta in the East. The Metropolitans, blanked on four hits, have lost five running — and their evening's true weight was carried off on a cart in the sixth, as the ward above records with the whole game's good wishes.

SEVEN SCORELESS FROM THE ROOKIE UREÑA, TWO MINNESOTA RALLIES, AND MECKLER'S SINGLE ENDS IT IN THE TENTH: HALOS 5, MINNESOTANS 4

The rookie Walbert Ureña gave the finest turn of his young year — seven scoreless innings, nine strikeouts, not a walk — and then watched his 3-0 lead nearly come to grief twice over. Minnesota's Jenkins, hitting but .143 on the year, scored on Lee's sacrifice fly in the eighth and tied the game himself with a two-run homer in the ninth; Lee's single in the tenth put the visitors ahead. But the last of the tenth belonged to Anaheim: Mike Trout lined the tying run home, and Meckler — two hits on the night — grounded the winner through the right side. The Minnesotans struck out fifteen times in defeat and get the same hosts again tonight; the Halos, long since out of the argument, keep collecting evenings worth the gate.

The Friday tables, then — with the champions' flag of the West newly sewn on. In the American: clinched Tampa Bay holds the East by five over clinched New York, Boston ten and a half astern; the Central is a dead heat once more, Chicago and Cleveland level at eight-and-seventy wins with the tiebreaker Chicago's; and the West is Houston's by one over Texas, the Silver Boot insurance still in Houston's keeping. The wild-card row: New York and Boston seated in comfort, the last chair Cleveland's, with Texas and Toronto two back — tonight's antagonists at Arlington, as the October desk sings — and Baltimore three. In the National: Atlanta by four over Philadelphia; crowned Milwaukee by ten over Chicago; crowned Los Angeles by nine over San Diego. The wild-card ledger: the Cubs and Phillies level at the row's head, San Diego in the third chair by four over Arizona, Miami and Pittsburgh eight back and dimming. Tonight's bill is a full fifteen engagements, verified against the schedule, and the desk commends: at 7:10 Boston opens at the Trop with a berth there for the clinching, Seymour opposing Suarez; at 8:05 Cease carries Toronto's case into Arlington against Rocker, with the West and the last chairs alike at hazard; at 8:10 Atlanta calls upon newly throned Houston; and at 9:40 the Yankees open at Phoenix against Rodriguez, fifteen victories and a 2.62 figure to his name. Nine mornings remain to the season's final page on the seven-and-twentieth; the Wild Card rounds open on the nine-and-twentieth. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
