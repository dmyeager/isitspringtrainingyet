Volume I · No. 81 · Sunday, the Seven-and-Twentieth of September, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 13 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

FIVE HUNDRED SEVEN-AND-FIFTY STARTS, AND THE LAST ONE ENDS IN TEARS AND THUNDER — VERLANDER SAYS FAREWELL, HIS DAUGHTER RUNS TO THE MOUND, AND VALENCIA WALKS IT OFF: TIGERS 4, PIRATES 3

Three-and-forty years old, six months removed from a big-league pitch and nursing a hamstring that never quite healed, the old lion gives Detroit five and a third innings and six strikeouts in the valedictory this paper has kept vigil over; seven-year-old Genevieve sprints to the mound as he departs and the man who has seen everything is seen sobbing; whereupon Eduardo Valencia, in the ninth, strikes the two-run blow that turns a 3-2 deficit into a 4-3 triumph, and Comerica Park empties its heart entire

Attend, this paper counseled its readers in Saturday's dispatch, if you stood within a day's ride of Detroit — there would not be another. Those who heeded were paid in full and then in currencies no scorecard has a column for. Justin Verlander, three-and-forty years of age, took a big-league mound for the five hundred seven-and-fiftieth and final time, against Pittsburgh at Comerica Park, and Vintage Verlander answered the bell.

Consider what the afternoon asked of him. He had not thrown a pitch in a Major League game in six months, had not faced an opposing batsman since June, had never fully healed the hamstring that shadowed his summer, and had ramped for this farewell on all of one-and-twenty pitches in a simulated game. The Detroit clubhouse had joked for days about sending him out like Billy Chapel, the celluloid Tiger who closes his career with a perfect game. Perfection was not on offer; nobility was. Five and a third innings, six strikeouts, two runs — a Brandon Lowe home run in the fourth and a Jacob Gonzalez blast in the fifth the only tolls collected — and when the manager Hinch came for the ball, the ambush was sprung: Genevieve, the pitcher's seven-year-old daughter, came sprinting from the wings to seize her father at the mound. "I was totally blindsided, and it hit me like a ton of bricks," said the man who has stared down October unblinking. "I was sobbing." He walked off with his family, which is, on reflection, how a man ought to leave.

The game itself then insisted on matching the occasion. Pittsburgh, its third run supplied by Bryan Reynolds' eighth-inning triple, carried a 3-1 lead into the ninth, and Detroit answered as if scripted: Kevin McGonigle's groundout cashed Max Clark to make it 3-2, and Eduardo Valencia, with Hao-Yu Lee aboard, struck the walk-off blow — his seventh home run, and none will ever be dearer — for a 4-3 verdict, Waguespack collecting the decision. Hollywood, the beat men agreed, would have rejected the draft as too neat.

The ledger closes at five hundred fifty-seven starts and a strikeout account that stands ten clear of one Max Scherzer — of whom the farewell desk below has this very morning's business to report.

News Around the League

THE OCTOBER DESK: THE SEASON'S LAST PAGE IS WRITTEN THIS AFTERNOON — PHILADELPHIA AND ARIZONA SETTLE THE NATIONAL'S LAST BERTH, HOUSTON AND TEXAS SETTLE THE WEST, AND THE LOSER OF THAT LATTER QUARREL GOES HOME OUTRIGHT

Game one hundred sixty-two arrives with two questions still open, and every one of the afternoon's fifteen contests done by suppertime — the whole schedule, fittingly, played in daylight.

The first question is the one this paper has chronicled through a fortnight of Philadelphia agonies: the Phillies, thumped 12-1 by Tampa Bay on Saturday for a fourth consecutive defeat — their bats managing but one unearned run to snap a one-and-twenty-inning scoreless drought — nonetheless still hold the National's final wild card by a single game over Arizona, whose own six-game winning streak was halted in San Diego. The arithmetic is mercifully plain. Philadelphia wins this afternoon against the Rays, with the ace Wheeler taking the ball, and it claims a fifth consecutive October; Philadelphia loses, and it survives only if Arizona also falls at San Diego, the tiebreaker being already in the desert's pocket, as this paper has noted. Lose while Arizona wins, and the Phillies complete what their own scribes are calling the second-worst collapse in the franchise's long and collapse-acquainted history.

The second question is the West's, and it is winner-take-all in the fullest sense: Houston and Texas stand level at eighty wins and one-and-eighty losses, and with all three of the American's wild cards long since spoken for — New York, Boston, and the South Siders — the loser of this race simply goes home. Houston, holding the Silver Boot tiebreaker in its saddlebag still, is champion of the West with a victory at West Sacramento or a Texas defeat in Minnesota; Texas must win and have Houston lose. And when the smoke clears, Tuesday opens the Wild Card rounds: Boston at New York in the Bronx, the Cubs at San Diego by the bay — and the West's new champion entertaining the South Siders.

THE FAREWELL DESK: TEN STRIKEOUTS APART AFTER FORTY YEARS OF SHARED HARNESS — SCHERZER TAKES A TORONTO MOUND THIS AFTERNOON WITH THE LEDGER, AND PERHAPS HIS OWN CAREER, IN THE BALANCE

The game's two great contemporaries have jockeyed for the same laurels since they shared a Detroit rotation from 2010 through '14, and they will not stop merely because one of them has retired. With Verlander's six strikeouts in Saturday's farewell, his all-time account stands precisely ten clear of Max Scherzer on the career leaderboard — and Scherzer, who is not ready to declare his own intentions, takes the mound for Toronto against Cincinnati this very afternoon, knowing it could be his final turn. Between them the two have banked four hundred ninety victories, six thousand six hundred seventeen and a third innings, and six Cy Young Awards; the strikeout ledger is the last account still open. Scherzer, for his part, will render no verdict on retirement until the winter — he means, he says, to see how things unfold. The Herald will keep the vigil it kept for his old stablemate: attend, Toronto, on the chance there is not another.

THE HONORS DESK: A LETTER 'C' IS STITCHED WHERE NONE HAS EVER BEEN — YELICH IS MADE THE FIRST CAPTAIN IN MILWAUKEE'S HISTORY, WITH YOUNT'S BLESSING SOUGHT AND GIVEN FIRST

Before Saturday's 3-2 dispatch of St. Louis, the club with the game's best record did a thing it had never done in its half-century and more: it named Christian Yelich captain and stitched the letter to his jersey. The man's lone condition does him as much credit as the honor: he would not accept until Robin Yount — in Yelich's own reckoning the true first captain, though no letter ever said so — had been asked and had given his wholehearted blessing. "Without his blessing," said Yelich, "I wouldn't have wanted to accept it." Someone in the clubhouse carried the idea to the manager Murphy, who walked it up through the owner Attanasio and the baseball chief Arnold before putting it to Yelich, and thence to Yount. A club sitting on a hundred and two victories has little need of ceremony; it made room for this one anyway, and rightly.

THE MILESTONE DESK: CAMINERO RINGS UP FORTY AND A HUNDRED FOR A SECOND CONSECUTIVE YEAR — AND IN A LOST COLORADO SEASON, GOODMAN STRIKES HIS FORTIETH AND ONE-AND-FORTIETH IN A SINGLE EVENING

Junior Caminero keeps three numbers dear — forty, one hundred, and one hundred sixty-two — and on Saturday in Philadelphia he banked the second of them for the second year running. His first-inning two-run blast off Aaron Nola, home run number two-and-forty, carried him past a hundred runs driven in for the season; the fortieth homer had come on the eleventh of September, on the first walk-off blow of his life, the same swing that clinched Tampa Bay's return to October. "We were looking for another season with 100 RBIs," said the young third baseman. "Thank God we complete it." There remains the third number: he means to start every one of the hundred sixty-two, and this afternoon closes the books.

Colorado's evening in Chicago, meanwhile, produced the season's least-heralded slugging landmark: Hunter Goodman struck his fortieth home run in the third inning and his one-and-fortieth — a three-run affair — in the ninth, a pair of thunderclaps from a club fifty-eight and one hundred three on the year. And atop the American's home-run table, Pete Alonso holds at three-and-forty, the sole proprietor of the crown he seized from a four-way knot on Friday, as this paper recorded, with one afternoon remaining to any challenger.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: TREINEN'S SHOULDER GIVES WAY IN THE BULLPEN AND HIS OCTOBER IS OVER BEFORE IT BEGAN — MIZE JOINS THE INVALID LIST IN SAN DIEGO, AND THE WARD'S EARLIER GUESTS AWAIT THEIR VERDICTS

The cruelest entry of the day required no batted ball at all: Blake Treinen, warming in the Dodger bullpen during Saturday's 4-3 victory by the bay, injured his shoulder in the act of preparing, and the manager Roberts pronounced the season — and thus the postseason — finished for him. "I don't know if it was a complete blowout, but really unfortunate," said Roberts, with imaging to come and the invalid list certain. In San Diego, Casey Mize — whose difficult Friday evening this paper recorded — was placed on the fifteen-day list with a sprained right ankle, Gavin Sheets returning from the invalid rolls in the corresponding move. The ward's earlier admissions hold their beds: Corbin Burnes, shelved Friday with the inflamed shoulder, will seek a second opinion before any next step is chosen, and Luis Arraez's examined ankle — the sprain is pronounced a serious one — leaves his availability for whatever October Philadelphia can still reach very much in doubt.

The Rest of the Card

DOWN THREE IN THE FIRST, THE FOREST CITY NINE ANSWER WITH ELEVEN AND TAKE THE CENTRAL ENTIRE — A THIRD STRAIGHT CROWN, A SEVENTH IN ELEVEN YEARS, AND A BYE BESIDES: GUARDIANS 11, ROYALS 5

Cleveland spotted Kansas City three runs in the first at Kauffman Stadium and then buried the kindness beneath fourteen hits: Patrick Bailey and Brayan Rocchio struck two home runs apiece, DeLauter added his sixteenth, and a four-run ninth sealed an 11-5 verdict — whereupon the White Sox' defeat in Chicago made the clinch official. It is the Central crown for a third consecutive year and the seventh in the last eleven, and with it the American's second seed and the first-round bye, Cleveland resting until Saturday next, the third of October, when the Division Series opens at Progressive Field against the South Siders or the West's survivor. This paper reminds its readers that this club stood one game over the break-even line in early July, minus Ramírez and his mended hand; nine victories in its last ten games is how such a story ends.

SEVENTEEN HITS AND A MILESTONE IN THE FIRST INNING — THE AMERICAN'S BEST TUNE THEIR ENGINES ON PHILADELPHIA'S MISERY: RAYS 12, BROTHERLY LOVE NINE 1

Caminero's two-run first-inning blast off Nola — his two-and-fortieth, and the swing that carried him past a hundred runs driven in, as the milestone desk sings — opened a procession of seventeen hits, Matz taking the decision as Tampa Bay banked victory eight-and-ninety, its fifteenth in one-and-twenty games. For Philadelphia the defeat was a fourth in a row at the cruelest possible hour, and the October desk sets out the arithmetic of the afternoon on which everything now rides.

MACHADO'S THIRTIETH CAPS THE SEEDING QUESTION AND THE SNAKES' STREAK DIES AT SIX — THE MISSION NINE WILL GREET CHICAGO AT HOME TUESDAY: MISSION NINE 10, SNAKES 7

San Diego struck early and often — Machado's three-run thirtieth and Bogaerts' thirteenth the heaviest blows in a thirteen-hit evening — to lock up the National's fourth seed, a third consecutive ninety-win campaign, and a home date when the Wild Card round opens Tuesday at Petco Park — against the very Cubs who ended San Diego's October at Wrigley Field a year ago. Arizona's six-game winning streak perished honorably, a three-run ninth off Mason Miller falling short at 10-7; the visitors' whole season now hangs on this afternoon, as the October desk records.

THE BOTTOM OF THE ORDER CARRIES THE LONE STAR NINE TO THE BRINK OF GAME ONE HUNDRED SIXTY-TWO — FOUR DOUBLES IN A SECOND-INNING BURST KEEP THE WEST ALIVE: LONE STAR NINE 6, MINNESOTANS 2

Shorn of Pederson and Jung, Texas got its rescue from the order's cheap seats: Jansen singled home a run and later homered, and Carter, Osuna, and Nimmo rattled doubles in a four-run second, Eovaldi supplying what his manager called one of the gutsiest starts he has seen before Garcia collected the decision, 6-2. Burger's eight-and-twentieth added the trimming; the wage of the evening is a share of first in the West at eighty wins apiece with Houston, and one afternoon to settle it.

PEÑA STRIKES TWICE, ALVAREZ MATCHES CAMINERO AT TWO-AND-FORTY, AND SPENCE'S LEGEND GROWS — THE HOUSTON NINE ARRIVE AT THE FINAL DAY LEVEL AND ARMED: HOUSTON NINE 13, ATHLETICS 2

A night after spilling six runs through a dropped relay, Houston administered a sixteen-hit correction at West Sacramento: Peña homered twice, Alvarez struck a three-run two-and-fortieth to stand level with Caminero on the American's table, and Lucas Spence — the taxi-squad thunderbolt of whom this paper sang on Friday — doubled and homered besides, Wesneski cruising to the 13-2 decision. The Silver Boot tiebreaker rides with them into this afternoon: win, or watch Texas lose, and the West is Houston's.

GOODMAN'S TWO THUNDERCLAPS GIVE THE MILE HIGH NINE THE LAST WORD OF A LOST SEASON — THE SOUTH SIDERS' TUNE-UP TURNS SOUR IN A FOUR-RUN NINTH: MILE HIGH NINE 9, SOUTH SIDERS 6

Colorado, one hundred three defeats deep and owing nobody anything, played spoiler with relish: Goodman struck his fortieth and — in a four-run ninth — his one-and-fortieth, a three-run blow, while Tovar added his thirteenth in a 9-6 verdict at Rate Field. Chicago's answer of three in the home ninth, Benintendi's fifteenth among it, arrived too late; the defeat cost the wild-card men nothing but tidiness, though it handed Cleveland the Central, as the card's top line attests.

TURANG'S TWO-RUN ANSWER MAKES IT ONE HUNDRED TWO ON THE CAPTAIN'S FIRST DAY — MAY STATES HIS OCTOBER CASE IN THREE HOT INNINGS: MILWAUKEES 3, CARDINALS 2

On the afternoon Yelich took the letter C — the honors desk tells the tale — Milwaukee fell behind on Burleson's six-and-twentieth and answered with Chourio's run-scoring single and Brice Turang's go-ahead two-run blast, his one-and-twentieth, for victory number one hundred two, the relief corps throwing six scoreless behind Dustin May's three striking innings as the club weighs its October rotation. Harrison took the decision over Mathews; St. Louis managed but three hits and made them all count for nearly enough.

HERNÁNDEZ'S NINTH-INNING BLOW SETTLES A SEESAW BY THE BAY — THE BOYS IN BLUE BANK NINE-AND-NINETY WHILE THE WARD CLAIMS TREINEN: BOYS IN BLUE 4, FRISCO NINE 3

Betts' three-and-twentieth had the Dodgers ahead early and San Francisco's two late runs had them level, until Enrique Hernández — October's oldest friend — struck a ninth-inning home run, his fourth, for the 4-3 decision, Scott sealing it. The win was the club's nine-and-ninetieth; its cost, set out at the infirmary ward, was the shoulder of Treinen, injured merely warming to enter.

ONE HIT ONE NIGHT, TEN THE NEXT — THE CROWNED BRAVES RESTORE ORDER AT THE GULF STREAM NINE'S EXPENSE: BRAVES 8, GULF STREAM NINE 3

A night after Eury Pérez silenced them on a single hit — a deed this paper duly sang — the champions of the East remembered themselves: Harris II crossed the plate thrice and doubled a run home, Dominic Smith and Drake Baldwin supplied home runs, and Mederos ran his mark to 6-1 in an 8-3 verdict at loanDepot park. Atlanta enters the season's final afternoon at four-and-ninety wins, the National's third seed.

MAURICIO STRIKES TWICE AND THE METROPOLITANS SPOIL THE SENATORS' SATURDAY — THREE HITS ARE ALL WASHINGTON CAN SHOW FOR NINE INNINGS: METROPOLITANS 7, SENATORS 1

New York's evening turned on a four-run seventh — Ewing's three-run blast, his eleventh, the centerpiece — and Ronny Mauricio homered in the seventh and again in the ninth as the Mets prevailed 7-1, Núñez taking the decision. Washington, held to three hits entire, thus saw its three-game winning streak — the third leg of which this paper recorded only yesterday — expire on the season's next-to-last evening.

BLEDAY'S BLAST AND RODRÍGUEZ'S TRIPLE SEND THE CINCINNATIS HOME WINNERS — TORONTO'S BATS OFFER FIVE HITS TO THE CAUSE: CINCINNATIS 5, JAYS 1

Bleday's four-and-twentieth, Suárez's run-scoring double, and Héctor Rodríguez's two-run triple gave Cincinnati a 5-1 verdict at the Rogers Centre, Lowder besting Yesavage on a night Toronto mustered five hits. The same grounds host weightier business this afternoon: Scherzer takes the Toronto mound against these same Reds, with the farewell desk's ledger — and perhaps a career — in the balance.

THE ANGELS TIE IT WITH SMALL BALL AND THE PUGET SOUND MEN ANSWER WITH THUNDER — CRAWFORD'S TWO-RUN BLOW CAPS A FOUR-RUN SEVENTH: PUGET SOUND MEN 8, ANGELS 4

Two clubs long excused from October staged a lively argument anyway: Los Angeles squared the account at four apiece in the sixth by way of a squeeze bunt and assorted industry, whereupon Seattle poured four across the seventh — Ward's two-run single and J.P. Crawford's fourteenth home run the freight — for an 8-4 verdict, Vargas improving to 3-0. Trout, be it noted, reached second base in the tying rally; the season permits him one more afternoon.

The Sunday tables, then — thirteen contests entered, a farewell wept over in Detroit, and a season that settles its last two arguments this very afternoon, every one of the day's fifteen games played in daylight and done by supper. The stakes, briefly: Philadelphia holds the National's last wild card by one over Arizona — Tampa Bay at Philadelphia at 3:05, Wheeler up; Arizona at San Diego at 3:10 — while Houston and Texas, level at eighty wins, settle the West and the loser's entire October at 3:05 in West Sacramento and 3:10 in Minneapolis respectively, the tiebreaker Houston's. Scherzer, perhaps for the last time, takes the ball in Toronto at 3:07; Boston and the Cubs close the books beneath a Florida roof at 3:05, as this paper recorded Saturday; and Cleveland's newly crowned Central champions play out the string at Kansas City at 3:10 with nothing at hazard but habit. Come Tuesday the Wild Card rounds open — Boston at New York, Chicago at San Diego, and the South Siders at the West's new champion — and this paper trades its summer economy for October's. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
