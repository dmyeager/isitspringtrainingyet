Volume I · No. 70 · Wednesday, the Sixteenth of September, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

TWO OUT IN THE NINTH, TWO RUNNERS WAITING, AND THE HOUSE CALLS THE MAN'S NAME BEFORE HE SWINGS — ROCCHIO'S DOUBLE TURNS THE CENTRAL ON ITS HEAD: GUARDIANS 7, WHITE SOX 6

Cleveland answers Monday's sermon in the last of the ninth: Bazzana strikes twice, Adell once, the pinch-runners Schneemann and Fry come flying home on the shortstop's two-run blow, and the division's argument narrows to half a game with the rubber match this very afternoon

They were chanting his name before the ball was ever pitched — "Ro-key-oh, Ro-key-oh," rolling down from every corner of the Cleveland park with two out in the last of the ninth, the tying and winning runs in scoring position, and the season's sternest argument leaning over the rail. Brayan Rocchio keeps a private arrangement with this month of the calendar; the natives have taken to calling it "Rocctember," and seven hits of his career have now ended games outright, four of them in Septembers. Against him stood the young Chicago fireman Taylor and a sinker that touches one hundred; behind him stood a 6-5 deficit with every look of a back-breaker about it. "That's the best thing ever, when I hear 'Ro-key-oh,'" the shortstop deposed afterward. "I can't disappoint the crowd when they're doing that." He did not: a double lined to left, his two-and-twentieth, and the pinch-runners Schneemann and Fry came flying home together. In the dugout the closer Smith, whose one-and-two-thirds hitless innings had kept the door ajar, confessed his only thought had been this is just too perfect.

The evening had opened as though Cleveland meant to settle Monday's accounts early. Jo Adell and Travis Bazzana struck back-to-back home runs in the first — Adell's two-and-twentieth with Kwan aboard, then Bazzana behind him — and the Chicago opener Murphy was gone inside two-thirds of an inning. But the Chicagos are not half a game from October by accident: they clawed level and then past, a four-run sixth built of Meidroth's single, Grichuk's sacrifice fly, and Vargas's two-run knock turning a two-run deficit into a 6-4 lead and letting the air out of the house. Bazzana put half of it back at once with his second homer of the night, the fourteenth of his season, and there the matter froze until the ninth could be arranged.

The arithmetic this morning is the connoisseur's kind. The clubs stand level at seven-and-seventy wins apiece, the whole difference a single defeat; Chicago's lead is half a game, though the White Sox still carry the season-series tiebreaker banked Monday night — as this paper reported yesterday — so Cleveland must finish ahead, not merely level, to claim a third straight Central title. The victory also stretched the Lake Erie men's hold on the American's last wild-card chair to a game and a half over Texas, with ten to play. "It's just so hard to get a hit, but in those situations he seems to come through," said the manager Vogt of his shortstop, calling it "a huge, huge moment," which from that economical man is practically a sonnet. The rubber match goes this very afternoon at ten past one — day baseball in September with first place riding on it, which is about all this correspondent has ever asked of the sport.

News Around the League

THE PENNANT DESK: A FOURTH STRAIGHT CENTRAL CROWN FOR MILWAUKEE — A THING NO CLUB HAS DONE SINCE THE DIVISION WAS DRAWN — AND THE CHAMPAGNE PHOTOGRAPH IS DOWN TO THREE MEN

The Suds men beat Pittsburgh five-to-one — the card below has the particulars — then repaired to the visitors' clubhouse at PNC Park to watch by wire as Chicago lost at Wrigley, and when that final out was recorded, the National's Central belonged to Milwaukee for the fourth consecutive year. The division was drawn in 1994; no club had won it four years running until last night. Five of the last six now, and six of the last nine. "These things never get old, and doing it four years in a row with these guys means the world to me," said the operations chief Arnold, who thanked "everyone in Wisconsin" for good measure.

On the mound for the champagne photograph stood three men: the owner Attanasio, the former Most Valuable Christian Yelich, and the pitcher Brandon Woodruff — the last souls remaining from the club of 2018 that began this run of celebrations. The photograph shrinks each year; Peralta stood in it last autumn and was traded to the Mets in January. Woodruff's presence is itself a campaign: shelved by the latest shoulder setback, he is throwing at one hundred five feet and will be examined at the month's end for a return to the mound — a long chance, but a better prognosis than the surgery once scheduled. "These guys are hungry. We play together as a team," said Yelich. "Hopefully finish this thing off strong and go into October on a high note." At four-and-ninety victories — the best record in all the game — the Brewers have every appearance of meaning to.

THE MILESTONE DESK: THE PROMISE OF YESTERDAY'S COLUMNS IS KEPT — ALONSO'S THREE-HUNDREDTH HOME RUN, STRUCK IN THE NINTH, IN THE PARK THAT RAISED HIM

One blow shy, these columns wrote yesterday of Pete Alonso's homecoming; the blow arrived within the day. Facing Senga with Baltimore a run down in the ninth at Citi Field, Alonso lined the three-hundredth home run of his career to left-center — in the very park where he struck the first two hundred four-and-sixty of them, more than any Met who ever lived — and the milestone landed as a game-tying blow against his old nine, in a game his new one would win in eleven innings, as the card records. "It was like a movie," offered his teammate Baz. Not even the picture-houses would dare the script: the first series of his life in visiting colors, the ovations of Monday, and then the round number delivered at the old address with the game in the balance. The man himself kept the ledger modest: "No. 300, it's a nice round number. But I'm just so proud of this group. The way we fought today, it's an unbelievable team win." The homer was his six-and-thirtieth of the season — the first-year dividend on the five-year compact he signed with Baltimore in December — and his one hundred one runs driven in lead the American League.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: LUZARDO GOES TO THE SHELF WITH OCTOBER UNPROMISED, BAZ TAKES A COMEBACKER ON THE BONE AND KEEPS HIS TURN, AND OHTANI PICKS UP A BAT AGAIN

The verdict this paper has awaited since Sunday is in, and Philadelphia will not like it: Jesús Luzardo, scratched from his Atlanta start last week with a stiff shoulder, was placed on the fifteen-day invalid list with inflammation, the stint dated back to Saturday. The throwing session that was to clear him never came; the shoulder declined to cooperate. "Trying to get the inflammation to calm down," said the All-Star left-hander, whose readiness for October the club would not venture to promise. The lefty Backhus comes up from Lehigh Valley to fill the vacancy.

In New York the fright was sharper and briefer. Shane Baz took a comebacker off Bichette's bat — the machines clocked it at one hundred five miles to the hour — square on the pitching elbow in the fifth, and knelt behind the mound in visible pain before leaving. The X-rays came back clean, and the man expects to keep his next turn. "It just got me right in the bone. Kind of just went numb," he said. "I was just playing it safe."

And from the western front, a bulletin of the better kind: Shohei Ohtani, shut down a week since with the balky biceps and knee, swung a bat off a tee in Los Angeles and came away feeling well — his first swings since the shelving. The invalid list holds him until the three-and-twentieth at the earliest, when five games would remain to the champions, and the manager Roberts expects him back in regular-season dress: "Today was positive, and hopefully we can just keep building towards that."

THE LEDGER DESK: THE BATTING RACES AT ELEVEN MORNINGS TO RUN — A DEAD HEAT IN THE AMERICAN'S STOLEN BASES, AND A WASHINGTON FLYER NINE CLEAR IN THE NATIONAL

With eleven mornings left to the ledger, the columns of figures now run pennant races of their own. In the American: Houston's Yordan Alvarez bats .310 to lead the averages, Tampa Bay's Simpson at .308 in close pursuit; Junior Caminero's one-and-forty home runs lead the loop — the latest of them last night's walk-off, as the card sings — with Alvarez himself at nine-and-thirty; Baltimore's Alonso, as the milestone desk records, tops the runs driven in at one hundred one; and the theft race stands a dead heat, Simpson and Witt Jr. at one-and-forty apiece with the Yankee Chisholm Jr. a single bag behind. In the National: Arraez holds the averages at .315; Kyle Schwarber, whose five-and-fortieth flew last night in a losing cause, leads the homers with Crow-Armstrong at two-and-forty; Burleson of St. Louis tops the runs driven in at one hundred eleven; and the Washington flyer Nuñez has six-and-forty stolen bases, a comfortable nine clear of the field. Eleven mornings. The abacus trembles.

THE REUNION DESK: THE MONARCHS OF SOUTH PHILADELPHIA MEET AGAIN AT BUSCH — MO'NE DAVIS IN THE STANDS, HER OLD TEAMMATE IN A GIANTS UNIFORM

Among the near eight-and-twenty thousand at St. Louis on Tuesday sat Mo'ne Davis, come to watch her longtime friend and former Little League teammate Scott Bandura wear a major-league uniform for the Giants in their ten-run evening. The friendship dates to 2008 and the Anderson Monarchs, the South Philadelphia youth program Bandura's father Steve has run since 1995, where a seven-year-old girl chasing footballs in the outfield caught the coach's eye. Two decades on, both are professionals: Davis plays for the Los Angeles Queens of the Women's Professional Baseball League, whose inaugural championship series opens Wednesday in nearby Springfield, Illinois — a coincidence of schedules she declined to waste. "This has always been his dream, to be in the bigs," she said. "So for me to come watch him play is pretty cool." One sandlot, two professional leagues; the Herald tips its cap to the man dragging the infield who saw it first.

The Rest of the Card

FOR EIGHT INNINGS THE RUNS ARRIVE ONLY BY MISADVENTURE — THEN CAMINERO ENDS IT PROPERLY, HIS SECOND WALK-OFF BLOW IN FIVE DAYS: TAMPA MEN 2, WHITE ELEPHANTS 1

Through eight the scoring was all mishap — Simpson home on a passed ball, Bolte answering on a wild pitch — while Perkins (six-and-two-thirds, eight strikeouts) and Jax (five spotless, seven) pitched better than the ledger will remember. Then the ninth, and Junior Caminero: a homer down the right-field line off Alvarado, his one-and-fortieth, which leads all the American — and his second game-ending blow in five days, the first having clinched the club's return to October. The Rays stand one-and-ninety and nine-and-fifty, first in the East by three and a half.

AFTER THE POLAR BEAR TIES IT, MAYO WINS IT WITHOUT THE BALL EVER LEAVING THE LAWN — AN INSIDE-THE-PARK THREE-RUN BLOW IN THE ELEVENTH: BIRDS 7, FLUSHING NINE 5

The milestone desk has the full account of Alonso's three-hundredth, which squared the game in the ninth; here let the record show the stranger thunder that followed. Baltimore nosed ahead in the tenth on a wild pitch and was pulled level again by Semien's double — whereupon Coby Mayo opened the eleventh with a fly ball to center that found neither glove nor fence, and circled the bases behind Taveras and Beavers for a three-run inside-the-park home run, his four-and-twentieth. Bichette answered with one; Kittredge banked his tenth save. Encarnacion-Strand and Jackson had homered early in a game that offered nearly everything.

A SIXTH STRAIGHT FOR DETROIT, TAKEN BY NINE — TORKELSON STRIKES A THREE-RUN BLOW AND STEALS THE FIRST BASE OF HIS SEASON FOR GARNISH: MOTOR CITY NINE 10, TORONTOS 1

Four in the fourth settled it — Clark's double, then Spencer Torkelson's four-and-twentieth with two aboard — and Detroit piled on serenely from there, Lee adding his ninth homer in a two-run ninth. Anderson gave five scoreless for the victory; Lorenzen, reached for five runs in two innings, fell to three-and-thirteen. Six straight now for the Motor City nine, while Toronto, losers of two running, slips two full games from the last wild-card chair.

FRIED CARVES SEVEN QUIET INNINGS WITHOUT A WALK, AND THE ROOKIE JONES SUPPLIES THE EXCLAMATION — A THIRD STRAIGHT FOR THE PURSUIT: YANKEES 8, NORTH STARS 1

Max Fried went seven innings of one-run ball, six strikeouts and not a walk among them — the manner of start Octobers are made of — while Bellinger opened the scoring with a two-run sixteenth and the rookie Spencer Jones crowned a four-run sixth with a three-run homer, his tenth. New York has won three straight and eight of ten, holds the East's second chair three and a half behind Tampa Bay, and carries its October ticket already punched. Minnesota has dropped three running.

SCHWARBER'S FIVE-AND-FORTIETH IS TRUMPED BY A BOY NAMED LILE — THREE RUNS OFF THE SEVENTEEN-GAME WINNER, AND A FOURTH STRAIGHT FOR THE CAPITAL: WASHINGTONS 6, PHILS 3

Cristopher Sánchez arrived seventeen-and-five and departed seventeen-and-six, reached for five runs in six-and-a-third — the decisive three on Daylen Lile's one-and-twentieth home run in the seventh. Schwarber's five-and-fortieth, which leads the National, had briefly restored Philadelphia's lead in the fifth; Chaparro answered it within the half-inning. Washington has won four straight while playing out the string, and Philadelphia — five and a half behind Atlanta — sees its wild-card cushion over San Diego shaved to a single game.

THREE TWO-RUN HOMERS UNDO A THREE-RUN LEAD — ACUÑA, ALBIES, AND BALDWIN STRIKE IN TURN, AND THE EVENING COSTS CHICAGO MORE THAN THE GAME: BRAVES 6, NORTH SIDERS 3

Chicago led three-nought after four with Gausman dealing; then Atlanta's answer came in matched pairs. Ronald Acuña Jr. in the sixth, Ozzie Albies in the seventh off the luckless Webb, Drake Baldwin in the ninth — two-run homers all, the eighteenth, two-and-twentieth, and four-and-twentieth respectively — and Iglesias closed for his three-and-thirtieth save. The defeat did double damage on the North Side: the Cubs' wild-card lead holds at a game and a half, but the Central was surrendered outright to Milwaukee, as the pennant desk records.

FIVE MORE SPOTLESS INNINGS FROM THE UNANIMOUS ONE, AND A CROWN ARRIVES BY WIRE FROM WRIGLEY — THE BOTTOM OF THE ORDER CARRIES THE CLINCHER: SUDS MEN 5, BUCCANEERS 1

Jacob Misiorowski — the unanimous first choice of the MLB.com Cy Young poll these columns reported yesterday, the award itself still unvoted — gave five scoreless with seven strikeouts, running his account to fifteen-and-five, an earned-run figure of 1.89, and two hundred three-and-forty strikeouts, all of them the game's best. The carrying was done from the order's cellar: the rookie Lara and the shortstop Ortiz, two runs driven in apiece. Lowe's thirtieth homer was Pittsburgh's whole reply. The victory set the table and the Cubs' defeat served the feast — a fourth consecutive Central crown, as the pennant desk sings above.

ONE HIT IN SEVEN INNINGS — YAMAMOTO TOYS WITH CINCINNATI, AND HERNÁNDEZ AND THE ROOKIE FREELAND FURNISH THE MARGIN: DODGERS 4, QUEEN CITY NINE 0

Yoshinobu Yamamoto allowed a single hit across seven innings, striking out seven for his fourteenth victory, and the whitewash was completed by Stewart and Scott behind him — Cincinnati's whole evening amounting to three hits. Teoscar Hernández struck a two-run fifteenth in the third; the rookie Freeland added a solo homer and a double besides. The clinched champions have won two straight in Cincinnati with their great convalescent swinging a bat again at home, as the ward reports.

SEVEN RUNS INSIDE THREE INNINGS AND A NIGHT OF FIRSTS — HILL'S MAIDEN HOMER OF THE YEAR, TIDWELL'S MAIDEN VICTORY: FRISCO NINE 10, MOUND CITY NINE 3

Bryce Eldridge opened the evening with a two-run homer, his seventeenth, and added a run-scoring double late for three driven in; Turner Hill matched the three, capping the account in the ninth with his first home run of the year. The rookie Tidwell gave six-and-two-thirds for his first victory of the season while Pallante absorbed eight runs in five innings. St. Louis's consolation was Burleson's three-and-twentieth homer — his one hundred eleven runs driven in still lead the National, as the ledger desk attests.

FOUR IN THE SECOND AND DEGROM MAKES THEM STAND — EIGHT STRIKEOUTS IN FIVE, AND A THIRD STRAIGHT KEEPS TEXAS A GAME FROM THE WEST'S CHAIR: ARLINGTON MEN 4, CRIMSON HOSE 2

All the scoring that mattered came in a four-run second — Díaz doubling two home, Seager singling in two more — and Jacob deGrom made it stand with eight strikeouts across five innings for his eleventh victory, the bullpen and Latz's thirtieth save finishing the ledger. Sogard's two-run homer was Boston's whole account. Texas has won three straight: one game behind Houston in the West, a game and a half from Cleveland's wild-card chair, and suddenly the most dangerous club nobody has seated. Boston's own cushion holds at five and a half.

PEÑA STRIKES TWICE AND THE LEAGUE'S LEADING BATSMAN ONCE, LOUDLY — TEN STRIKEOUTS IN FIVE FOR BROWN, AND THE WEST'S EDGE HOLDS AT ONE: HOUSTONS 4, KANSAS CITY NINE 2

Jeremy Peña homered in the first and again in the third, and Yordan Alvarez — batting .310, the American's best — struck a two-run nine-and-thirtieth in the fifth, which closed the evening's arithmetic. Hunter Brown fanned ten across five innings for the victory and Hader banked his six-and-twentieth save; Pasquantino's twelfth homer and Jensen's double made the visitors' account. Houston keeps the West by a single game over Texas, both clubs having won.

THE EIGHT-GAME PROCESSION DIES IN THE MOUNTAIN AIR — CARRIGG STRIKES TWICE AND DRIVES IN FIVE FOR THE GAME'S POOREST CLUB: ROX 9, FRIARS 3

San Diego did its early business — Tatis's three-and-twentieth, Hays, and Campusano all homered inside five innings — and led by a run before the floor gave way: five runs in the sixth, crowned by Cole Carrigg's second homer of the night, and two more in the seventh with Johnston doubling in each frame. Carrigg's ledger read two homers and five driven in; Feltner followed with five scoreless innings of relief for the victory. So the eight-game streak that matched San Diego's season best — chronicled in yesterday's columns — perishes against the worst record in the game, though the Friars keep the National's last chair, two and a half ahead of Arizona, whose evening went no better, as the item below records.

THREE IN THE ELEVENTH ON RUIZ'S BASES-CLEARING DOUBLE, AND ARIZONA'S CHANCE TO CLOSE GOES BEGGING: FISH 4, SONORAN NINE 2, IN ELEVEN INNINGS

Moreno's fourteenth homer opened the account in the first; Conine's sixteenth squared it in the fourth; and there the game sat through seven innings of scoreless attrition. In the eleventh Esteury Ruiz emptied the bases with a double to center — Serna, Stowers, and Lopez all scoring — and Marte's answering double bought Arizona back only the one. The defeat is the costlier kind: on a night San Diego lost too, the Sonoran nine could not close an inch of the two and a half games between themselves and the last chair, and ten remain to them.

LOWE'S TWELFTH IS THE WHOLE DIFFERENCE, AND JOHNSON MATCHES GILBERT PITCH FOR PITCH: ANAHEIM NINE 2, SEATTLES 1

Grissom's double and Rodríguez's answering one traded single runs in the fifth and sixth; then Josh Lowe struck his twelfth homer, and no one scored again. The Angel starter Johnson gave six innings of one-run ball with seven strikeouts to best Logan Gilbert — six innings and seven strikeouts himself, and the loss to twelve-and-ten, such being a pitcher's justice — and Watson closed for his third save. Two straight now for Los Angeles, playing spoiler with conviction.

The Wednesday tables, then — with Milwaukee's fourth pennant of the Central now sewn upon them. In the American: clinched Tampa Bay holds the East by three and a half over clinched New York, Boston nine and a half astern; the Central is the half-game argument the lead columns sing, Chicago over Cleveland with the tiebreaker banked; the West stands Houston by one over Texas, both winners last night. The wild-card row: New York and Boston seated in comfort, the last chair Cleveland's by a game and a half over Texas, with Toronto two back and Baltimore three. In the National: Atlanta by five and a half over Philadelphia, whose rotation the ward's news has lightened; crowned Milwaukee by ten and a half over Chicago; the clinched champions by ten over San Diego. The wild-card ledger: the Cubs at the row's head by a game and a half, Philadelphia a game above the cut, San Diego in the third chair by two and a half over Arizona, with Pittsburgh seven back and Miami and St. Louis seven and a half. Tonight's bill is again the full board of fifteen, verified against the schedule, and the desk commends: at ten past one, day baseball with first place in the balance — the rubber match of the Central's great argument at Cleveland; at 6:40 the White Elephants try the Rays again; at 6:45 Philadelphia, wounded in the ward, resumes at Washington; and at 9:40 Arizona gets the Fish once more with its season thinning. Eleven mornings remain to the season's final page on the seven-and-twentieth; the Wild Card rounds open on the nine-and-twentieth. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
