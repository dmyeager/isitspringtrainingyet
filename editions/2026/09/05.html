Reporting 16 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

A GRAND SLAM TO COME LEVEL AND A WALK-OFF BLAST TO END IT — ROCCHIO JOINS A LIST FIVE NAMES LONG SINCE 1924: GUARDIANS 7, TIGERS 6

Down six runs to one by the third inning of a doubleheader's opening chapter, Cleveland is restored by one man's two swings: Brayan Rocchio's first career grand slam levels the account in the fourth, his first-pitch blast over the nineteen-foot wall in the ninth ends it — five runs driven in, a page in the Elias ledger beside Greenberg and Miñoso, and the nightcap, as the card below sings, obliged with a second walk-off besides

The gentlemen of the Elias Bureau keep a very short list: men who struck a grand slam and a walk-off homer in the selfsame game. When Friday began it read five names, and the freshest of them was five-and-thirty years old — George Kelly of the 1924 Giants, Hank Greenberg of 1938, Minnie Miñoso of 1960, Otto Velez of 1980, Jack Clark of 1991. By Friday evening at Progressive Field it read six, and the sixth was Brayan Rocchio, shortstop of the Guardians, a man batting .177 since the All-Star interval and lately so far gone in frustration that he hurled his bat to the earth Thursday and wanted the counsel of two veterans to gather himself. "I told them, 'Bro, I feel so comfortable at the plate. But I don't have the results,'" Rocchio confessed of that conversation with Hedges and Lowe. Friday, the results arrived all at once.

The day had opened as badly as a September day can. Detroit battered the starter Allen for four first-inning runs — two-run singles from the rookie Malgeri and the young Max Clark — added a fifth on a double play, and a sixth on Spencer Torkelson's two-and-twentieth homer: six runs to one before the third inning was cold. The home fourth answered in two strokes: Travis Bazzana's run-scoring single, and then, with the bases full, Rocchio turning on a hung curveball from Montero and depositing it over the right-field wall — the first grand slam of his major-league life, in the first two-homer game of it, the account level at six. Four relievers — Cecconi, Yoho, Sabrowski, and Smith — held the line from there, and in the ninth Rocchio stood in against the left-hander Sommers and wasted not a single pitch: the first offering, a four-seamer, sent 405 feet over the nineteen-foot wall in left. Bedlam, a bat flung skyward, and a 7-6 verdict — struck, the scorers noted, from both sides of the plate, the twentieth such game in the club's annals and the first since José Ramírez in 2023.

The faithful of northeast Ohio have a name for this seasonal phenomenon — "Playoff Rocchio," earned by a .375 division series in 2024 and a walk-off three-run blow in last year's finale — and his manager Vogt asked only that the moment be enjoyed: "What a cool day for him today. That was really fun." The reliever Sabrowski put the clubhouse's faith plainer still: "When it's a clutch situation and he's up, we believe it's going to happen." The arithmetic was not done with the day, for a second game waited and delivered a second walk-off, as the card below relates; taken together the two verdicts keep Cleveland's last wild-card chair from a gathering crowd — the Canadians now a single game astern, Texas two, Baltimore three — with the Central's leaders but two and a half ahead. The desk merely observes that the season's great virtue is its patience: it waited until September to hand a struggling man the finest day of his baseball life.

News Around the League

THE VESTIBULE OF SPRING: THE 2027 EXHIBITION CALENDAR IS PUBLISHED — ALL THIRTY CLUBS TO OPEN ON THE NINETEENTH OF FEBRUARY, ONE HUNDRED SEVEN-AND-SIXTY MORNINGS FROM THIS ONE

The league on Friday published the whole of the 2027 spring exhibition schedule, and this paper — whose masthead exists to ask a single question of the calendar — records the answer with particular satisfaction: for the first time under the new arrangement, all thirty clubs of the Cactus and Grapefruit Leagues open on the same day, the nineteenth of February. The opening bill includes the Dodgers and White Sox at their shared Camelback Ranch grounds in Glendale and the Yankees calling at Dunedin to try the Blue Jays; the spring's closing ornaments include the Freeway Series between the Los Angeles clubs from the one-and-twentieth of March to the three-and-twentieth, and Tampa Bay receiving Toronto at Tropicana Field on the twentieth. The regular campaign follows at once: an Opening Night engagement on the four-and-twentieth of March — a date the schedule-keepers at Baseball-Reference confirm — with the traditional Opening Day of fourteen games the day after.

The desk performs the arithmetic its readers expect of it: one hundred seven-and-sixty mornings separate this edition from the first spring pitch. It is a comfort peculiar to September that even as the present season sharpens toward its last three weeks, the next one has already posted its address.

THE RESTORATION DESK: THE CAPTAIN TAKES HIS SWINGS AT LAST — JUDGE SPRAYS LINE DRIVES AT PETCO AND EYES THE HOMESTAND

The return these pages have watched hovering since midsummer drew visibly nearer Friday afternoon in San Diego, where Aaron Judge stepped into the cage on a big-league field for the first time since the one-and-thirtieth of May and sprayed line drives about the Petco outfield. The captain of the Yankees, mending from the fractured right rib he sustained in late April, called the session "checking another box"; he ran the bases besides, took fly balls in right field, and delivered a one-hop throw to third from medium depth. "I'm mad how long it has taken," the man admitted. "We're getting close. I can't wait to be back out there." What remains is live pitching — "a couple of times, at least," by his own reckoning — and the question of whether a minor-league errand precedes the return; Judge would prefer to skip it, and his manager Boone has decided nothing. The hope, by the wires' account, is the homestand the Yankees open against Colorado on the eighth, and the paper notes one date the club has already circled: the eleventh of September, when the Mets visit and, as the team announced Friday, alumni of the 2001 Yankees gather for the pregame ceremonies. "If I'm playing or not playing, it's going to be special," said Judge. The ward, for its part, would simply like its most famous patient discharged.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: TAMPA'S ACE RETURNS TO THE INVALID LIST, CLEVELAND'S SWEEP EXACTS ITS TOLL, AND A BLUE JAY LEAVES AFTER TWO INNINGS

The ward's heaviest entry is Tampa Bay's: Shane McClanahan, beaten in Arlington Thursday as yesterday's card recorded, reported tightness in the left forearm after his fifth inning there and was dispatched Friday to the fifteen-day invalid list — his second visit of the season, for a man only this year returned from two lost seasons and the surgeon's table. "Anytime a guy talks about his arm, we're going to do right by him," said the manager Cash, while the pitcher himself counseled calm: the club moved quickly, he said, "to not let this compound, not let this snowball." His ledger stands at 3.36 across 118 innings; the American's best record can absorb the absence, but October arithmetic is another matter.

Cleveland's glorious Friday, the lead column notwithstanding, sent two men to the ward's counter: Chase DeLauter left the nightcap with a sprained right wrist and is called day-to-day, and Angel Martínez departed the same game with a shoulder contusion after colliding with Rocchio in the outfield — precautionary, the club says, and the ward chooses to believe it. In Kansas City, Toronto's Jameson Taillon, activated that very day from the forearm trouble that shelved him, lasted two innings before right elbow discomfort ended his evening — the sort of sentence a training staff reads twice. The Yankees' Trent Grisham left San Diego with a balky right hamstring; and in Los Angeles the great Ohtani sat out a second consecutive game, the knee-biceps-neck catalogue this ward has tracked all week still under management. Swift knitting to every name above.

THE VALEDICTORY DESK: DETROIT APPOINTS TWO DAYS FOR VERLANDER'S FAREWELL, AND THE OLD LION WANTS ONE MORE TURN ON THE HILL

The Tigers have fixed the ceremonies: the five-and-twentieth and six-and-twentieth of September, the final home weekend, two days given over to Justin Verlander — a pregame tribute with speakers and gifts on the Friday, a charitable endowment in his name on the Saturday, bobbleheads for the first fifteen thousand through the gates, and the faithful encouraged to wear orange, that being the one color all four of his employers — Tigers, Astros, Mets, and Giants — have flown. What remains unfixed is the part the man himself cares most about: whether he pitches again before the curtain. A hamstring has set him back by weeks at a stretch, and at each setback the calendar has shortened. "I'm not giving up, but trying to be as smart as I can about not having a setback," Verlander said Friday, reporting a bullpen session near and his velocity lately "really healthy." He is equally plain about his terms: he will not take the hill hurt, and he will not take it as theatre — "I'm not trying to go out there and make a fool of myself. But if I'm healthy enough, then I don't see why I can't." The Herald, which has set down twenty years of the man's deeds, hopes the last entry is written on the mound and not merely beside it.

THE MILESTONE DESK: THIRTY AND THIRTY ON THE SAME EVENING — VARGAS AND MONTGOMERY DO WHAT NO PAIR OF WHITE STOCKINGS EVER DID

The South Side's evening produced a first the franchise has waited a century and a quarter to record: Miguel Vargas and Colson Montgomery, who began the night tied at nine-and-twenty homers apiece, each struck his thirtieth against Minnesota — the first White Sox teammates ever to reach thirty in the same season in the same game, by the certification of the STATS bureau. Vargas claimed the bragging rights in the first inning; Montgomery answered with a two-run blow in the fourth; and between them Braden Montgomery — no relation, merely a namesake — added a homer of his own, the first time the two Montgomerys have gone deep in the same game. The clubhouse race remains crowded besides: Munetaka Murakami, whose American crossing these pages have chronicled, sits at nine-and-twenty and now trails both men by one. "Doing it on the same night and being able to share with Vargas is awesome," said the younger Montgomery, and the desk notes what the milestone serves: a young club, three seasons removed from hundred-loss misery, holding first place in September. The fuller account of the evening stands in the card below.

The Rest of the Card

THE NIGHTCAP OBLIGES IN KIND — BAZZANA LEVELS IT IN THE EIGHTH AND ENDS IT IN THE TENTH: GUARDIANS 4, BENGALS 3, IN TEN INNINGS

The day's second chapter matched the first beat for beat. Detroit built a 3-0 account on Kevin McGonigle's double and singles from Lee and Valencia; Cleveland answered by degrees — Nathaniel Lowe's double in the sixth, and in the eighth Travis Bazzana, who had entered only as a substitute when DeLauter's wrist sent him to the ward's columns, taking a 2-0 fastball 389 feet to center to level it at three. In the tenth, Rocchio — who else, on this of all days — stole second and then third, and Bazzana knocked a 2-2 cutter from Kenley Jansen up the middle to end it. The sweep is the club's first of a doubleheader by two walk-offs since the nineteenth of August, 1984, and no man needed it more than the rookie second baseman, whose August ran to a .566 OPS: "It's that time of year where you've got to make stuff happen," Bazzana said, "and this team does that." Gaddis took the decision; the Bengals, twice pierced in one day, have dropped seven of their last ten.

TEXAS STRIKES ITS FIRST GRAND SLAM OF THE YEAR AND LOSES ANYWAY, AS A TEN-GAME ROAD HOODOO DIES AT ARLINGTON: RAYS 7, RANGERS 6

Tampa Bay had lost ten consecutive road engagements with the Rangers — a hoodoo of genuine vintage — and buried it under a four-run fifth: Jonathan Aranda's ground-rule double, Junior Caminero's double behind it, Mesa's single, and a run cashed on a double play, all stacked upon earlier homers from Aranda and Richie Palacios. Whereupon Arlington nearly reclaimed the evening wholesale: Elias Díaz, batting with the bases full in the seventh, struck the Rangers' first grand slam of the entire season and added a ninth-inning sacrifice fly for five runs driven in. It wanted Caminero's run-scoring single in the top of that ninth to make the verdict stand, Wells sealing his sixth save behind Nick Martinez's fourteenth victory. The freight runs two ways: the Gulf Coast club banks its four-and-eightieth win on the day its ace entered the ward, as the columns above relate, while Texas — beaten with its biggest blow of the year — slips two games behind Cleveland's chair with the same clubs meeting again at 7:05 this evening, Rasmussen opposing deGrom.

FOUR RUNS BEFORE AN OUT OF CONSEQUENCE, AND THE CANADIANS CLIMB BACK TO LEVEL AT LAST: CANADIANS 9, ROYALS 2

Toronto spent the first inning of a ninety-seven-degree Kansas City evening settling the matter — Kirk and Okamoto singling home runs, Ernie Clement doubling home two more — and spent the rest of it padding the ledger: Kirk's double in the second, a sacrifice bunt cashed in the sixth ahead of George Springer's two-run double, and the rookie Sean Keys's second homer in the ninth, with Kirk, Clement, and Springer driving in two apiece. Bobby Witt Jr. and Vinnie Pasquantino supplied Kansas City's homers in a losing cause. The night's alarm was the starter Taillon's two-inning exit, the ward above having the particulars, and the reliever Mantiply took the decision in his stead. The sum is the story: at one-and-seventy wins against the same in defeats, the Canadians stand restored to level, winners of three running and seven of ten — and their reward is a place in the morning's tightest arithmetic, one game behind Cleveland's chair.

THREE RUNNERS CUT DOWN, ONE BALL LAUNCHED, AND THE LAST CHAIR CHANGES HANDS BY TEN O'CLOCK: MISSION NINE 3, PINSTRIPES 2, IN TEN INNINGS

The catcher Luis Campusano gave Petco Park a night for the annals: he threw out Grisham in the second, Chisholm in the fifth, and — after Spencer Jones's tenth-inning single had put New York ahead — cut down Jones himself at third with a perfect throw, before turning on the first pitch of the home half's decisive moment and launching a two-run walk-off homer off Blackburn. The scorers certify that only one other catcher since the leagues began keeping the count in 1951 has paired three runners caught stealing with a walk-off blast in one game: Sammy Taylor, in 1961. "You can't ask for more, honestly," ruled the manager Stammen. The visitors' evening deserves its own line: the piggyback of Max Fried and Will Warren, twice advertised in these pages, ran precisely to specification — Fried five and two-thirds bearing a single run, Warren two and a third without a hit — and was beaten anyway, Ben Rice's six-and-thirtieth homer the only other Pinstripe coin against Walker Buehler and the San Diego relief. The consequence sits atop the National's wild-card table: the Mission nine reclaim the last chair by half a game over Arizona, whose own quiet evening in Houston the card below relates.

HERRERA'S NINTH-INNING THUNDER TURNS SIX-FOUR INTO SEVEN-SIX, AND THE REDBIRDS ARE LEVEL ONCE MORE: CARDINALS 7, ROCKIES 6

Colorado had assembled its lead by the eccentric route the mountain air encourages — Cole Carrigg's two-run inside-the-park homer the jewel of a four-run sixth, Veen's squeeze bunt cashing another, and TJ Rumfield homering in the seventh, the third time in four mornings these pages have caught that man deciding or nearly deciding a game. St. Louis had answered with small coinage of its own, including the rookie Leo Bernal's first two-base hit and Jordan Walker's sacrifice that carried him to precisely one hundred runs driven in, third in the National behind clubmate Burleson's hundred and two. So it stood 6-4 against them in the ninth when Iván Herrera, facing the reliever Romano with two aboard, struck his fifteenth homer to center — three runs, the lead, and the game, O'Brien closing for his six-and-thirtieth save one night after these pages watched him blow one at Chavez Ravine. The Cardinals stand restored to level at one-and-seventy apiece, three and a half from the last chair; the Rockies, twice game and twice beaten this week, are owed better luck than September gives out.

THE APPOINTED DUEL KEEPS ITS APPOINTMENT THIS TIME — SALE OUTLASTS SÁNCHEZ AT THE SUMMIT OF THE EARNED-RUN TABLE: BRAVES 5, PHILLIES 2

Twice this week the desk has advertised a pitching card and once been made a fool for it; Friday in Philadelphia the pastime honored the bill. Chris Sale, second on the National's earned-run table at 2.10, gave six innings of two runs and seven strikeouts; Cristopher Sánchez, third at 2.58 and the league's leader in victories, matched him at six innings and was undone by his defense — a Harper error opening the gates on a three-run Atlanta second, Riley and Acuña singling home the honest portion. Alec Bohm supplied both Philadelphia runs with a double and a homer, and Mauricio Dubón closed the accounts with a two-run blast in the eighth, Iglesias sealing his thirtieth save. The standings turn the screw: Atlanta, winners of eight in ten, stretch the East's lead to five full games over the Phillies with the same clubs meeting at 6:05 this evening, Pérez opposing Wheeler — and the wild-card ledger now reads Chicago's top chair by a half-game over Philadelphia, a race within the race worth a morning eye.

NINE STRIKEOUTS, ONE DOUBLE, AND THE RESTORED PHENOM CROSSES WITH THE ONLY RUN OF THE NIGHT: SMOKY CITY NINE 1, SERAPHS 0

Pittsburgh's evening was economy itself: Jared Jones struck out nine across six innings of three hits, Dotel and Montgomery finished the whitewashing, and the game's entire scoring was transacted in the second, when Konnor Griffin — the twenty-year-old whose return from two months' exile these pages heralded yesterday — reached, ran, and came home on Jake Mangum's double. The restored man thus pays his first dividend within a day of the ward's discharge. The Seraphs, who mustered four hits and struck out twelve times, have now dropped three running and nine of ten — the sale of the franchise, this paper suspects, cannot close quickly enough for the Anaheim faithful — while the Smoky City nine bank a second consecutive victory and hold their September form at seven wins in ten games, with Ashcraft and his fourteen victories taking the hill against the same visitors at 6:40 this evening.

A FIRST-INNING BLAST, EIGHT INNINGS OF SILENCE, AND A FULL-DRESS FRACAS BESIDES: HUB NINE 1, ORIOLES 0

Mickey Gasper opened the evening at Camden Yards with a homer, his eighth, and the pitching declined to add another run all night: Ranger Suárez carried seven scoreless innings of five hits, Whitlock bridged the eighth, and Chapman shut the ninth for his two-and-thirtieth save while Baltimore's Baz bore a hard-luck loss. The theatre came between: after an inning-ending double play in the eighth, Blaze Alexander's slide into second drew stares, the stares drew words, and both benches and bullpens emptied onto the infield grass — warnings issued, nobody ejected, and the antagonists afterward the mildest men in Maryland. "Not a big deal, not a huge thing," ruled Boston's interim manager Tracy; "just two teams competing," agreed Baltimore's Albernaz. The standings are less forgiving than the testimony: the Orioles have lost four running, stand nought-and-four in September with their fifth whitewashing of the year, and have slipped three games from Cleveland's chair — while the Hub nine, twice winners running, keep the wild card's second chair in comfort. The clubs go again at 7:05 this evening, and the desk notes the visitors send Sonny Gray and his sixteen victories, the American's most, against Bassitt.

IMANAGA CARRIES SIX SPARE INNINGS UNDER THE CLOSED ROOF, AND THE FLORIDIANS FADE A LITTLE FURTHER: CUBS 6, FLORIDIANS 1

The Cubs' evening in Miami was a study in sufficiency: Shota Imanaga permitted three hits and a single run across six innings, Civale closed with three frames for a rare long save, and the scoring gathered without a single mighty blow until the ninth — a second-inning force out, then a sixth built of Ian Happ's bases-loaded walk, Nico Hoerner's single, and a sacrifice fly, with Pete Crow-Armstrong trotting home on a wild pitch in the seventh. Michael Busch's eighteenth homer dressed the final line. The center fielder the milestone desk sang all week added his eight-and-twentieth double and three-and-thirtieth stolen base, the forty-homer question still standing at one swing; the Floridians, held to four hits, drop a second straight and slip three and a half games from the National's last chair — the arithmetic that whispered to them a week ago now barely audible.

FIVE BALLS OVER THE FLUSHING FENCES, TWO OF THEM ALVAREZ'S: METS 10, FRISCO NINE 6

New York settled the argument in its first two turns at bat — Mark Vientos's three-run homer in the first, then a four-run second crowned by Francisco Alvarez's first blast and Bo Bichette's two-run drive, seven runs before San Francisco had its bearings — and kept the hammer handy thereafter, Francisco Lindor adding a two-run homer in the sixth and Alvarez his second of the night in the eighth: five over the fences in all. Nolan McLean rode the cushion to his eleventh victory with seven strikeouts. The visitors' account was very nearly all Rafael Devers: two doubles, a three-run homer — his three-and-thirtieth — and five runs driven in, magnificent and unavailing, the Frisco nine dropping a second straight in a season that has long since stopped keeping score of its indignities. The same clubs meet at 4:10 this afternoon, Molina opposing Thornton.

THE MILESTONE DESK'S BUSINESS IS TRANSACTED BY THE FOURTH INNING: PALE HOSE 4, MINNESOTANS 1

The evening's history stands in the news columns above — Vargas in the first, Braden Montgomery in the second, Colson Montgomery's two-run blow in the fourth, three homers doing all the scoring a first-place club required — and the pitching kept the ledger clean: Erick Fedde threw five scoreless innings on the season's most humid night, running his career account against Minnesota to five victories without a defeat and a 1.18 figure. "Maybe they just bring the best out of me," the pitcher allowed. Josh Bell's eighteenth homer in the ninth spoiled the shutout and nothing else. The Pale Hose thus open a six-game homestand by protecting what Cleveland's twin heroics had made precious: the Central's lead holds at two and a half games with one-and-twenty to play, and the Minnesotans, twice beaten running, recede toward the season's margins. The clubs resume at 7:10 this evening, Bradley opposing Kay.

JAVIER PERMITS ONE HIT IN FIVE, AND ARIZONA'S CHAIR IS GONE BY MIDNIGHT: HOUSTON NINE 3, DIAMONDBACKS 1

The evening under the Houston roof was settled in a single frame: Yainer Diaz's eleventh homer opened a three-run third, Isaac Paredes doubled home the second run, and Altuve's sacrifice fly cashed the third. Cristian Javier made it stand — one hit across five innings with eight strikeouts, the one hit being Gabriel Moreno's answering homer in the fourth — and four relievers carried the arrangement home, Josh Hader sealing his three-and-twentieth save. Arizona finished with three hits entire, and the toll was exacted elsewhere before it was counted at home: with San Diego's ten-o'clock heroics, the Diamondbacks surrender the National's last chair by half a game, the very seat this paper has watched change hands four times in a week. Houston, for its part, has won three running and holds the West by three full games over beaten Texas — a lead that stood at a single game on Wednesday's masthead.

FOUR BALLS LEAVE ONE INNING — MUNCY, WILLIAMS, McNEIL, AND GELOF IN PROCESSION — AND THE SPOILERS SPOIL AGAIN: ATHLETICS 7, MARINERS 6

The third inning at Seattle produced a curiosity the wires certify: four Athletic homers in the single frame — solo blasts from Max Muncy and Alika Williams, Jeff McNeil's two-run drive, and Zack Gelof's seventeenth in procession — five runs erasing an early deficit against Logan Gilbert, who struck out ten and was beaten anyway. Cal Raleigh's two-run homer dragged the home nine level at five, whereupon Gelof tripled and scored the tiebreaking runs' first in a two-run fifth, Butler singling him home. Naylor's eighth-inning knock closed the gap to one and no further, Harris sealing his fifteenth save. The Athletics have now won three running in the role September assigns a last-place club with self-respect — spoiler in the yards of the fading — while the Mariners, losers of seven in ten, watch a once-plausible September recede; the clubs meet again at 9:40 this evening.

SNELL THROWS SIX SPOTLESS AND TUCKER REMEMBERS THE FENCES: TROLLEY DODGERS 5, WASHINGTONS 3

The champions' evening ran to the old specifications: Blake Snell carried six scoreless innings of five hits and seven strikeouts, the third inning banked three on Mookie Betts's run-scoring single and Teoscar Hernández's two-run double — that man's bat still warm from Thursday's heroics in these pages — and in the sixth Kyle Tucker struck his twelfth homer, his first baseball over a fence since the eighth of August, a drought of nearly a month ended to the visible relief of Chavez Ravine. Washington's answer came all in the ninth — three runs on James Wood's double and singles around it — close enough to require Dreyer's second save, no closer. The great Ohtani rested a second consecutive night, as the ward relates, and the champions scarcely missed him: at four-and-eighty wins they hold the West by ten full games, and receive the Washingtons again at 9:10 this evening with Glasnow and his 2.79 taking the hill against Cavalli.

DOWN TWO IN THE FIFTH, THE LEAGUE'S BEST ANSWER WITH FIVE — PRATT'S TRIPLE CLEARS THE BASES: MILWAUKEES 10, REDLEGS 7

Cincinnati built its lead with proper thunder — Matt McLain's two-run homer in the second, Eugenio Suárez's three-run blast in the third, six runs on the board against four — and Milwaukee dismantled it with the patient tools that have built the game's best ledger, eight-and-eighty wins now against four-and-fifty defeats. The fifth told the tale: Turang struck by a pitch with the bases full, then Cooper Pratt — twenty years old, playing shortstop for the best team in baseball — clearing them with a triple to right and trotting home himself on a wild pitch, five runs in the frame and a season-high four driven in for the young man. William Contreras doubled home the tenth in the sixth, Brice Turang finished with three runs batted in, and Megill struck out the side in the ninth for his six-and-twentieth save. The Redlegs' consolations were real — Sal Stewart's run-scoring single carried the majors' leading count to one hundred six, the freshman total these pages have sung since August — but the arithmetic holds: Milwaukee leads the Central by eight, and the clubs go again at 6:40 this evening, May opposing Abbott.

First the standings, then tonight's bill. In the American: Tampa Bay's lead in the East is four over New York, with Boston seven and a half astern; the White Sox keep the Central by two and a half over doubly-triumphant Cleveland; Houston holds the West by three over Texas. The wild-card row reads New York and Boston comfortable, and then the morning's crush: Cleveland's last chair at two-and-seventy wins, Toronto — level at last — one game behind, Texas two, and reeling Baltimore three. In the National: Atlanta by five over Philadelphia; Milwaukee, still the game's best ledger, by eight over Chicago; Los Angeles by ten over San Diego — and the last chair returned overnight to the Mission nine by half a game over Arizona, with Miami and St. Louis three and a half back and the top of that row its own race besides, Chicago holding first chair by a half-game over the Phillies. Tonight's card runs to fifteen engagements, all verified against the schedule, and the desk directs the eye to five: at 6:05 in Philadelphia the East's leaders press their advantage, Pérez opposing Wheeler; at 6:10 in Cleveland the walk-off heroes receive Detroit once more, Messick and his 2.46 against Valdez; at 7:05 in Baltimore the American's winningest arm takes the hill, Gray and his sixteen victories opposing Bassitt; at 7:05 in Arlington the Rays and Rangers resume their argument, Rasmussen against deGrom; and at 7:15 in San Diego the Pinstripes seek their remedy for the catcher's midnight thunder, Rodón opposing Ray. The night owls are commended to Seattle at 9:40, where the spoilers of the third inning try for four straight. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
