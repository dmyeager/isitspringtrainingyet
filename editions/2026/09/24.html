Volume I · No. 78 · Thursday, the Four-and-Twentieth of September, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 16 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

FOUR-AND-FORTY PITCHES BUY HOUSTON NO PEACE — HADER WALKS THE BASES FULL IN THE TENTH, NAYLOR EMPTIES THEM, AND THE WEST'S DEAD HEAT SURVIVES THE NIGHT: MARINERS 6, ASTROS 5, IN TEN INNINGS

Houston builds a four-run lead by the third and watches Seattle claw it level; Diaz's tenth-inning triple restores the advantage and the rested Hader is sent back out to keep it; whereupon three walks in four batsmen — Rodríguez forcing home the tie with the bases loaded — and Naylor's line single undo the whole evening's work; Texas beaten at home besides, the throne stays shared at eight-and-seventy wins apiece with four to play, the tiebreaker still in Houston's keeping

Josh Hader had already spent nineteen pitches on the ninth inning when the manager Espada sent him back out for the tenth, and no one in the American's maddest division would call the decision timid: Texas had already lost at Arlington, and one Houston victory would have seized sole command of a West that has refused all season to be commanded. "We used our entire arsenal there trying to get through that one," Espada said afterward, which was the plain truth — and the arsenal was not enough.

For three innings the evening had gone entirely to Houston's design. Altuve's single — a ball that struck an umpire on its way through the infield — cashed the first run, Paredes lifted a sacrifice fly, and Yainer Diaz drove his fourteenth home run to left with Alvarez aboard: four to nothing before Seattle had drawn a breath. The mariners of the Sound answered in the home third with Canzone's two-run knock and Rodríguez's run-scoring double, squared the account at four on Canzone's single in the fifth, and then the two nines played six scoreless innings of the tautest September baseball while the scoreboard watchers in two time zones kept their sums. In the tenth Diaz struck again — a triple, his first three-bagger of the season, sent to the right-field line to fetch home Alvarez — and Houston stood three outs from the division's sole lead.

Whereupon the tenth's bottom half arrived, and Hader — five days rested, his sinker running to 96 — could not find the plate. Three of the four men he faced walked, Julio Rodríguez taking the last of them with the bases loaded to force the tie, and Josh Naylor lined the next offering to center to end it. Four-and-forty pitches the closer threw in all, tied for the fifth-heaviest night of his career and his heaviest since 2019. "It sucks that we lost this one and how we did," Hader owned, "but we've still got the tiebreaker and that's fortunate for us — we can't bank on that. We've got to win out." So the dead heat this paper entered Wednesday survives its first test intact: Houston and Texas level at eight-and-seventy wins, four to play — the Astros' four all at the ninety-five-times-beaten Athletics, the Rangers closing with the Mets this afternoon before three at Minnesota — and the Silver Boot tiebreaker still says the West is Houston's to win if Houston simply refuses to lose. Wednesday it did not refuse; it merely ran out of strikes.

News Around the League

THE OCTOBER DESK: THE WEST'S DEAD HEAT HOLDS, TORONTO'S CANDLE GOES OUT AT LAST — AND THE PALE HOSE AND THE FRIARS EACH STAND ONE RESULT FROM A BERTH

The American's West survived the wildest night of its dead heat unchanged — Houston beaten in ten at Seattle as the account above relates, Texas beaten at home by the eliminated Mets — so both clubs stand at eight-and-seventy wins with four to play and Houston's tiebreaker still governing. And the same day closed one ledger for good: Toronto, champion of the American League a year ago, was formally eliminated when the first game of the Baltimore twin bill went against it, the fall from pennant to autumn silence complete in a single calendar's turn.

The two nearest berths may be clinched this very day. The White Sox, beaten five-to-four at Kansas City on Garcia's seventh-inning double, need one victory or one Texas defeat to seat themselves in October for the first time since 2021 — the front office traveled to Kansas City Wednesday for what might have been the festivities, and got a lesson in presumption for its trouble. Their larger prize stays live besides: Cleveland's shutout defeat at Boston keeps the Central's gap at one game, and Chicago holds the season-series tiebreaker, seven games to six. In the National, San Diego's victory at Los Angeles put the Friars one step from the postseason — one win, or one Arizona defeat at Denver this afternoon, clinches — while the Cubs and Phillies both lost and sit knotted at seven-and-eighty wins for the second wild-card chair, Chicago holding the tiebreaker over Philadelphia and San Diego alike. Arizona, alive by its own hand, stands three back of the third chair with four to play.

One almanac note with October consequence: foul weather forecast for Boston has moved the Cubs' Saturday engagement forward, so Chicago now plays a separate-admission twin bill at Fenway Park on Friday — a scheduling hand the manager Counsell reckons was dealt against him, with his club still chasing its clinch. "It always kind of seems like the baseball gods are going to test you," said his pitcher Gausman, "and it always seems to come at a time when you don't want it to."

THE MILESTONE DESK: FOUR MEN NOW SHARE THE AMERICAN'S HOME-RUN CROWN AT ONE-AND-FORTY — AND SALE BANKS A NINTH SEASON OF TWO HUNDRED STRIKEOUTS

The American's home-run race, which began the week as one man's story, ended Wednesday as a four-part harmony. Pete Alonso — whose fortieth this paper sang on Tuesday to the strains of Earth, Wind & Fire — struck his one-and-fortieth in the twin bill's opener at Camden Yards, and by nightfall Ben Rice of the Yankees had hit two in a single game, his fortieth and one-and-fortieth, to draw level with Alonso, with Houston's Alvarez, and with Tampa Bay's Caminero. Four men, one number, four days remaining. Rice's pair carried its own footnote: the manager Boone had prophesied him thirty-nine home runs in the spring, and Rice took the figure as an insult to be avenged. "I was pretty close, but he went soaring past," Boone allowed. The young man is the thirteenth Yankee ever to reach forty, and only Judge and DiMaggio managed it within their first three seasons in that uniform. In the National the arithmetic holds one rung higher: Crow-Armstrong and Schwarber share the lead at five-and-forty, with Atlanta's Olson joining the one-and-forty club Wednesday by a blow of his own.

At Truist Park, Chris Sale struck out ten Cincinnati batsmen in his final tuning before October and, with the last of them, banked his two-hundredth strikeout of the year — the ninth season of two hundred in his career, a company few living arms keep. He had ended the fifth inning at 199 and asked to go back out. "I told myself before this game that I really wanted to make it feel like a playoff game," said the southpaw, who takes the ball for the first game of the Wild Card round Tuesday. The evening asked more of him than strikeouts, as the colors desk below records.

THE FAREWELL DESK: APPEARANCE FIVE HUNDRED, AND AN ANNOUNCEMENT WITHIN DAYS — SCHERZER POINTS THE FINGER AT HIMSELF AND ASKS FOR THE BALL ONE MORE SUNDAY

Max Scherzer took the mound at Baltimore Wednesday for the five-hundredth appearance of his career — the four-hundred-ninety-first of them a start — and left it beaten, four runs in five innings, undone chiefly by Alonso's milestone blow. Afterward the two-and-forty-year-old said plainly that he will likely have something to say about his future within a day or two, and that he hopes to return on short rest for Sunday's season finale. The self-accounting he offered meanwhile was the old competitor's, unsoftened: "Instead of pointing the finger at everybody else, I'll point the finger at myself and what I've done this year. I got beat on one pitch here. I feel like I've said that a lot, but that's the big leagues."

The week thus carries two farewells in close formation: Verlander's final start comes Saturday at Detroit, as this paper recorded Wednesday, and Scherzer's reckoning — whatever he announces — follows hard upon. Two of the age's great right arms, whose duels ornamented a decade of Octobers, reaching the same page of the calendar together; the Herald will keep both accounts with care.

THE HERALD LOWERS ITS COLORS: DON COOPER, WHO TAUGHT THE SOUTH SIDE ITS PITCHING FOR THREE-AND-THIRTY YEARS, IS DEAD AT SEVENTY

Word came Wednesday, confirmed by his family, that Don Cooper — Coop to every man who ever threw a baseball in his sight — has died at seventy. He served the White Sox organization three-and-thirty seasons from 1988 through 2020, eighteen of them as the big club's pitching coach, and his monument is the 2005 championship staff that threw four consecutive complete games in the League Championship Series before sweeping the World Series — a feat of preparation as much as of arms. The testimony poured in from his fraternity: Contreras, who said losing him felt like losing family; Konerko, who remembered a man who "could talk somebody up into thinking they were good"; and Sale, who learned the news moments before his warmups Wednesday and went out and struck out ten. "I wouldn't be here without him," said the southpaw, whose first big-league pitching coach Cooper was.

That his passing came on a night his old organization stood a single result from its first October in five years is the kind of arithmetic Coop would have waved off with something fast-talking and unprintable. The Herald lowers its colors for a teacher of the craft: the game is subtracted by his going, and every pitcher he ever talked into believing himself good is the proof of what it gained by his staying so long.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: JUDGE VOWS THE BRONX WILL NOT OPEN OCTOBER WITHOUT HIM — RAFAELA'S RETURN LASTS SEVEN OUTS, AND GAUSMAN'S SHOULDER JOINS THE WATCH

Aaron Judge spoke Wednesday like a man arguing with a calendar. The captain, shelved Saturday with what his manager called a moderate strain of the right calf — the soleus, Judge specified, a muscle "a little more crucial with running and hitting" — received a plasma injection Monday and intends to be ready for the first game of the Wild Card round Tuesday in the Bronx. "The Bronx is a magical place in October," he said. "I don't want to miss out on that opportunity." Boone, after the nine-run evening entered on the card below, would rule nothing out: "We're going to listen to the body and see how he heals. If he's in a position to play, obviously we're going to want him."

At Boston the ward giveth and taketh in the same evening: Ceddanne Rafaela, back from three weeks on the shelf, opened the game with a diving, extra-base-robbing catch and lined a single in the second — then came up lame down the line, right hamstring tight, and was gone before the third. The pictures are ordered; the man himself professed little alarm. At Wrigley Field, Kevin Gausman left a fine outing with trouble in the left shoulder that his club prays does not linger, a subtraction the wild-card arithmetic can ill afford. And at Kansas City, Erick Fedde took a 104-mile-an-hour drive off the right calf in the second inning, pitched on, and limped to his locker afterward with clean X-rays — the White Sox will call it a bruise and a bargain.

THE CONVALESCENT DESK: THE VIGIL CLOSES WITH A LINE SINGLE TO RIGHT — OHTANI RETURNS TO THE CHAMPIONS' ORDER, AND CHAPMAN COMES BACK THROWING LIGHTNING

The watch this paper has kept over Shohei Ohtani — the named day, the manager's promise, the batting-order deliberations — closed Wednesday night at Chavez Ravine precisely as promised. Activated from his fifteen-day shelf-leave, batting second for the first time all year, he lined the evening's first plate appearance into right field for a single, walked in the third, and finished one-for-four in a defeat the card below records. The verdict wanted was health, not heroics, and health is what the eye reported: "There wasn't any chase," said the manager Roberts. "When you feel healthy and strong, you feel like you can just see the baseball, trust your eyes and take your swing. I thought that's what you saw tonight." The operations chief Friedman wants only at-bats now — "as many as we can, some rhythm and timing" — with five games to sharpen the sword before October.

At Fenway the convalescence ran ahead of schedule: Aroldis Chapman, silent since the thirteenth with a balky lat, returned to strike out the side — all three swinging, twelve pitches — for the four-hundred-second save of his career and five-and-thirtieth of the year. "I feel normal. I feel like I'm at a hundred percent right now," said the eight-and-thirty-year-old, and the Guardians who faced him offered no rebuttal.

THE CURIOSITIES DESK: A TWIN BILL UMPIRED BY TWINS — THE BROTHERS JONES MAKE HISTORY AT CAMDEN YARDS, AND A PITCHER IN AMARILLO CELEBRATES A PENNANT UPSIDE DOWN

The doubleheader at Baltimore carried a wonder in plain sight: Austin and Tyler Jones, identical twins of Charleston, became the first such pair ever to serve on the same umpiring crew in a Major League game — Austin at first base and Tyler at second for the opener, the two swapping stations for the nightcap. Umpires together since the age of fifteen, schooled together at eighteen, they had crossed paths professionally almost never in a dozen years of minor-league roads. "This is something we've dreamed about forever," said Tyler. That the twin bill they worked produced twin scores — Baltimore four, Toronto two, twice over, as the card below attests — is the sort of coincidence this paper declines to explain and simply enters in the book.

And in the Texas League, the Diamondbacks' young right-hander Alfred Morillo closed Double-A Amarillo's championship victory Wednesday with a 97-mile-an-hour strikeout — and then, as he did upon clinching the finals berth last week, turned a full standing backflip on the mound and stuck the landing. The parent club's manager Lovullo, shown the earlier rendition, reviewed it as an aesthete and a worrier at once: "That's pretty impressive. Yeah, that's hard to do."

The Rest of the Card

FOUR THUNDERCLAPS AND A GRAND SLAM FROM McMAHON — RICE SOARS PAST HIS MANAGER'S PROPHECY TO A SHARE OF THE CROWN: BOMBERS 9, GULF COAST CLUB 2

The Bombers made short work of the newly crowned division champions: Rice's fortieth home run in the first, McMahon's grand slam in a five-run third, the youngster Lombard Jr.'s sixth, and Rice again in the fourth for the one-and-fortieth that ties the American's home-run lead four ways, as the milestone desk relates. Cole took the decision to ten victories, and Tampa Bay's answer — DeLuca's three hits and two thefts, Mullins' answering blow — never threatened the account. Tonight the same two nines send Seymour against Schlittler and his league-best 1.94 earned-run figure.

ALONSO'S ONE-AND-FORTIETH SETTLES THE MATINEE AND TORONTO'S CANDLE GOES OUT — SCHERZER'S FIVE-HUNDREDTH APPEARANCE ENDS IN DEFEAT: BIRDS 4, MAPLE LEAF NINE 2

The opener of the Camden Yards twin bill turned on one swing: with Baltimore ahead two, Pete Alonso met a Scherzer offering in the fifth and sent it to left center with Beavers aboard — the one-and-fortieth, and the blow that formally snuffed Toronto's October candle, guttering since this paper first marked it Monday. Bassitt took the decision to nine wins, Kittredge sealed the save, and the two runs Toronto scratched back in the sixth, gifted on a throwing error, fell short by the very margin of Alonso's stroke. Scherzer's own reckoning, and the farewell it may portend, stands at the desk above; the elimination closes Toronto's ledger one year after a pennant.

THE NIGHTCAP COPIES THE MATINEE TO THE VERY DIGIT — TREY GIBSON THROWS SIX AND TWO-THIRDS SPOTLESS AND THE SWEEP IS TWINS ALL THE WAY DOWN: BALTIMORES 4, BLUE JAYS 2

The second game ended precisely as the first — four to two, Baltimore — and the symmetry extended to the men in blue, as the curiosities desk records. Trey Gibson supplied the substance: six and two-thirds scoreless innings with seven strikeouts, while Encarnacion-Strand drove home three — a two-run single in the first and his eleventh home run in the fourth — and Cowser doubled home the other. Toronto's whole evening was Schneider's two-run blow in the ninth. The Birds ride three straight victories and, at eight-and-seventy wins, keep a mathematician's breath of wild-card life at three games and a half from Chicago's chair with three to play.

TWELVE HITS BUY ONE RUN, AND ONE RUN IS RICHES — GRAY'S EIGHTEENTH AND CHAPMAN'S FOUR-HUNDRED-SECOND CLOSE THE WHITEWASH: FENWAY MEN 1, LAKE ERIE MEN 0

Sonny Gray closed his regular season with six scoreless innings on a mere seven-and-seventy pitches — the league lead in victories now his at eighteen against five — and Chapman, back from the shelf, struck out the side in the ninth for the save, as the convalescent desk sings. Boston stacked twelve hits and cashed exactly one, Monasterio's sixth-inning single fetching Duran home, and one sufficed; Griffin, beaten despite a fine account, falls to fifteen-and-six. Cleveland's six-game winning streak dies at Fenway, though the Central's lead survives the night at one, Chicago having stumbled in its turn.

THE PARTY WAITS IN THE LUGGAGE — GARCIA'S SEVENTH-INNING DOUBLE HOLDS OFF THE PALE HOSE BY ONE: KANSAS CITY NINE 5, PALE HOSE 4

Kansas City struck four in the first off the opener Hudson, Chicago clawed all four back in the third — Teel's two-run single and Murakami's double the heart of it — and there the matter stood until Maikel Garcia doubled home Witt with two out in the seventh. Antonacci's deep fly died at the center-field track to end it. The defeat postpones a clinch the White Sox front office had traveled to witness: one victory, or one Texas defeat, seats the South Side in October for the first time since 2021, and the Central remains but a single game distant with the tiebreaker in Chicago's keeping — the arithmetic is at the October desk, and Fedde's bruised calf at the ward.

SALE STRIKES OUT TEN WITH A HEAVY HEART AND DUBÓN SETTLES IT IN THE TENTH: TOMAHAWK NINE 3, QUEEN CITY NINE 2

An evening that began with hard news — Sale learning of his old teacher Cooper's death moments before his warmups, as the colors desk mourns — ended in a walk-off: Mauricio Dubón singled home Keirsey Jr. in the tenth to give the crowned champions of the East their victory. Between the two, Sale struck out ten across five and two-thirds for his two-hundredth of the year, Olson squared the game with his one-and-fortieth home run, and Riley's eighteenth answered Cincinnati's seventh-inning lead. Abbott's seven strong innings for the Queen City nine deserved better company; Suter took the decision.

TURANG AND YELICH STRIKE IN THE OPENING FRAMES AND VICTORY NINE-AND-NINETY IS BANKED — THE HUNDREDTH WAITS ON TONIGHT: SUDS MEN 4, PHILADELPHIAS 1

Turang led off the game with his twentieth home run, Yelich added his twelfth an inning later, and Contreras' two-run single in the third completed all the scoring Milwaukee required; Henderson took the decision to eleven wins against three, and Megill sealed his thirtieth save. Nola struck out eight in defeat, Realmuto's groundout cashing Philadelphia's lone run. The Suds men stand at nine-and-ninety victories — one more, and they enter the game's hundred-win registry for the first time since the 2016 Cubs took the Central — with the chance at it this evening in the same park.

FIVE UNANSWERED AFTER LOWE'S EARLY THUNDER — THE MOUND CITY NINE END PITTSBURGH'S STREAK AT FIVE: MOUND CITY NINE 5, BUCCANEERS 1

Lowe's four-and-thirtieth home run gave Pittsburgh the second inning, and nothing thereafter belonged to the home nine: Burleson's single squared it — his league-leading hundred-and-twelfth run driven in — Bernal singled home two more, and Winn and Wetherholt stitched the fourth and fifth runs nose to tail. Liberatore took the decision, and Eisert the loss. The Buccaneers' five-game run, which had made September ask permission of no one, closes at eighty wins; St. Louis plays out its string with the spoiler's relish, and the same two nines meet again at midday.

PERFECT THROUGH FOUR AND SPOILERS TO THE LAST — McLEAN'S SEVEN INNINGS AND BATY'S NINTH-INNING SLAM SINK ARLINGTON DEEPER: FLUSHING NINE 7, ARLINGTON MEN 2

Nolan McLean retired the first twelve Texas batsmen in order and finished seven innings of two runs for his twelfth victory, while Soto's seven-and-twentieth home run built the early lead; when Texas squared it at two in the fifth, Semien answered with a blow in the eighth and Brett Baty broke the thing open with a grand slam in the ninth. The eliminated Flushing nine thus play executioner in a race not their own: the defeat is Arlington's third straight and drops the first home series since July, the West's arithmetic — must-win now, by the first baseman Burger's own accounting — entered at the October desk.

FOUR UNEARNED IN THE FIRST KEEP THE EXECUTIONER WAITING — THE SONORAN NINE LIVE TO FIGHT ANOTHER DAY: SONORAN NINE 5, COLORADANS 3

An error in left by Veen opened the gate in the first, and Arizona marched four unearned runs through it — Moreno and Arenado singling runs home, Carroll tripling home two more — before Tawa's eleventh home run supplied the fifth in the eighth. Kelly took the decision and Loáisiga the save against Colorado's scattered answers. The victory means the Sonoran nine cannot yet be eliminated, and the day's losses in Chicago and Philadelphia pulled them to three games from the last wild-card chair with four to play; this afternoon's finale at Denver doubles as San Diego's clinch-watch, as the October desk explains.

THE FISH KEEP WRIGLEY'S CHAMPAGNE ON ICE YET ANOTHER NIGHT — AN OVERTURNED CALL AT SECOND CASHES THE WINNER: FISH 3, CUBS 2

Stowers' one-and-twentieth home run — the lone blemish on Gausman's evening before the shoulder trouble entered at the ward — had Miami ahead until Swanson's two-run single squared it in the sixth; whereupon the seventh turned on the moving pictures, a challenged force play at second overturned to let Ramírez score the go-ahead run on Mack's grounder. Alcantara sealed it. The Cubs are two-for-six-and-twenty with runners in scoring position across the two defeats, the win-and-in box now unchecked a second consecutive night, and the wild-card knot at the October desk grows no gentler for the waiting.

WALTON'S THREE-RUN BLOW SETTLES MATTERS EARLY AND THE WHITE ELEPHANTS TRAMPLE ON — TROUT'S TWO-AND-TWENTIETH IS ANSWER ENOUGH FOR NOBODY: WHITE ELEPHANTS 7, ANAHEIM NINE 3

Five runs inside two innings — Butler's run-scoring knock, Walton's three-run homer, Bolte's double — decided the West Sacramento evening before it fairly began, and Langeliers' two-run double in the sixth answered Anaheim's middle-innings push. Trout's two-and-twentieth home run and Grissom's thirteenth made it briefly respectable; Hartlieb took the decision, and Ureña the defeat. The Athletics, at three-and-sixty wins, now host Houston for four with the West's throne in the balance — the spoiler's chair is theirs if they care to sit in it.

A SCORELESS KNOT CUT IN THE SIXTH BY ABRAMS AND MORALES — THE WASHINGTONS TAKE THE SERIES FROM THE MOTOR CITY NINE: WASHINGTONS 4, TIGERS 2

Five and a half innings of nothing gave way all at once: Abrams lined home the first run and Yohandy Morales followed with a two-run homer, his fourth, before Wood's groundout cashed a fourth run in the seventh. Malgeri — the No. 53 whose deeds christened the newly renamed park in Monday's account — answered with a home run and a late run-scoring single, but Dion and the Washington relief corps held, Cruz sealing the save. Detroit, at four-and-seventy wins, turns its eyes wholly to Saturday and Verlander's farewell; the visiting Pirates arrive Friday.

KEASCHALL'S BAT BOOKENDS THE AFTERNOON AND GÓMEZ HOLDS THE MIDDLE — THE NORTH STARS TAKE THE RUBBER MATCH BY THE BAY: NORTH STARS 4, JINTS 2

The young Keaschall opened the scoring with his tenth home run in the third and squared the game with a sacrifice fly in the fifth after Whitcomb's two-run single had given San Francisco the lead; Jenkins and Bell singled home the deciding runs in the seventh, nose to tail. Gómez took the decision and Morris his fifth save. Minnesota thus claims the series two games to one and heads home to receive Texas for the season's final three — an engagement the West's dead heat has made consequential far beyond the Twin Cities' own concluded ambitions.

MACHADO GREETS THE GREAT RETURN WITH THUNDER OF HIS OWN — THE FRIARS STAND ONE STEP FROM OCTOBER: FRIARS 5, DODGERS 1

On the night Ohtani rejoined the champions' order, as the convalescent desk relates, the visitors wrote the larger headline: Manny Machado's nine-and-twentieth home run put San Diego ahead in the fourth, his sacrifice fly and a Yamamoto throwing error built the fifth inning's two, and Fermin singled home a fifth in the ninth. Rodriguez took the decision over Yamamoto, whose fourteen victories could not survive the evening's bookkeeping. The Friars, at eight-and-eighty wins, hold the wild-card row's head chair by one and may clinch a berth before tonight's first pitch — by Arizona's defeat this afternoon, or failing that by their own hand against these same Dodgers.

The Thursday tables, then — sixteen contests entered, and the racing lines redrawn by inches. In the American: Tampa Bay is crowned at six-and-ninety wins with the first seed; New York hosts the Wild Card round from Tuesday the nine-and-twentieth, Judge's calf willing; the Central is Cleveland's by one over Chicago with the season-series tiebreaker in the Pale Hose's keeping, and the White Sox clinch a berth with one victory or one Texas defeat; the West remains a dead heat at eight-and-seventy wins, Houston holding the Silver Boot tiebreaker with its final four at the Athletics while Texas closes against the Mets today and three at Minnesota; Toronto is eliminated, and Baltimore's thread is three games and a half with three to play. In the National: the three crowns are sewn — Atlanta, Milwaukee at nine-and-ninety wins with the bye and the hundredth win one victory away, and Los Angeles — while the wild-card row reads San Diego a game clear at the head, the Cubs and Phillies knotted at seven-and-eighty with Chicago holding both tiebreakers, and Arizona three back of the last chair; the Friars clinch with a win tonight or an Arizona defeat this afternoon. Today's bill of twelve, verified against the schedule: St. Louis at Pittsburgh at 12:35, Skenes taking the ball; the White Sox at Kansas City at 2:10 with the clinch in the air; Miami at Wrigley at 2:20, the Cubs' last home hand; the Mets at Texas at 2:35, must-win for Arlington; Arizona at Denver at 3:10, Rodriguez and his fifteen wins against the Rockies; Milwaukee at Philadelphia at 6:05 for the hundredth-win try; Cleveland at Boston at 6:45; Tampa Bay at New York at 7:05, Schlittler and his league-best 1.94 taking the ball; Cincinnati at Atlanta at 7:15; and at night's end Houston at the Athletics and the Angels at Seattle at 9:40, with San Diego at Los Angeles at 10:10. The season's last page falls Sunday the seven-and-twentieth; the Wild Card rounds open Tuesday the nine-and-twentieth. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
