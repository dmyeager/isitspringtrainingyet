The Game of the Day

THE ADVERTISED DUEL OF ACES YIELDS FIVE-AND-TWENTY RUNS, AND THE LAST ONE SCORES ON A THROW GONE ASTRAY: CORSAIRS 13, GIANTS 12

Billed in these pages as the month's handsomest pitching card, Webb against Skenes becomes instead a carnival of five-and-thirty hits: Webb is excused after two innings bearing nine runs, Pittsburgh scores nine in the third without an extra-base blow among them, San Francisco claws all the way level with five in the eighth — and in the ninth Brandon Lowe's fourth hit begets the winning run on a third baseman's throw pulled wide of first

The desk begins with a confession of its own part in the evening's comedy. Yesterday's number directed the reader to Pittsburgh for as handsome a pitching card as September would offer — Logan Webb opposing Paul Skenes, an engagement for the connoisseurs — and the pastime, which reads this paper's appointments chiefly for the pleasure of mocking them, returned instead five-and-twenty runs and five-and-thirty hits. Webb was excused after two innings bearing nine hits and nine runs; Skenes did not survive the fifth, charged with five. The Giants had struck first and heavily, a five-run second built on singles by Koss and Hill and crowned by Rafael Devers's three-run blast, his one-and-thirtieth — whereupon the home third answered with nine runs assembled entirely of small coinage: singles by Oneil Cruz (twice in the frame), Davis, Horwitz, and their fellows, walks to Gonzalez and Triolo with the bases full, and not one extra-base blow in the lot. "I don't think I've seen that many singles and walks," marveled Brandon Lowe, "and to be able to put that kind of production up on the board without some sort of extra-base hit."

An eleven-to-five lead by the middle innings looked like safe harbor, and was nothing of the sort. Lowe's nine-and-twentieth homer stretched the account to twelve in the seventh, but the eighth undid nearly all of it: the reliever Sisk walked three of the four men he faced, Weaver arrived to no better fortune, and San Francisco's five-run reply ran Basabe's bases-loaded walk into Drew Cavanaugh's two-run double and the pinch-hitter Furman's two-run single — twelve runs apiece, the evening's ledger wiped clean. The accounting to that point was general on both sides: Cruz four hits and four driven in, Turner Hill four hits of his own, Devers three runs batted in, nineteen Pittsburgh hits against sixteen for the visitors.

The ninth supplied the ending such an evening deserved. Lowe led off with his fourth hit, took third on the traffic that followed, and with two out Rafael Flores Jr. bounced a grounder to Basabe — shifted by then from second base across to third — whose throw sailed past Devers at first as Lowe came home with the thirteenth run. "Eventful," ruled the manager Kelly, in the evening's finest economy of language, before crediting the third inning's democracy: "Everybody contributed in that inning to help us score nine runs." Flores took the wider view: "It's kind of what you live for. You want to play games like that." The win fell to the reliever Montgomery, the loss to Seymour, and the Corsairs have now won three running — eight-and-sixty victories against one-and-seventy defeats, five games from the National's last wild-card chair and refusing, as yet, to be shown the door. The clubs go again this evening at 6:40, Roupp opposing Jones, and the desk makes no promises whatever about the pitching.

News Around the League

THE OWNERSHIP DESK: THE ANGELS CHANGE HANDS — KROENKE OF THE RAMS AGREES TO BUY THE FRANCHISE, AND THE MORENO ERA CLOSES AT THREE-AND-TWENTY YEARS

The morning's heaviest news arrived not from any diamond but from the counting-house: Stan Kroenke, proprietor of the Los Angeles Rams and of the sprawling Kroenke Sports & Entertainment concern, has reached an agreement to purchase the Angels from Arte Moreno and the Moreno family, the transaction expected to close in the first quarter of 2027 pending the approval of the league's owners. The price was not disclosed — the gentlemen of the purse rarely volunteer such figures, though the Herald observes that no man sells a ball club these days save at a fortune of the first order. Moreno, who bought the club in 2003 and has held it three-and-twenty years, will retain a minority stake; his stewardship brought six division titles in its first dozen seasons and not one winning campaign since 2015, a ledger the faithful of Anaheim know by heart.

The testimonials were duly exchanged. "The Angels are a storied franchise anchored in a great market," said Kroenke. "We look forward to an exciting future." Moreno pronounced KSE "the best next owner." And Mike Trout, who has spent his entire glorious career in the uniform and deserves an October more than any man living, offered the endorsement that matters most in the clubhouse: "Kroenke's got a track record of success and winning championships with his teams. I'm super excited." The club marked the occasion in the evening by losing to New York, as the card below relates — the sale closing on a nine of three-and-fifty wins against six-and-eighty defeats, with everywhere to go but down.

THE DEBUT DESK: TWO FIRST-NIGHT HOMERS IN A SINGLE EVENING CARRY THE FRESHMAN CLASS TO A RECORD FIFTEEN — MONTES THUNDERS AT FENWAY, MORALES IN THE CAPITAL

This desk, which attended Walker Jenkins daily through his first week, now finds the whole freshman class crowding its columns. Tuesday evening produced two first-game homers in two different cities, and with them a record: fifteen men have now homered in their major-league debuts this season, no year since 1900 having produced more than the thirteen of 2022. At Fenway Park, Lazaro Montes — the Mariners' summons from the September expansion, owner of eight-and-thirty minor-league homers this summer, the second-most in all the bushes — walked in his first appearance, singled home a run in his third inning, and in the fifth deposited a low slider 407 feet to center field. "I'm trying to be me, be myself," said the one-and-twenty-year-old. "I give it my 100 percent." His manager Wilson testified that "certainly that energy has been a big lift" — the fuller destruction his club visited upon Boston stands in the card below. In Washington the selfsame night, Yohandy Morales struck his own first-game homer in the third inning of the Senators' rout of Atlanta, the record-breaking fifteenth of the class.

Nor was the night's debuting done: in Los Angeles the Cardinals' Leo Bernal, their seventh-ranked prospect, announced himself with a bases-loaded, two-run single at 107.6 miles to the hour by the trackers' certificate — "Being in this clubhouse with these people, against the team that we faced... was special and the hard work paid off," said the young man, whose fuller evening the card below sings. The desk notes for the almanac that the class of 2026 has made a habit of arriving loudly: Wetherholt, Benge, Murakami, and DeLauter all homered on Opening Day itself, and the Cardinal Báez struck three in his August debut.

THE LAUREL DESK: THE SEPTEMBER CANVASS FINDS McGONIGLE AND STEWART PULLING CLEAR IN THE ROOKIE RACES

The wire desks' September polling of the Rookie of the Year question finds both races acquiring the shape of verdicts. In the American, Detroit's Kevin McGonigle commands 186 points and thirty first-place ballots of the electors, his 6.1 wins above replacement leading every rookie in either league, his .375 on-base figure and 140 hits leading the American's freshmen. Cleveland's left-hander Parker Messick (132 points) and the White Sox slugger Munetaka Murakami (124, with his nine-and-twenty homers) divide the remaining first-place sentiment. In the National, the case this paper weighed on Sunday has only hardened: Cincinnati's Sal Stewart, at one-and-thirty homers and one hundred five runs batted in — the latter figure leading all baseball, not merely the rookies — polls 173 points and four-and-twenty firsts, and the historical brief is unanswerable: every freshman to pair thirty homers with a hundred runs batted in since the award began in 1947 has taken the trophy home. The Cardinal JJ Wetherholt stands second at 140 points, though his candidacy campaigns from the invalid list, whither his wrist dispatched him on Sunday as this ward recorded; the Met pitcher Nolan McLean is third. The Herald, which does not vote, ventures only that the freshmen have rarely given the electors so little suspense and so much pleasure.

THE VESTIBULE DESK: SALAS ARRIVES IN CINCINNATI AFTER THE FIRST PITCH, AND WAITS ONE DAY MORE FOR HIS FIRST

The summons this paper's dealings desk recorded yesterday came due — after a fashion. Ethan Salas, the Padres' anointed catcher of the future, received the call of his life Monday night ("It's a cool phone call, one you dream about every single day as a kid"), then spent his first day as a big-leaguer marooned at the El Paso airport, arriving at the Cincinnati grounds only after the first pitch had been thrown. He changed into flannels bearing the No. 8, emerged into the dugout in the third inning, and worked his way down the railing shaking hands. His manager Stammen, with a one-run game and a pennant race in his charge, declined to spend the debut so cheaply — though he allowed that had the game reached extra innings, the twenty-year-old was his next man off the bench. The club envisions him for now as a third catcher and a useful left-handed bat; when he does first don the tools of ignorance in a game, he will be the youngest man to catch in the majors since Iván Rodríguez in 1991, as yesterday's number observed. The debutant himself kept the long view: "The end goal was never to make it here. It's obviously to stay." His new club's evening went less serenely — the card below has the particulars — and the vestibule may open as soon as this afternoon's 12:40 rubber match.

THE RESTORATION DESK: THE CHAMPIONS RECEIVE THREE MEN BACK IN ONE AFTERNOON — AND IN CINCINNATI, DE LA CRUZ ANSWERS THE WARD'S ALARM AT A RUN

The appointment stood and was kept: the Dodgers on Tuesday activated their catcher Will Smith from three months' exile with an inflamed disc of the neck, precisely as this paper's restoration desk appointed on Monday, and returned the reserve catcher Dalton Rushing and the pitcher Justin Wrobleski from the invalid rolls in the same stroke — three men in an afternoon, the September reinforcement arriving on schedule even if, as the card below relates, the evening's baseball declined to cooperate. Smith, for his part, doubled in his first game back, which is more than most men manage after a season's middle spent in a neck brace. And in Cincinnati the ward retires an alarm it sounded Sunday with foreboding: Elly De La Cruz, whose left quadriceps lifted him from that afternoon's game and sent this desk to its worry-drawer, was back at shortstop Tuesday and crossed the plate with the go-ahead run in the eighth inning of the Reds' 4-3 victory. The quadriceps, it appears, has been persuaded; the Herald's worry-drawer closes gladly on both cities.

The Rest of the Card

THE GAS HOUSE NINE STRIKE FIVE HOMERS AND A NUMBER NO CARDINAL CLUB EVER POSTED AT CHAVEZ RAVINE: CARDINALS 13, DODGERS 8

On the very evening the champions restored three men to the ranks, St. Louis spoiled the housewarming with the heaviest night any Cardinal club has ever spent at Dodger Stadium — thirteen runs, a figure never before reached in 337 games on those grounds. The blows came five over the fence: Ramón Urías and Thomas Saggese in succession in the second, Jordan Walker's two-run drive in the fourth — 409 feet at 109.3 miles to the hour, and a curiosity for the cabinet: his five-and-fiftieth career homer struck off his five-and-fiftieth different pitcher, the second-longest such run since 1900 behind Adames's seven-and-fifty — Saggese again in the fifth, and the young Joshua Báez in the seventh. Saggese's pair, struck hard by his boyhood California home with his father in the stands, made the first two-homer night of his major-league life and doubled his career count to four in a single evening. The rookie Bernal's 107.6-mile debut single crowned a five-run third, as the debut desk relates; McGreevy pitched to the victory. The champions' account was chiefly Mookie Betts, two doubles and four driven in, with Smith's return-day double noted in the restoration desk above. The Boys in Blue thus surrender a half-game of their bye cushion nowhere — idle Atlanta lost too, as the card below records — but the West's lead over San Diego is nine and a half, and the rematch comes at 10:10 this evening with Yamamoto restored to the hill.

THREE MEN CONVERGE UPON ONE POP FLY AND ALL RISE; MILWAUKEE RISES LIKEWISE, FROM THREE DOWN TO A ROUT: BREW CREW 9, CUBS 4

The Wrigley argument's second evening reversed the first with interest. Chicago banked three fourth-inning runs — Taylor's two-run double the freight, Monday's unfortunate emergency pitcher Andrew Vaughn contributing to the rally's setup from first base — and Milwaukee answered with nine unanswered until a late Cubs run: Pratt's run-scoring single in the fifth, then a four-run sixth in which Vaughn, restored to his proper trade, doubled home the tying run and Christian Yelich struck the go-ahead two-run homer, his tenth. William Contreras doubled twice more in the seventh, Frelick singled home another, and Joey Ortiz closed the accounts with a two-run drive in the eighth — the Brew Crew's six-and-thirtieth comeback victory of the season, Senzatela collecting the decision in relief. The evening's held breath came in the sixth, when Chourio, Pratt, and Ortiz converged at full gallop upon Busch's pop fly to shallow left and went down in a tangle as the ball fell for a double; the trainers attended all three, and all three stayed in the game. "I think everyone wanted it, which is a good thing," said Ortiz. The arithmetic this paper set out Monday now runs the other way: the Central gap is back to eight games, Milwaukee holds the season's series five games to three with five meetings left inside eight days, and tonight the visitors send Misiorowski — fourteen victories, an earned-run figure of 1.73, and the National's strikeout lead — against Peterson at 7:40.

HARPER REACHES FIVE TIMES, LUZARDO CARRIES THE SEVENTH, AND THE SIXTH STRAIGHT BUYS SOLE TITLE TO THE TOP CHAIR: PHILLIES 7, SONORAN NINE 1

The Brotherly Love nine made it six victories running by the simplest of formulas: Bryce Harper refused to be retired — four hits and a walk, five times aboard, his on-base streak now four-and-thirty games — and Jesús Luzardo pitched into the seventh bearing a single run, his ledger now thirteen victories against five. Turner's run-scoring double and a first-inning double play cashed two early; Bohm singled home Harper in the fifth; and the late innings added four by instalments, Crawford and Harper singling home runs around sacrifice flies from Schwarber and Stott. Arizona's whole account was the young Lawlar's fourth homer, and the defeat — a fourth straight — is costly coin: the Snakes of Monday's number slip a half-game from the National's last chair. Philadelphia, meanwhile, banks the prize this surge has been buying toward: sole possession of the wild-card's top chair at nine-and-seventy wins, one clear of Chicago, with the Eastern gap to Atlanta down to three. The clubs conclude in the desert at 3:40 this afternoon, Painter opposing Clarke.

PERALTA MEETS HIS FORMER EMPLOYERS AND SUBMITS THE EVIDENCE OF HIS RECOVERY: TAMPA MEN 6, METS 2

Freddy Peralta, shipped from New York to Tampa Bay at the deadline for three prospects after a season of five-and-nine and an earned-run figure near five, faced the Mets for the first time since the transaction and pitched as if settling the account line by line: six innings, three hits, one run, not a walk, seven strikeouts. "I have my confidence back," said the right-hander, whose two starts as a Ray are now two victories. "I think that's a more important thing for a pitcher, when you trust in your stuff again." His catcher Yandy Díaz furnished the offense besides the testimony — a homer, a double, and two runs driven in — with Vilade, Fortes, and Walls filling out a thirteen-hit evening. Lindor's leadoff homer and a bases-loaded walk were the whole of the visitors' account. The Tampa men, at three-and-eighty wins, keep the American's best ledger and a four-and-a-half-game lead in the East; the rubber match goes at 6:40 this evening under the dome.

TATIS STRIKES TWO, AND IT BUYS NOTHING — THE RED STOCKINGS TAKE THE EIGHTH AND THE GAME: REDS 4, PADRES 3

Fernando Tatis Jr. gave San Diego a solo homer in the fifth and another in the ninth, his eighteenth and nineteenth, and departed Cincinnati beaten anyway. The decisive eighth belonged to the home nine: Trevino's single cashed De La Cruz with the go-ahead run — the restoration desk above has the gladder meaning of that sentence — and Brito's sacrifice fly added the insurance that Tatis's second blast could only trim. The reliever Burke took the decision, Pagán sealed his twentieth save, and the loss fell to Morejon, whose two-thirds of an inning in the eighth unraveled a game his club led nowhere but never trailed until it mattered. The defeat, set beside Arizona's below, leaves the Padres' hold on the National's last chair at half a game — with the new arrival Salas watching from the dugout rail, as the vestibule desk relates — and this afternoon's 12:40 rubber match, Mize opposing Williamson, decides the set.

WILLIAMS STRIKES OUT THIRTEEN FOR A CAREER HIGH, AND THREE TWO-RUN BLASTS DO THE REST: LAKE ERIE MEN 6, MAPLE LEAF NINE 1

Gavin Williams opened the set that bears most directly upon the American's last chair by striking out thirteen Torontos across seven innings — the highest count of his life, two hits and an unearned run the only blemishes — and his mates supplied the scoring in matched pairs: two-run homers from the catcher Bailey in the fifth, the young Genao in the seventh, and Angel Martínez in the eighth — six runs in three identical strokes, Martínez crossing the plate three times himself. Fourteen strikeouts in all for the home staff; the visitors' run scored on a wild pitch. Cleveland has now won eight of ten, and the victory holds the last wild-card chair by a game and a half over Texas and Baltimore alike. The set continues at 6:40 this evening with a delicacy for the connoisseur: Cease, who shares the American's strikeout lead at 217 with the very Williams who just cleared his own bar, takes the hill for Toronto against Cantillo — the co-leaders in the same yard on consecutive nights.

FIVE RUNS IN THE SECOND, A DEBUT OF THUNDER, AND THE SKID IS SNAPPED AT FIVE: MARINERS 9, FENWAY MEN 6

Seattle, five times beaten running as yesterday's number tallied, spent one Fenway second inning ending the argument: five runs, Crawford's sacrifice fly and Rodden's single opening the ledger before Randy Arozarena's three-run homer, his two-and-twentieth, closed it. Canzone had led off the game with a homer, Raleigh doubled home another, and the debutant Montes — walked, singled, and homered, as the debut desk sings above — closed the scoring at nine. Bryan Woo took his tenth victory. Boston's reply came in instalments too late: Gasper, whose overnight arrival these pages recorded Tuesday, homered for a second consecutive day, White doubled home two, and Jones's two-run drive in the ninth dressed the final account. The rookie Paez bore the loss. The Fenway men, still four and a half clear in the wild-card row, get the rubber match at 4:10 this afternoon; the Seattle nine sail on with their first victory of September and a young man who looks like a reason to watch it.

TEN RUNS IN THE SIXTH INNING ALONE, AND THE BENGALS ARE BURIED WHOLESALE: TWIN CITIES NINE 15, TIGERS 2

Minnesota's sixth inning produced more runs than most clubs manage in an evening: ten of them, opened by Royce Lewis's three-run homer and sustained through Keaschall's single, Culpepper's double, and a procession the Detroit bullpen could not staunch — Finnegan facing six men and retiring one. The fuller ledger reads fifteen runs on thirteen hits: Clemens homered in the first, Jeffers added a homer and a triple among three driven in, Lewis and Culpepper three runs batted in apiece, and the rookie Jenkins, hitless on the night, scored twice anyway. Prielipp carried five scoreless innings in relief for the victory; Melton, whose earned-run figure had been among the American's best, bore seven runs and the loss. Three straight now for the Twin Cities nine, whose September appetite this paper remarked yesterday — the last chair sits three and a half games distant, improbable but no longer unmentionable — while the Motor City men have lost three running and eight of ten, and must send Anderson against Kremer on the same grounds at 7:40 tonight.

FOUR IN THE SECOND, A DEBUT BLAST IN THE THIRD, AND ATLANTA DROPS A SECOND STRAIGHT IN THE CAPITAL: SENATORS 9, BRAVES 5

Washington's second inning broke the game open — CJ Abrams's two-run single and Brady House's two-run double the heavy freight — and the rookie Morales opened the third with the first-game homer the debut desk celebrates above. House added a sacrifice fly among his three driven in, Young and Chaparro singled home late runs, and when Atlanta's eighth closed to within three on a wild pitch and Michael Harris II's two-run homer, the Senators answered at once and shut the door. The loss went to Smith-Shawver, four innings and five runs in a September audition that gave the wire desks fresh cause to wonder whether he figures in Atlanta's October arrangements; the win went to the reliever Simpson, three perfect innings with three strikeouts. Albies homered besides for the visitors. The Tomahawk men have now dropped two straight on the heels of their seven-game streak, and the cost is counted in two ledgers: the East's lead over surging Philadelphia is down to three, and the second bye belongs this morning to Los Angeles by half a game. The clubs meet again at 1:05 this very afternoon, Holmes opposing Lord.

SIX STINTS WITH THE CLUB THIS SEASON, AND AT LAST A NIGHT TO KEEP: FISH 6, ROYALS 3

Graham Pauley has been summoned to Miami and returned to the minors so often this summer — six separate engagements with the big club, by the wires' count — that the man might reasonably keep his bags packed by the clubhouse door. Tuesday he unpacked: a two-run homer in the second, a two-run triple in the seventh, and a run scored on Sanoja's single directly after, four driven in altogether on the finest night of his major-league life. Stowers added a solo blast, his seventeenth, and Phillips pitched five innings of one hit for the victory while Kansas City's account waited until the eighth — Jensen's double and Witt's sacrifice fly, with Loftin's ninth-inning homer the coda. The Fish, three games from the National's last chair, have designs on the same arithmetic as half the league; the Royal blues, whose August surge these pages chronicled at length, have now dropped three of four, and the clubs go again at 7:40 this evening.

NIMMO COLLECTS FOUR HITS AND FIVE RUNS BATTED IN, AND THE WEST'S GAP SHRINKS TO ONE: LONE STAR NINE 8, MACKMEN 5

Brandon Nimmo spent the evening driving in Texas runs by every legal means short of the homer: a run-scoring double in the first, singles cashing runs in the third, fifth, and sixth — four hits, five driven in, and the Arlington grandstand chanting a man's name in September for reasons beyond nostalgia. Seager doubled twice and scored ahead of him repeatedly, sacrifice flies from O'Hoppe and Seager fattened the sixth, and Gore pitched into the sixth for his eighth victory, Latz recording the final two outs by strikeout for his eight-and-twentieth save. The Mackmen's late push — McNeil's two-run homer in the ninth the chief of it — arrived too late by three innings. Three straight now for the Lone Star nine, whose home ledger of nine-and-thirty wins against nine-and-twenty defeats keeps fueling the push: they stand this morning one game from Houston's crown — the leaders having lost, as the card below relates — and a game and a half from the wild-card row. The same clubs meet at 2:35 this afternoon.

SEAN BURKE ALLOWS ONE HIT INTO THE SIXTH, AND THE MEETING OF LEADERS TILTS SOUTH SIDE: WHITE SOX 5, ASTROS 1

The September collision of division leaders now reads two games to none for Chicago: Sean Burke carried a single hit into the sixth inning — five and two-thirds scoreless in all, six strikeouts against four walks he stranded methodically — and Houston finished the evening with three hits entire. The scoring waited for the fifth, when Benintendi singled home the first run and Colson Montgomery doubled home two more; Grichuk's two-run double in the seventh supplied the margin, and Paredes's eighteenth homer in the eighth spoiled only the shutout. The South Siders hold the Central by three over Cleveland, which matched them win for win; the Houston nine's crown in the West, two games wide on Monday, is down to a single game over Texas — and the set concludes at 8:10 this evening, Martin opposing Wesneski, with the standings of two divisions leaning on the result.

RUMFIELD BREAKS THE TIE, THE HOME SKID DIES AT TEN, AND BALTIMORE'S STAY AT .500 LASTS EXACTLY ONE DAY: MILE HIGH NINE 4, ORIOLES 2

The Coloradans had lost ten consecutive games at their own yard — the longest home skid in all the franchise's annals — and chose the visit of a surging club to end it. Amador's homer and McCarthy's run-scoring single built an early two; Baltimore clawed level by increments, Narváez scoring on a double play in the third and Jackson Holliday — three hits on the night — tying the account with his seventh homer in the seventh; whereupon TJ Rumfield answered in the home half with a two-run drive to right-center, his fourteenth, and the thing was done. Hill took his first victory of the season, Romano the save. For the Baltimores the toll is precise and a little cruel: level at .500 on Monday for the first time since April, they stand a game under this morning, a game and a half from Cleveland's chair — the pursuit, as this paper said yesterday, general, but the quarry now a step farther off. The set concludes at 3:10 this afternoon in the mountain air.

TWO HOMERS BEFORE THE FIRST OUT OF THE EVENING, AND THE SOLD CLUB IS BEATEN BESIDES: BRONX BOMBERS 7, ANGELS 3

On the day the franchise changed hands — the ownership desk above has the particulars — the Angels' evening began with Spencer Jones and Jazz Chisholm Jr. striking back-to-back homers before the first inning was a batter old, and improved little thereafter. Jones finished with three driven in and two stolen bases, Chisholm three hits, and Bellinger three hits of his own; Gerrit Cole battled into the sixth bearing eight hits but only three runs for his eighth victory, and the late innings added on through Grisham's single, Goldschmidt's sacrifice fly, and Jones's ninth-inning knock. Moore's two-run triple had briefly made a game of it. New York, four and a half behind Tampa Bay with the top wild-card chair eight and a half games deep in its pocket, sends Schlittler — the American's earned-run leader at 2.09 — into tonight's 9:38 rubber match against Detmers; and the rotation's plans run further ahead: the club intends to pair the restored Fried with Warren in piggyback fashion for Friday's opener at San Diego, the captain's own return hovering over the homestand beyond, as this ward has chronicled.

The tables at midweek, taken in one turning. In the American: Tampa Bay holds the East by four and a half over New York, with Boston eight and a half astern; the White Sox keep the Central by three over Cleveland; and the West is suddenly a knife-fight — Houston by a single game over Texas, the leaders beaten two straight on the South Side's account while the Lone Star men won their third running. The wild-card row reads New York, Boston, and Cleveland's last chair by a game and a half over Texas and Baltimore alike, with Toronto two and a half back. In the National: Atlanta's lead in the East is pared to three over Philadelphia, whose six straight have bought sole title to the wild-card's top chair, one clear of Chicago; Milwaukee's Central lead is restored to eight; Los Angeles holds the West by nine and a half — and the second bye is the champions' by half a game over Atlanta, both clubs having lost Tuesday. The last chair reads San Diego by half a game over Arizona, with Miami three back, St. Louis four, and Pittsburgh five and coming loudly, as the lead column attests. Tonight's card runs to fifteen engagements, all verified against the schedule, and the desk directs the reader's eye to four: at 12:40 in Cincinnati the rubber match may open the vestibule for young Salas, Mize opposing Williamson; at 6:40 in Cleveland the strikeout co-leaders pass in the corridor, Cease and his 217 taking the hill one night after Williams's thirteen; at 7:40 the Wrigley argument's third chapter sends Misiorowski and his 1.73 against Peterson; and at 8:10 in Houston two divisions lean on the finale of the leaders' set. The 9:38 rubber in Anaheim, Schlittler opposing Detmers, is commended to the night owls. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
