Volume I · No. 29 · Thursday, the Sixth of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

TWO HOMERS FOR OHTANI, TWO FOR CROW-ARMSTRONG, AND THE LAST WORD TO WRIGLEY: NORTH SIDERS 7, TROLLEY DODGERS 6

The great Japanese opens the evening with a leadoff circuit and closes his account with a two-run blast in the eighth, but the young Chicago centerfielder answers him stroke for stroke — five Chicago doubles behind him, four runs batted in of his own — and Webb bars a quiet ninth while Los Angeles's slide reaches six

Some evenings the standings step aside and the argument is conducted by artillery alone. Shohei Ohtani served notice on the day's first swing worth the name, opening the contest with his five-and-twentieth home run, a line drive to right center — whereupon Pete Crow-Armstrong, who evidently regards the Most Valuable conversation as unfinished business, answered in the bottom of the same inning with his own five-and-twentieth. From there the North Siders simply kept hitting: Swanson and Crow-Armstrong doubled home runs in the second, the centerfielder struck a two-run circuit in the fourth — number six-and-twenty — Bregman singled one across behind him, and Hoerner's seventh-inning double made it seven runs to one. Fifteen hits Chicago laid upon Lauer and his successors, eleven of them in the loser's four innings alone.

The champions did not go quietly. In the eighth, against the Chicago relief corps, Max Muncy hammered a three-run homer, his two-and-twentieth, and Ohtani followed with a two-run blast of his own — number six-and-twenty, squaring the pair's ledgers at the top of the National's power column — and suddenly a 7-to-1 stroll stood 7 to 6 with an inning yet to play. But Webb pitched a ninth as calm as a Sunday parlor for his sixth save, and Imanaga, who had scattered eight hits across five innings while conceding only Ohtani's first salute, took the decision.

The arithmetic, when it reclaimed the floor: Crow-Armstrong finished with three hits, two homers, and four runs batted in, nine-and-sixty on the year; Betts collected four hits in a losing cause. Los Angeles has now dropped six running — two and eight across the last ten — though its Western cushion still measures eight games over Arizona. The Cubs have won three straight, stand at six-and-sixty wins, and hold the National's first wild-card chair while trailing Milwaukee by five and a half. Eight-and-thirty thousand at Wrigley went home hoarse, and the clubs part company — Chicago entertains Toronto this afternoon.

News Around the League

THE TRIPLE CROWN GLIMMERS OVER HOUSTON: ALVAREZ HOLDS ALL THREE COLUMNS AT ONCE

The Herald directs the reader's attention to a rare alignment in the American's ledgers: Yordan Alvarez this morning sits atop the batting column at .329 — nearly thirty points clear of the field — atop the home-run column at five-and-thirty, and atop the runs-batted-in column, where last night's single in the tenth inning ran his account to five-and-eighty. The wires have begun speaking the words Triple Crown aloud, and the nearest pursuit is real but distant: Tampa Bay's young Caminero, whose three-and-thirtieth circuit last evening keeps him two behind in the power column. No man has swept the American's three columns since Miguel Cabrera in 2012. The Herald makes no prophecy; it merely notes that August has arrived and the big Houston left-hander of the bat shows no inclination to share.

THE INFIRMARY WARD BRIGHTENS: JUDGE CLEARED TO RUN, AND BELLINGER SWINGS UPON THE FIELD

A better bulletin from the Bronx than yesterday's. Aaron Judge, absent since the last of May with the fractured rib, underwent his latest round of scans Tuesday, and the pictures showed healing enough that the thrice-honored man has been cleared for light labors — running out of doors, resistance work for the upper half — and took the field at the Stadium before Wednesday's contest to begin them. "I'll be back this season," the captain declared. "I don't know why I wouldn't be." His fellow convalescent Bellinger took batting practice upon the field for the first time the same afternoon. The Bombers, who have soldiered on without their colossus since May's close, permit themselves a measure of optimism.

FIVE MEN PLUNKED, TWO MEN BANISHED: THE KETTLE BOILS OVER IN SEATTLE

The simmering acquaintance of the Tigers and the Mariners — postseason adversaries last autumn, brawlers in Detroit two months since — came to a rolling boil Wednesday night. Bryan Woo threw seven scoreless innings while striking three Detroit batsmen with pitches, a combination the wires report no major-league pitcher had managed in four years; when the first man out of the Seattle bullpen, Speier, promptly hit Torres in the eighth, the benches emptied. No blows were struck, but Speier was banished along with the Seattle manager Wilson, five hit batsmen stood on the evening's docket in all, and the clubs — who settle the series this very afternoon at ten past four — exchanged home runs and hard looks the rest of the way. The particulars of the 4-to-2 verdict are itemized upon the card below; the Herald merely observes that the kettle has not been removed from the stove.

EIGHT HUNDRED NINETY-SIX, AND MUSIAL YIELDS THE RUNG: OLSON STANDS EIGHTH ALONE

The iron procession advanced a step in Atlanta: Matt Olson answered the bell Wednesday for the eight hundred and ninety-sixth consecutive game, passing the sainted Stan Musial outright for sole possession of eighth place on the all-time roll — a streak unbroken since the second of May, 2021, begun in Oakland's elephant livery and carried on in Atlanta's. Yesterday the Herald reported him drawing level with the great Cardinal; today he tips his cap and walks past. The nearest active pursuer, Baltimore's Alonso, stands at five hundred and twenty-three — which is to say, more than two full seasons behind. The seventh rung waits within reach of a man who simply keeps showing up, and no man in the game shows up like Olson.

THE FIRST ENTRY IN A LONG LEDGER: CHOLOWSKY LACES HIS FIRST PROFESSIONAL KNOCK

A day after the glove gave its first sermon, the bat spoke. Roch Cholowsky, first name called in this summer's draft and the costliest signature in White Sox annals, stung a ground ball up the middle Wednesday for High-A Winston-Salem — an RBI double, as the scorer ruled it, and the first hit of his professional life — and drove in two runs altogether in a 7-to-4 victory over Asheville. One game, no hits; two games, a knock and two runs batted in. The Herald repeats its counsel of yesterday — careers are judged by the full run and not the first page — but notes with approval that the first page now bears ink.

The Rest of the Card

CLEMENT STRIKES IN THE TENTH AND GIMÉNEZ WINS THE LAST ARGUMENT WITH HIS WITS: CANADIANS 5, HOUSTONS 4

Down three runs after three, Toronto drew level in the sixth on Okamoto's single and Jesús Sánchez's two-run circuit, then won the extra session when Ernie Clement hammered a two-run homer in the tenth. Houston's reply fell one short by way of the evening's cleverest larceny: Alvarez singled home a run with the tying man aboard, but Giménez, receiving the throw at second, deked the great slugger into complacency on the throw in from Lukes, and the Triple Crown candidate was tagged out at the bag for his ambition. Altuve had homered early; Lee recorded his first save of the season. The Astros' Western margin over Texas is a game and a half.

A BULLPEN PARADE AND NOT A RUN ALLOWED: LONE STAR NINE 6, FRISCO NINE 0

Texas passed the baseball from hand to hand all evening and no San Francisco man crossed the plate against any of them, Alexander taking the decision with five outs of hitless relief. Nimmo supplied the thunder with his twelfth homer and two runs batted in, Foscue tripled home a pair, and the 6-to-0 verdict pulls the Rangers within a game and a half of Houston while the Frisco nine's long summer lengthens. Whisenhunt bore the loss.

THE SWEEP IS SEALED IN THE MOUNTAIN AIR: TAMPA MEN 4, ROX 0

Martinez threw five and two-thirds scoreless for his eleventh victory against three defeats, the relief corps finished a combined shutout, and Junior Caminero struck his three-and-thirtieth homer — two shy of Alvarez's American lead, as the news column relates. Mesa Jr. tripled twice and Simpson stole his thirtieth base to join the league's leaders. The sweep of Colorado complete, the Rays — winners of three running and holders of the American's finest ledger at eight-and-sixty wins — carry their trade west to Seattle on the morrow. Sugano fell to eleven-and-five for the Mountain men.

THREE CIRCUITS AND NINE STRIKEOUTS SETTLE A TIDY AFTERNOON'S WORK: BALTIMORES 5, HALOS 2

O'Neill, Taveras, and Mayo each cleared the Camden wall, Rogers struck out nine across six innings for his seventh victory, and Kittredge affixed the fifth seal. Siri's sixth homer was the better part of Anaheim's reply, and Detmers — for all his league-class strikeout figures — fell to three-and-eight. The remade Birds have won two straight and sit a bare half-game from the American's final wild-card chair.

MCLAIN'S NINTH IS THE DIFFERENCE AND THE MACKMEN'S MISERY REACHES SEVEN: REDLEGS 3, ATHLETICS 2

Lowder gave six workmanlike innings, McLain's ninth homer supplied the decisive run, and Pagán barred the ninth for his thirteenth save in a 3-to-2 verdict. Butler's seventh circuit kept the visitors close, but the Athletics have now lost seven running — one win in their last ten — and the Redlegs, winners of three straight, keep the wild-card table within their sight.

THREE FLUSHING HOMERS AND THREE CLEVELAND ERRORS: FLUSHING NINE 6, FOREST CITY NINE 5

Benge, Baty, and Polanco all went over the wall, Cleveland's gloves committed three errors in support of the cause against them, and the Mets held on 6 to 5 — McDermott earning the victory with two hitless innings and Duarte his first save of the year. The Guardians have slipped a game below the level mark and cling to a share of the wild-card line's last chair; the Mets, sellers turned spoilers, have won two straight and go again at Cleveland this very afternoon.

SIX RUNS IN THE ELEVENTH AND A PITCHER'S BUNT AMONG THEM: CAPITAL NINE 10, PHILS 4, THE SKID SNAPPED AT SEVEN

Level at three after nine and at four after ten, Washington emptied the larder in the eleventh: Abrams tripled home two — three runs batted in on his night, eight-and-eighty on his year — whereupon Miles Mikolas, a pitcher pressed into pinch-hitting service, laid down a squeeze bunt that scored a run and scandalized nobody who loves the old game, and Chaparro doubled home two more behind him. Ten runs to four, the seven-game skid snapped, and — the almanac savors this — Mikolas takes the hill in earnest this evening in the same park. House's eighth homer had squared the affair in the seventh; Arraez's fifth, with its now-famous flourish of the bat, opened Philadelphia's account.

WETHERHOLT DOUBLES THE DECISIVE PAIR THROUGH THE BRONX RAIN: REDBIRDS 3, BRONX BOMBERS 1

An hour's rain delayed matters and St. Louis needed only the fifth inning to settle them: Wetherholt's two-run double and Herrera's ground-rule double made it three, and Pallante — five and a third innings of three-hit work for his twelfth victory — handed the balance to a bullpen that walked the tightrope, O'Brien recording his seven-and-twentieth save. New York's lone reply was a bases-loaded walk. The Redbirds thus claim the series in the Bronx and climb back to a game below level; the Bombers, still holders of the American's first wild-card chair, await Atlanta on the morrow.

VINTAGE GRAY, AND THE STREAK RUNS TO SEVEN: BOSTONS 4, SOUTH SIDERS 0

Sonny Gray struck out eight across six shutout innings for his fourteenth victory against two defeats — no man in the American has more — and Abreu's twentieth homer headed a 4-to-0 verdict that runs Boston's streak to seven straight, nine wins in ten. The South Siders, blanked on four hits, still hold the Central by three over Cleveland, and the clubs meet again at Fenway this evening, where the Hub nine will try for eight.

ELDER PERMITS ONE HIT IN SEVEN AND THE MARCH REACHES SEVEN: ATLANTAS 4, FLORIDIANS 1

Bryce Elder carried a single hit allowed through seven innings for his eighth victory, Baldwin drove in two, and Fuentes closed the book on a 4-to-1 verdict — Atlanta's seventh consecutive win, its Eastern margin now eight and a half games. Olson's attendance, as the news column records, made history of its own. The Gulf Stream nine fall three games off the wild-card table.

CAMERON SCATTERS TWO HITS ACROSS EIGHT AND MASSEY SUPPLIES THE MARGIN: MISSOURIANS 2, NORTH STARS 1

Noah Cameron gave eight innings of two-hit, nine-strikeout pitching — as fine a line as the American produced Wednesday — and Massey's ninth homer stood as the difference in a 2-to-1 verdict sealed by Cruz. Minnesota, held without an extra-base hit, has dropped four running and slips a full game off the wild-card line. The clubs meet tonight for the last time this season, as the desk note relates.

TEN STRIKEOUTS, EIGHT OF THEM RUNNING, AND THEN THE LEGS GIVE OUT: SUDS MEN 4, ALLEGHENYS 2

Kyle Harrison, returning from the infirmary, struck out ten Pittsburgh batsmen across five one-hit innings — the last eight in unbroken succession, two shy of the all-time record — and then, warming for the sixth with history holding its breath, summoned the trainer instead: cramping in both calves, the club reported, and the record must wait. The Brewers won regardless, 4 to 2, Contreras homering and Megill sealing his twentieth save; hard-luck Skenes allowed but two hits in five innings, was undone by four walks and three runs, and fell to nine-and-ten. Milwaukee's ledger, one-and-seventy wins, remains the game's finest; Rodríguez homered for the Smoky City cause.

WOO THROWS SEVEN SCORELESS AND THE EVENING THROWS PUNCHES IT NEVER QUITE LANDS: SEATTLE NINE 4, MOTOR CITY NINE 2

The pugilistic particulars are given their own column above; the baseball ran thus: Bryan Woo threw seven scoreless innings for his eighth victory, Julio Rodríguez struck a three-run homer, his seventeenth, Emerson added an eighth, and Muñoz closed a 4-to-2 verdict for his twentieth save. Dingler's five-and-twentieth circuit — five-and-seventy runs batted in now, second in the American — supplied Detroit's answer, and the defeat ends the Motor City men's four-game run. This afternoon's finale promises close attention from the umpires.

A FIRST VICTORY EARNED ON ONE HIT AND TREMBLING LEGS: SONORAN NINE 10, MISSION NINE 4

The rookie Mitch Bratt threw seven innings of one-hit baseball with nine strikeouts for the first victory of his major-league life — "couldn't feel my legs," the young man confessed afterward — while Casey Mize, making his first appearance in San Diego colors after a whirlwind week, was struck for nine hits and eight runs inside four innings. Vargas drove in four, Tawa homered, and the 10-to-4 verdict keeps Arizona in the National's wild-card chairs with San Diego two games in arrears. Tatis Jr.'s ninth homer salvaged what dignity the evening allowed. The clubs conclude the set tonight in the desert.

This day's bill runs to eleven contests, and the connoisseur is spoilt. The afternoon offers Toronto at Wrigley at twenty past two, Cease and his 2.41 figure against the Cubs' hot hand, and the powder-keg finale in Seattle at ten past four, where Tigers and Mariners resume their acquaintance under, one presumes, a magistrate's gaze. Come evening: the bunting hero Mikolas takes the hill in Philadelphia at five past six against Sánchez, whose fourteen victories and 2.61 figure lead the National; Boston seeks its eighth straight against the Central-leading South Siders at Fenway at ten past seven; the Twins and Royals meet at forty past seven for the final time this season; and the Friars and Snakes settle their desert argument at forty past nine. The almanac notes besides that Friday brings the full fifteen and a set worth circling — the nine-and-sixty-win Braves call upon the Yankees in the Bronx. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
