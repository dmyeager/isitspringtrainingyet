Volume I · No. 26 · Monday, the Third of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

SIXTEEN SUMMERS ENDED IN A SINGLE INNING: THREE CIRCUITS IN THE THIRD, AN 8-TO-4 VERDICT, AND BOSTON SWEEPS THE TWICE-CROWNED ANGELENOS

Rafaela, Abreu, and Yoshida all clear the fences in one thunderous frame — the wires certify it Boston's first sweep of the Dodgers since 2010 — Duran adds a fourth circuit, Bennett takes the decision, and the Hub nine's fifth straight victory sends the champions to a third consecutive defeat on the eve of Skubal's arrival

Not since the year 2010, the wires attest, had a Boston nine put the broom to the Dodgers — and the sixteen-year account was settled in the space of one inning. Los Angeles had actually claimed the lead in the second, Andy Pages's two-run single capping a three-run reply to Boston's early pair; whereupon the third inning arrived, and with it the artillery. Ceddanne Rafaela lifted his fifteenth home run to left-center to square the matter, Wilyer Abreu followed with his seventeenth to right-center for the lead, and Masataka Yoshida struck his fifth to right — three circuits in the one frame, five runs to three, and six-and-forty thousand at Chavez Ravine gone quiet as a reading room.

Jarren Duran made it four home runs on the day with his fourteenth in the sixth, and the eighth inning supplied a codicil of pure vaudeville: a single wild delivery skipping far enough away that both Caleb Durbin and Andruw Monasterio came around to score upon it. Bennett worked five and two-thirds for his seventh victory against four defeats; Sheehan, chased in the third with five runs against his name, absorbed the loss. Pages drove in three of the four Los Angeles runs and was the whole of their argument.

The ledgers this morning make stark reading in both cities. Boston has won five straight and eight of ten, and sits four games clear at the American's second wild-card chair; Los Angeles has dropped three running, and its lately purchased ace — Tarik Skubal, of whom more in the news column — arrives to a clubhouse freshly swept in its own yard, the game's best record having passed to Milwaukee besides. The West's cushion, ten games over the Diamondbacks, remains the club's chief consolation.

News Around the League

THE BELL TOLLS AT SIX THIS EVENING: A SABBATH OF COMMERCE ITEMIZED, WITH GAUSMAN NORTH-BY-SOUTH, PERALTA TO THE GULF, AND MAHLE TO ATLANTA

The deadline arrives today, at six o'clock in the evening by Eastern reckoning, and Sunday passed in a proper fever of dealing. The Cubs, wanting an arm for the stretch, obtained Kevin Gausman from Toronto for the young outfielder Brett Bateman and the shortstop Ty Southisene — this but a day after the veteran walked off to a standing ovation at Toronto — while in the same breath the Cubs dispatched Jameson Taillon and cash to those very Blue Jays for a player to be named, the two rotations passing one another like ships. The Rays fortified their league-leading staff with the twice-an-All-Star right-hander Freddy Peralta from the Mets for a trio of young men — Gill Hill, Smith, and Pitre — and Atlanta, its rotation thinned by injury, secured Tyler Mahle from San Francisco for the right-hander Anthony Molina.

Nor was that the whole of it: the wires report the Yankees have landed Washington's left-handed slugger García Jr. in exchange for a quartet of pitchers, and the White Sox are said to have added the Mets' reliever Brazobán to their first-place cause. The rumor mill grinds on through the final hours — deGrom's name is still spoken, Boston is said to be inquiring after Anaheim's Neto, and Seattle's intentions remain the subject of much learned guessing. The Herald counsels its readers to take supper early.

THE SKUBAL AFTERMATH: THE PRICE IS PUBLISHED, THE FAREWELL IS DAMP-EYED, AND THE FIRST TURN IN NEW FLANNELS COMES TUESDAY AT WRIGLEY

The particulars of Saturday's thunderclap are now entered in the official ledger: Los Angeles sends the outfielder Zyhir Hope and the right-handers River Ryan and Brady Smith to Detroit for Tarik Skubal, the left-handed lion and twice the American's Cy Young laureate. The scribe Passan pronounced the verdict heard round the wires — "The Dodgers have assembled the Avengers" — while Skubal himself, nine seasons a Tiger, met the press with visible emotion, and his former mates in Detroit confessed the clubhouse to be in a strange country between gratitude and grief.

The calendar now supplies the theater: Skubal's first turn in Dodger flannels is slated for Tuesday evening at Wrigley Field, where Los Angeles opens with the Cubs tonight — as keen a stage as the schedule could have contrived. The Herald notes, with a chronicler's impartiality, that since bidding their ace farewell the Tigers have won three straight, while his new employers have lost as many.

THE TRIPLE CROWN COMES INTO VIEW: ALVAREZ NOW LEADS THE AMERICAN IN ALL THREE JEWELS AT ONCE

The Herald directs the reader's eye to the American League's leader boards, where a rare alignment is forming. Yordan Alvarez of Houston this morning stands first in batting average at .330 — nine-and-twenty points clear of Tampa Bay's Díaz — first in home runs at five-and-thirty, four ahead of the Yankee Rice, and first in runs batted in at four-and-eighty, eleven ahead of the same pursuer. Three doubles in last evening's conquest of Texas (see the card, below) did the case no harm whatever, and his club has won six straight into the bargain. Two months remain, and the chase of all three jewels at once — the batsman's triple crown — will henceforth be a standing entry in this column.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: FRALEY AND SOSA TO THE LONG LIST, LÓPEZ'S KNEE THINS ATLANTA, AND TWO NOTABLE ARMS TAKE THE REHAB ROAD

The weekend's medical ledger runs long. Tampa Bay has placed the outfielder Jake Fraley on the sixty-day list with a hernia, and Toronto has done likewise with the infielder Lenyn Sosa, his right wrist the trouble. Atlanta's Reynaldo López goes to the fifteen-day list with inflammation of the left knee — the very vacancy the Mahle purchase was made to fill. On the brighter side of the ward, the Mets' Clay Holmes has begun a rehabilitation assignment with Brooklyn, and Detroit's prized young right-hander Jackson Jobe is likewise at work with West Michigan, both men on the road back.

THE ALMANAC: TEN DAYS TO THE CORNFIELD, THEN WILLIAMSPORT, AND THE FINAL BELL ON THE SEVEN-AND-TWENTIETH OF SEPTEMBER

For the forward-looking reader, the fixed stars of the calendar: the Field of Dreams engagement comes the thirteenth of August — ten days hence — with the Twins and Phillies to play amid the Iowa corn at Dyersville; the Little League Classic follows on the three-and-twentieth, Brewers against Braves at Williamsport; Roberto Clemente Day falls upon the fifteenth of September; and the regular season closes its books on the seven-and-twentieth of that month. Before any of it stands this evening's deadline bell, of which the reader has already been amply warned.

The Rest of the Card

THE DELAY OUTLASTS THE BASEBALL: QUAKERS 8, ORIOLES 0, IN SIX INNINGS AND UNDER TWO HOURS OF ACTUAL PLAY

The rains at Baltimore wrote the oddest line of the day: two hours and seventeen minutes of delay against one hour and four-and-fifty minutes of baseball, the affair called complete after six frames with Philadelphia eight runs to the good. Wheeler's evening was clipped at two hitless-in-earnest innings, and four Quaker arms in all combined upon a four-hit shutout, the win falling to Mayza. Bryce Harper struck his three-and-twentieth circuit, De La Cruz added a home run and three runs batted in for the second productive evening running, and Bradish bore the defeat. The wires certify it Philadelphia's first series taken since the All-Star respite; the Birds are checked again.

THE ATLANTAS MAKE IT FIVE RUNNING: OLSON'S ONE-AND-THIRTIETH ANCHORS A 4-TO-2 VERDICT OVER THE WASHINGTONS

Matt Olson homered for the second day in succession, his one-and-thirtieth, and Yastrzemski supplied a circuit and a sacrifice fly for two runs besides — steady commerce enough to decide a quiet evening in Georgia. The young Ritchie, summoned from Gwinnett that very morning, gave four serviceable innings in the start; Dodd took the win in relief and Iglesias barred the ninth for his four-and-twentieth save. James Wood's run-scoring knock, his three-and-seventieth of the season, led the visitors' thin account. Atlanta has won five straight and rules its division by eight full games; the Capital men have lost as many in a row.

SCHERZER'S FINE EVENING COMES TO NOTHING: THE MOUND CITY NINE STRIKE FOUR TIMES IN THE NINTH, 5 TO 1

The ancient Max Scherzer gave Toronto six innings of one-run craft, five strikeouts among them, and departed with the match square at one — whereupon the ninth inning undid the whole evening's economy. Four St. Louis runs crossed against Varland, the frame greased by a throwing error, with Jordan Walker's two-run single the heaviest stroke and Nootbaar doubling home another. Liberatore had matched Scherzer with six innings of one-hit work; Soriano, though he surrendered the tying run in the eighth, was rewarded with the victory. The Cardinals thus arrest a three-game slide; the Maple Leaf nine, freshly parted from Gausman, drop a fourth in five.

A PERFECT-GAME ESSAY, A BUNT, AND BOTH BENCHES IN THE RAIN: LAKE ERIE MEN 5, DIAMONDBACKS 0

Williams carried a perfect-game essay deep enough into the wet Cleveland evening to make the grandstand believe, fanning ten men across five and two-thirds — and when Arizona's Tim Tawa essayed a bunt with the spell still unbroken, both benches emptied into the pouring rain to debate the etiquette of it, the wires recording more oratory than fisticuffs. The bid dissolved in due course, but scarcely the verdict: the Snakes managed two base hits in all the evening. Chase DeLauter doubled and drove in three, Bailey struck his sixth circuit, and Kelly took the defeat. The victory is Williams's eleventh, second in the American only to Boston's Gray, and draws Cleveland within three of the Central's first chair.

ELEVEN HITS, ONE RUN: THE SUNSHINE NINE STRAND A REGIMENT, AND THE SOUTH SIDERS ANSWER SATURDAY WITH A 9-TO-1 THUMPING

The league will not soon see a stranger ledger: Tampa Bay out-hit Chicago eleven to nine and was beaten by eight runs, the regiment left aboard the bases being the whole explanation. Kay wove through seven Rays safeties across six and a third scoreless innings, and the South Side artillery did the rest — Pereira a double and a home run for four runs batted in, Vargas his four-and-twentieth circuit, Benintendi his twelfth, with four runs in the ninth for emphasis. The newly purchased Luis Castillo was activated before the match and watched his new mates avenge Saturday's 1-to-0 defeat; Chicago's Central lead stands at three, and the Rays' Eastern cushion holds at two and a half.

ALCANTARA'S TWELFTH, AND THE FISH BLANK THE SELLING METROPOLITANS, 2 TO 0

Sandy Alcantara allowed four hits across six scoreless innings for his twelfth victory, Joe Mack supplied the margin with his eleventh home run, and Fairbanks closed the account for his seventeenth save. The Mets' side of the ledger carries its own melancholy: Robert Stock, his contract purchased from Syracuse that very day while the front office sold Peralta and Brazobán out from around him, gave five creditable innings and took the defeat in his first decision. Miami has won three straight and presses within a game and a half of the National's last wild-card chair; the Metropolitans, shut out and stripped for parts, play out the string.

TWELVE DECISIONS, TWELVE VICTORIES: BURNS RUNS HIS ACCOUNT TO 13-AND-1 AS THE QUEEN CITY NINE POUND THE BUCS, 10 TO 2

Chase Burns struck out nine across six innings of two-hit work, and the wires observe the astonishing arithmetic: twelve consecutive winning decisions, a season's account of thirteen victories against a single defeat. His mates spotted him seven runs inside three innings against Keller, and the heavy strokes kept coming — Stewart's four-and-twentieth home run carried his runs-batted-in count to five-and-eighty, drawing him level with Washington's Abrams atop the National, while Elly De La Cruz drove in two. The newly acquired Doval made his first appearance in Pittsburgh flannels, an inning with a run against it — a rough house-warming on a rough evening for the visitors.

FIFTEEN HITS AND A SIXTH STRAIGHT: HOUSTON NINE 7, TEXAS NINE 3, AND THE WEST'S GAP WIDENS TO TWO AND A HALF

The Houston bats laid fifteen hits upon their neighbors, none more elegant than Yordan Alvarez's three doubles — the triple-crown ledger is examined in the news column — while Jeremy Peña homered his thirteenth and drove in two. Rocker was made to answer for ten hits in five and a third; a four-run seventh settled the issue, Abreu took the win in relief, and the rookie Blubaugh recorded the first save of his career. Houston has now won six straight and nine of ten since seizing the division's first chair; Texas, beaten a fifth consecutive time, slips two and a half back with the Mariners a half-game further astern.

COLE GRINDS, CABALLERO THUNDERS, BEDNAR BARS THE DOOR: HIGHLANDERS 2, BRUINS 1, AT WRIGLEY

Gerrit Cole had not his sharpest article — four hits and three walks across five and two-thirds — but he permitted the one run only, and José Caballero's two-run circuit in the third, his eleventh, stood as the whole of the scoring New York required. Headrick wriggled out of the tightest corner of the evening, the wires making particular note of it, and Bednar sealed his four-and-twentieth save. Rea deserved kinder: six innings, two runs, seven strikeouts, and the defeat. The Bombers thus keep within two and a half of Tampa Bay; the Cubs, still holders of the National's first wild-card chair, yield the game's stage tonight to grander theater — see the news column.

FREELAND GOES THE FULL ROUTE IN THE THIN AIR: MILE HIGH NINE 8, KANSAS CITY NINE 1

Kyle Freeland pitched all nine innings — a complete game at altitude, five hits, one run, eight strikeouts — and his reward is a record of 3-and-10 that libels the man entirely. A six-run sixth broke the matter open, Jake McCarthy's three-run circuit the centerpiece, with Sullivan doubling twice and Carrigg twice stealing. Lugo, five walks in five innings, took the loss, and the lately summoned Bummer's single frame went poorly. The Royals have dropped four straight; the Coloradans, whatever their station, have won three of five and played the crispest game on the card.

TEN STRIKEOUTS AND A DEFEAT: THE SERAPHS BLANK THE CREAM CITY NINE, 3 TO 0, ON FOUR HITS

Jacob Misiorowski, the National's earned-run sovereign, fanned ten and allowed but two hits across seven innings — and lost, for Anaheim's four hits were arranged with malice. Wade Meckler's two-run circuit, his third, supplied the lead in the second; Neto singled home a third run in the eighth; and Ureña's six scoreless innings, with Zeferjahn's four-strikeout save behind them, made the arithmetic hold. The shutout in ninety-nine-degree heat is Anaheim's second victory in ten games, so the upset wants savoring. Milwaukee's consolation is considerable: at nine-and-sixty wins against two-and-forty, the Brewers keep the game's finest ledger, a half-game clear of Los Angeles.

LIFE AFTER THE LION, CHAPTER THREE: MOTOR CITY NINE 11, ELEPHANTS 0

Detroit's third straight victory since bidding Skubal farewell was the least suspenseful of them: eleven runs on twelve hits, with Eduardo Valencia doubling twice and driving in four, Torkelson adding three runs batted in, Torres two, and Outman closing the account with his fifth home run in the ninth. Montero worked six scoreless frames for his eighth win while Jump issued six walks and paid for every one. Some eight thousand souls in ninety-five-degree heat watched the White Elephants absorb their fifth consecutive defeat — a sparse and sweltering congregation for an eleven-run sermon.

SIX RUNS COME BACK, AND EMERSON'S FIRST WALK-OFF SETTLES IT ANYWAY: MARINERS OF THE SOUND 7, NORTH STARS 6

For the second evening running Seattle struck Minnesota down at the last stroke, and this one wanted a novelist. George Kirby, whose name rattles through every trade rumor, gave seven scoreless innings as if to raise his own price, and Cal Raleigh's three-run twelfth circuit swelled the lead to six runs clear — whereupon the Twins hauled the whole deficit back, Larnach's two-run homer and Lewis's three-run blast fueling a five-run eighth and Keaschall's sixth squaring it in the ninth. In the home half, young Colt Emerson lined a single to right to send Fairchild home — the first walk-off blow of his career, the wires note — and Muñoz, though he surrendered the tying run, claimed the victory over Gómez. Seattle has won two straight by identical heartbreak; Minnesota falls a game below the wild-card line.

BASABE HOMERS AGAIN, BUT THE FRIARS' EARLY SALVO HOLDS: SAN DIEGO 5, JINTS 4

A four-run second inning proved provisions enough, Jackson Merrill's sixteenth home run and two runs batted in leading the outburst with Cronenworth driving in a pair. San Francisco spent the evening closing the gap — Osleivis Basabe, Saturday's unlikely hero, homered for the second day running, and Rafael Devers's three-and-twentieth in the eighth drew the visitors within one — but Morejon extinguished the rally across the final frames for his second save, striking out three. Canning took the win, Roupp the loss. The Friars have won three straight and eight of ten, and stand but one game from the National's wild-card table; the Jints have dropped three running.

The reader will want two timepieces this evening: one for the deadline bell at six o'clock, and one for the eight-game card that follows hard upon it. Nola opens against Washington at forty past six; Schlittler, whose 2.04 figure is the American's best, receives the Cardinals in the Bronx at five past seven; the Dodgers open their engagement at Wrigley at five past eight — the newly acquired Skubal to wait until Tuesday, say the wires — while Texas, its slide at five, receives the Jints at the same hour; Bieber opposes Javier at Houston at ten past eight; the Rays sample the thin air of Colorado at forty past; and King against Pfaadt closes the bill in the desert at forty past nine. Whatever the six-o'clock bell rings in, the Herald will have it sorted by morning. Until the next dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
