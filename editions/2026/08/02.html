Volume I · No. 25 · Sunday, the Second of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

ALVAREZ WINS IT WITH HIS LEGS: A WILD PITCH, A DASH FOR HOME, AND THE BAYOU CITY NINE EDGE TEXAS, 5 TO 4, TO STRETCH THE WEST'S NEW ORDER

The game's leading slugger scores the decisive run not by the fence but by the basepath, deGrom is saddled with the defeat, Díaz's late circuit falls a run short, and Houston's fifth straight victory pushes the Lone Star nine a game and a half adrift

The fourth inning held the whole argument in miniature. Christian Walker had just singled the Houston men back in front, 4 to 3, with Yordan Alvarez pulling into third, when a delivery from the Texas battery skipped loose in the dirt — and Alvarez, five-and-thirty home runs and two-and-eighty runs batted in to his name, the game's foremost artillerist, came pelting down the line like a man half his size. He beat the throw. That run, the fifth of Houston's evening, proved in the fullness of time to be the verdict itself, and the wires this morning are adding baserunning to the man's case for the Most Valuable laurels.

Texas had made a proper contest of it. Wyatt Langford's three-and-tenth circuit squared an early deficit, Elias Díaz laid down a squeeze bunt in the fourth that knotted matters at three, and when the same Díaz hammered his fifth home run in the sixth the Lone Star nine stood within a single run, where they remained. Jacob deGrom, who worked the middle of it, was charged with the loss and stands at seven victories and seven defeats; Okert took the win in relief, and Hader barred the ninth for his fifteenth save.

The ledger tells the larger tale. Houston has now won five running and nine of its last ten — this the fourth straight taken from Arlington's men in the season's late meetings — and the West, which changed hands but two evenings ago, now shows the Astros a game and a half clear. Texas, losers of four straight, has fallen to a game and a half back with the Mariners lurking three behind, and the schedule offers no mercy: the same two clubs meet again this very afternoon, Rocker opposite Lambert, at ten past two.

News Around the League

THE THUNDERCLAP FROM THE COAST: SKUBAL IS TRADED TO THE CHAMPIONS, AND THE BAZAAR MAY NEVER BE THE SAME

The pot that boiled all week has at last boiled over, and in the grandest manner imaginable: the wires report the Dodgers have finalized the acquisition of Tarik Skubal, the left-handed lion of Detroit and winner of the American League's Cy Young award in each of the two seasons past, sending three highly regarded prospects to the Tigers in exchange. The champions of the last two Octobers, already owners of a ten-game cushion in the National's West, thus add the finest arm on the market to a stable that includes Yamamoto — the scribes are already counting the aces on one hand and finding they need most of it.

The leave-taking was not a dry-eyed affair. Skubal, nine seasons a Tiger and nine-and-twenty years of age, fought back tears before the assembled press on Saturday night and called his Detroit years something he would never take for granted. The Herald notes the day's small ironies: the very evening the news broke, Skubal's new employers were beaten by Boston (see the card, below), and his old mates in Detroit won their game and cleared the benches doing it. The deadline itself tolls Monday at six o'clock.

THE SATURDAY COMMERCE, ITEMIZED: CASTILLO TO THE SOUTH SIDE, DOVAL TO PITTSBURGH, THOMAS TO ATLANTA, NAYLOR TO MILWAUKEE

The Skubal thunderclap rather drowned the day's other dealings, which were considerable. The White Sox, first in the American's Central and evidently meaning to stay there, secured the veteran right-hander Luis Castillo from Seattle in exchange for Seranthony Domínguez, Nolan Jones, and the prospect Boston Smith. The Pirates, chasing an end to a postseason drought now eleven years long, obtained the reliever Camilo Doval from the Yankees for two prospects. Atlanta added the outfielder Lane Thomas and the left-hander Bailey Falter from Kansas City for a pair of minor-league arms, and the Brewers — owners, as of this morning, of the game's best ledger — brought in the catcher Bo Naylor from Cleveland in a four-player exchange.

Nor is the market spent: the wires hold that Baltimore may yet entertain offers upon the catcher Adley Rutschman with half the contending world short of catching, and Seattle's George Kirby is slated to make what may be a farewell start as the offers come in. Two days of shopping remain.

THE INFIRMARY WARD AND THE ROAD BACK: CONTRERAS'S EXAMINATION, CORREA'S TIMETABLE, ARRAEZ'S CRAMPS

Boston held Willson Contreras from Saturday's order at Los Angeles while the physicians completed the concussion examinations occasioned by Friday's fastball to the helmet; the club expects him restored to the lineup this very day, which would be swift work indeed. In Houston there is brighter news still: Carlos Correa's recovery from the surgery upon his torn ankle tendon runs ahead of the announced schedule, and the man himself declines to rule out a return before the season's close. And in San Francisco, Luis Arráez — batting .328, the National's best figure, and the subject of no little deadline speculation — was scratched minutes before the evening's engagement with cramping, the club calling it a precaution. His understudy's evening is chronicled below, and it wants reading.

A NEW LEAGUE TAKES THE FIELD: THE WOMEN'S PROFESSIONAL GAME MAKES ITS DEBUT, AND THE QUEENS OF LOS ANGELES TAKE THE OPENER

The Herald sets down a date for the almanacs: the Women's Professional Baseball League played its inaugural contest this weekend, and the Los Angeles Queens claimed the first victory in the young circuit's ledger. The Herald, which holds that the game belongs to all who love it, tips its cap to the new enterprise and wishes it a long run of full grandstands.

THE ALMANAC: CORNFIELDS IN AUGUST, WILLIAMSPORT BEYOND, AND FIVE-AND-FIFTY DAYS OF REGULAR COMMERCE REMAINING

The forward calendar, consulted as ever at the Herald's desk: the Field of Dreams engagement comes the thirteenth of August, the Twins and the Phillies amid the Iowa corn at Dyersville; the Little League Classic follows the three-and-twentieth, Brewers against Braves at Williamsport; and the regular season's final day falls upon the seven-and-twentieth of September. Between now and all of that stands Monday's deadline bell, at six o'clock.

The Rest of the Card

GAUSMAN DEPARTS TO AN OVATION, AND PERHAPS TO PARTS UNKNOWN: BLUE JAYS 5, CARDINALS 1

Kevin Gausman scattered nine Redbird hits across five and two-thirds frames, permitted but a single run, and walked off to a standing ovation from one-and-forty thousand at Toronto — the wires observing it may have been his final start in Canadian flannels before Monday's bell. George Springer singled home one run and doubled home another, Alejandro Kirk's two-run single broke the seventh open, and the rookie Mathews absorbed the defeat in his first decision. The Torontos have won three straight; St. Louis, whose lone tally rode Masyn Winn's double, has dropped three running and sinks further below the even mark.

ONE RUN, TEN HITS, AND NOTHING WASTED: THE GULF COAST CLUB SHADES THE PALE HOSE, 1 TO 0, IN THE BATTLE OF FIRST CHAIRS

The American's two division leaders produced a Saturday of splendid parsimony: five hits apiece, and the game's only run crossing in the fifth, when Nick Fortes lined a single to left and Jonny DeLuca came home. Rasmussen claimed his tenth victory, Baker his one-and-thirtieth save — the American's best figure — and Chicago's Schultz was hard done by in defeat. Tampa Bay thus answers Friday's four-circuit indignity, holds its Eastern cushion at three and a half over New York, and keeps the season's finest home ledger; the South Siders' Central lead stands at three.

SANOJA, CONINE, AND THE STEADY HICKS: THE GULF STREAM NINE TAKE FLUSHING AGAIN, 6 TO 2

Javier Sanoja's two-run circuit in the second and Griffin Conine's tenth in the sixth supplied the thunder, and Liam Hicks singled home a run in the first and another in the sixth for good measure. Tyrone Taylor's two-run answer, his tenth, was the whole of the Amazins' account. Phillips took the win, Bachar his second save, and Miami — winners of two straight, at seven-and-fifty victories — presses within a game and a half of the National's last wild-card chair whilst the Mets play out the string and mind Monday's market.

RALEIGH'S HUMBLE ROLLER SETTLES IT IN THE NINTH: MARINERS 4, TWINS 3, AT THE LAST STROKE

Seattle built a three-run start — Julio Rodríguez and Mitch Garver forcing runs across before Randy Arozarena's fourteenth circuit — and then watched the Minnesotans claw it all back, Keaschall and Larnach homering and Caratini's groundout squaring the account in the seventh. The ninth brought the plainest of endings: Arozarena aboard, and Cal Raleigh rolling a ground ball to first that could not be turned quick enough — the winning run crossing as the grandstand rose. Muñoz took the victory. The Puget Sound men thereby halt a three-game slide; Minnesota slips back to the even mark, level with Cleveland at the wild-card table's edge.

THREE RUNS ON TWO WALKS AND A GROUNDER, AND ASHCRAFT DOES THE REST: CORSAIRS 4, REDLEGS 1

Pittsburgh's third inning was a study in patience: Bryan Reynolds and Esmerlyn Valdez walked with the sacks full and Nick Gonzales's fielder's choice made it three, never a base hit among the lot. Ashcraft ran his account to eleven victories against four defeats, Soto sealed his fourteenth save, and Jose Trevino's solo circuit was Cincinnati's entire reply. Henry Davis singled home a fourth run in the sixth. The Corsairs, back to the even mark at six-and-fifty wins, sit two and a half from the wild-card table; the Redlegs get Burns, twelve victories against a single defeat, for this afternoon's answer.

SÁNCHEZ HEARS THE CHATTER AND ANSWERS WITH ELEVEN STRIKEOUTS: PHILS 5, BALTIMORES 0

Cristopher Sánchez, whom the rumor mill had lately fitted for an Oriole uniform, took the hill at Baltimore and delivered the argument personally: eleven strikeouts in a scoreless start — a wild one, the wires allow, but never a run of it — and his four-and-tenth victory, the National's best count. Bryan De La Cruz singled home the first run and the eighth inning supplied four more, Justin Crawford's two-run single the largest stroke. Baz bore the defeat. Philadelphia thus arrests its two-and-eight skid at a stroke and clings to the National's final wild-card chair; the Birds' brief revival is checked.

THE SONORAN NINE EMPTY THE ARSENAL: DIAMONDBACKS 12, GUARDIANS 8, AND CLEVELAND'S SLIDE FINDS NO FLOOR

A seven-run seventh settled this carnival of base hits: Perdomo's ninth circuit, McCann's two-run double, and Corbin Carroll's three-run blast, his sixteenth, all in the one frame, with Nolan Arenado's fifteenth following in the eighth for garnish. Perdomo drove in four in all. Rhys Hoskins and Chase DeLauter homered for the Forest City men, who twice drew near and twice were repulsed. Carrillo took the win. Arizona has won four straight and grips the National's second wild-card chair a game clear; Cleveland, two victories in its last ten, falls level with Minnesota in the American's scramble.

CROW-ARMSTRONG RUNS WILD AND THE NORTH SIDERS TAKE THE RUBBER TEST: CUBS 5, PINSTRIPES 2

Pete Crow-Armstrong scored on a wild pitch in the first, tripled home the go-ahead run in a three-run seventh, and came home himself directly after on Suzuki's single — a one-man insurgency that decided the meeting of these well-matched nines. Nico Hoerner's sixth circuit had squared the account after Spencer Jones, the young Yankee, struck his fifth. Thornton won it and Thielbar closed the door. Chicago, at three-and-sixty victories, holds the National's first wild-card chair and takes the series two games to one; New York's Eastern deficit stretches to three and a half.

PÉREZ, SUMMONED ON SHORT NOTICE, FLIRTS WITH THE IMMORTALS: TOMAHAWK NINE 8, CAPITAL NINE 3

Pressed into the start on abbreviated rest, Pérez carried a no-hit essay deep into the Georgia evening — the wires made much of it — and Washington's whole account, four hits and three runs, was crowded into a single eighth-inning flurry. By then the issue was long past dispute: Ronald Acuña Jr. and Matt Olson opened the first with back-to-back circuits, Olson's the round thirtieth of his season, Dominic Smith added his eighth, and a five-run seventh crowned by Austin Riley's two-run double made it eight. Atlanta has won four straight and rules the East by eight games entire; Mikolas took the loss for the Senators, whose late knocks came from Crews and Abrams.

GOODMAN THUNDERS TWICE AND DRAWS LEVEL WITH SCHWARBER AT THREE-AND-THIRTY: ROCKIES 12, ROYALS 6

Hunter Goodman struck his two-and-thirtieth circuit in the third, his three-and-thirtieth in the seventh, and singled home a run besides — five runs batted in on the day, and a share of the National's home-run crown alongside Philadelphia's Schwarber, with only Alvarez's five-and-thirty above the pair in all the game. The Coloradans piled up twelve runs in the thin air: Kyle Karros's twentieth double began the counter-attack, TJ Rumfield singled home three runs across the evening, and Willi Castro added his twelfth homer. Jac Caglianone's eighteenth and Salvador Perez's sixteenth had staked the Missourians to an early four-run lead that lasted precisely half an inning. Feltner took the win; the same clubs conclude this afternoon.

THE UNDERSTUDY DRIVES IN FOUR, BUT CRONENWORTH HAS THE FINAL SYLLABLE: MISSION NINE 6, ORANGE AND BLACK 5

With Arráez scratched at the eleventh hour, the journeyman Osleivis Basabe stepped into the breach and delivered the game of his life — the first home run of his career, a two-run stroke in the third, and a two-run double in the eighth that squared the account at five. San Diego required a last word, and Jake Cronenworth supplied it in the ninth, a single that sent Bogaerts home with the verdict. Freddy Fermin had homered and driven in three for the Friars; Merrill's two-run double opened the scoring. Miller took the win in relief. The Mission nine, winners of seven of ten, draw within a single game of the wild-card table; the Bay men's brave evening goes unrewarded.

RAFAELA TWICE FINDS THE LEFT-CENTER SEATS, AND THE CRIMSON HOSE TAKE A SECOND FROM THE CHAMPIONS: RED SOX 3, DODGERS 2

Ceddanne Rafaela struck solo circuits in the first and third, his thirteenth and fourteenth, and the deciding run of the eighth arrived by the humblest conveyance in the game's law — Connor Wong banging into a double play whilst Monasterio crossed from third. Teoscar Hernández's two-run answer was the whole of Los Angeles's account, and Yamamoto, on the very evening his new rotation-mate was secured (see the news, above), fell to eleven-and-seven. Chapman sealed his seven-and-twentieth save. Boston has won four straight, ten of its last dozen, and holds the American's second wild-card chair; the Boys in Blue have dropped two running, and with Milwaukee's victory (below) surrender the game's best ledger besides.

BAUERS AGAIN, AND THE SUDS MEN INHERIT THE GAME'S BEST LEDGER: BREWERS 3, ANAHEIM NINE 1

Jake Bauers, who homered Friday, homered Saturday — his twentieth, squaring the account in the fourth after Soler's early double had put the Halos ahead — and Milwaukee's late singles settled it, Turang's in the eighth for the lead and young Cooper Pratt's in the ninth for margin. Patrick took his sixth win and Megill his eighteenth save. At nine-and-sixty victories against one-and-forty, the Brewers this morning own the finest record in all the game — a half-length past the Dodgers — with their Central lead at six and a half; Anaheim has now lost five straight.

CLARK'S FIRST CIRCUIT, TORRES'S THUNDER, AND A WARM WORD AT THE END: BENGALS 8, MACKMEN 6

Max Clark, three hits in Friday's debut, went one better on Saturday: his first major-league home run, a two-run stroke in the sixth, with Kevin McGonigle opening the game with his eleventh and Gleyber Torres adding a two-run blow in the fifth. The White Elephant nine made a late charge on Henry Bolte's three-run homer in the eighth, and matters ended warmly indeed — the benches emptying over a ninth-inning bunt single, though the wires record more milling than mayhem. Valdez took the win, Jansen his thirteenth save. Detroit, its farewell to Skubal notwithstanding (see the news, above), has won two straight behind its splendid youngsters.

This Sunday's card runs the full fifteen, and the connoisseur is spoiled for choice: Wheeler, ten victories against two, takes the hill for Philadelphia at Baltimore at five-and-thirty past one; Burns, twelve-and-one, receives the Corsairs at Cincinnati at twenty of two; the Yankees and Cubs conclude at Wrigley at twenty past two, Cole opposite Rea; Misiorowski, whose 1.58 figure leads the National, appears at Anaheim at a quarter past three; Houston and Texas renew their quarrel at ten past two; and Boston seeks the sweep of the champions at Chavez Ravine by evening light, twenty past seven. Beyond it all stands Monday's deadline bell at six o'clock, and the Herald counsels a hearty breakfast — the wires will not slow between now and then. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
