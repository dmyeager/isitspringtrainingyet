THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 38 · Saturday, the Fifteenth of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 14 contests from the day prior.

Is it spring training yet? No — but the championship season is upon us, a finer thing still.

The Game of the Day

TWICE DOWN TO THE FINAL STRIKE, HOUSTON RISES FIVE RUNS AT A BLOW: WALKER'S WALK-OFF THUNDERBOLT IN THE TENTH — ASTROS 10, MARINERS 7

Seattle scores twice in the tenth to seize the lead, whereupon the Houston nine — twice a single strike from defeat against the estimable Muñoz — answers with five runs in the bottom of the frame, Paredes and Varsho supplying the levelers and Christian Walker the three-run blow that empties the dugout, fires the rockets, and stretches the West lead to a season-high three games

Let the reader be advised that the tenth inning at Daikin Park produced seven runs, two lead changes, and one baseball struck so emphatically by Christian Walker that the outcome was never in doubt from the crack of it. The mariners of the Sound had done everything demanded of a visiting club: two runs in the first upon Canzone's single and Naylor's sacrifice fly, a patient recovery after Houston batted round its early deficit, single runs in the sixth and a pair in the eighth to draw level at five, and then two more in the tenth — Robles tripling home one and Rodden lofting home another — to stand three outs from a virtuous road victory with Andrés Muñoz, among the finest closers in the game, upon the hill.

What followed will be recounted in Houston for some while. Twice the count reached its final strike; twice the Astros declined the invitation. Paredes singled home a run — his third driven in of the night — and Varsho singled home the leveler, and then Walker, granted a pitch to his liking, dispatched his three-and-twentieth home run of the season far into the left-field seats with two aboard. Ten runs to seven; fireworks overhead; the entire dugout emptied at the plate. 'To be able to tie it and then force more baseball, and then obviously take the lead, is great,' Walker submitted afterward, with the becoming modesty of a man who has just detonated a ballgame. The earlier chapters ought not be forgotten: Yordan Alvarez opened Houston's account with his six-and-thirtieth home run, the American League's largest crop and a single blow shy of Schwarber's major-league lead, before the hosts stacked four more in the second upon Cam Smith's triple and singles by Peña and Paredes.

The ledger renders the drama consequential: Houston, at three-and-sixty wins, extends its hold upon the American's West to three full games — its most comfortable of the season — over an Arlington club that has now dropped four straight, while Seattle departs six games adrift with but two victories in its last ten. The decision fell to De Los Santos; the defeat, and a seventh spilled save, to the unfortunate Muñoz.

News Around the League

THE MAPLE LEAF NINE HOLD THEIR BREATH: GUERRERO IN THE CONCUSSION PROTOCOL

The gravest bulletin of the evening issued from Toronto, where Vladimir Guerrero Jr. was removed from the Jays' conquest of New York after a chaotic passage at third base in the sixth inning. Racing from first upon an errant throw, Guerrero collided with the Yankee shortstop George Lombard Jr. — the young man's knee meeting the left side of Guerrero's face — and though he rose and completed his journey home with the tying run, he departed the game soon after and now undergoes the full concussion examination. 'You've always got to be careful,' said skipper John Schneider, a man acquainted with such injuries from his own catching days. 'We'll see how he wakes up tomorrow.' The timing is cruel: the Jays have lately been the hottest article in the American's wild-card scrum, and stand now but a single game from the final chair with eight-and-thirty to play. The whole of Ontario awaits the morning's report.

A FIRST SAVE AT ONE HUNDRED MILES AN HOUR, AND THE FRIARS MAKE IT SIX RUNNING

The night's warmest small story belongs to Bradgley Rodriguez, two-and-twenty years of age, who was handed the ninth inning of the Friars' engagement at Cleveland and converted it into the first save of his major-league life — surrendering a leadoff double, then retiring the side with two strikeouts, the last upon a called heater registered at one hundred and eight-tenths miles per hour, the swiftest pitch of the game. 'I'm just very emotional about getting that first save,' the young man confessed. 'The first of many, hopefully.' The established closer Mason Miller, who shares the National's lead at thirty saves, pronounced his understudy 'one of the best relievers in baseball' and professed himself excited in the present tense. The victory, chronicled upon the card below, was San Diego's sixth in succession.

FOUR-AND-THIRTY HOMERS IN MEMPHIS, AND NOW THE BARREL COMES TO THE SHOW

The Mound City club has summoned its No. 3 prospect, the outfielder Joshua Báez, who is expected to make his major-league debut this very day at Wrigley Field. The credentials are considerable: four-and-thirty home runs for Memphis, the largest total in all of Triple-A this season, alongside a .573 slugging figure across one hundred and three games. The wires rank him the nine-and-fiftieth prospect in the game, and St. Louis — whose attack has lately produced three hits in an afternoon, as the card below attests — will not be reviewed harshly for hastening the experiment.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: OHTANI ASCENDS A MOUND, MARTE DESCENDS TO THE TRAINER'S TABLE

The most encouraging admission first: Shohei Ohtani threw four-and-thirty pitches from a bullpen mound before the evening's engagement at Dodger Stadium — his first such session since the July setback that halted his throwing — and employed his full assortment. The stated plan calls for another session or two, then live hitters at least twice, before he pitches in a game; the management contemplates deploying him as a multi-inning opener once he returns. The catchers Will Smith and Dalton Rushing likewise took batting practice upon the field for the first time since their injuries. The debit column: Arizona's Ketel Marte departed the victory at Atlanta in the second inning with soreness of the left knee — an ailment he has been gutting out for some days — and is listed day to day. The Snakes will hold their breath; the man's importance to their wild-card position wants no elaboration.

THE ALMANAC DESK: THE CORN WILL PART AGAIN, AND THE MIDSUMMER CLASSIC GOES TO THE BAY

Three entries for the long calendar. First: scarcely had the Dyersville corn swallowed its last baseball when the league announced the pageant shall return — the Royals and the Red Sox will contest the next MLB at Field of Dreams game on the twelfth of August, 2027. Second: the Giants have been awarded the All-Star Game of 2028, returning the Midsummer Classic to San Francisco for the fourth time and the first since 2007; the 2027 edition remains bound for Wrigley Field. Third, and nearer at hand: the almanac notes the Little League Classic at Williamsport on the three-and-twentieth of this month, Brewers against Braves, a fixture the Herald commends to any reader who enjoys watching millionaires made bashful by twelve-year-olds.

The Rest of the Card

YAMAMOTO DEALS, DÍAZ ATONES, AND THE GAP IS ONE: BOYS IN BLUE 3, BREW CREW 1

The rematch the Herald commended to its readers repaid the vigil. Yoshinobu Yamamoto, made to labor through eight-and-thirty pitches across the first two innings, reorganized his mind — his own account of it — and finished six innings of one-run ball with nine strikeouts, the Milwaukee reply amounting to a solitary sacrifice fly. Betts singled home the game's first run, and Pages and Edman supplied solo homers, the former's twentieth. Then came the ninth and Edwin Díaz, the day after the management renewed its faith in him: two walks, a strikeout, and — at last — three outs, his seventh save and his first since the recent spillage. 'At the end of the day, I made my pitches,' said the closer, plainly relieved. Los Angeles stands one game behind Milwaukee for the National's finest ledger, and the clubs meet again this day.

BURNS RETURNS TO HIS ROOTS AND LOCKS THE DOOR: QUEEN CITY NINE 1, FLORIDIANS 0

The pitching bill the desk praised in advance delivered in full. Young Chase Burns, chastened by his roughest outing a week prior, permitted Miami four singles across seven scoreless innings, striking out eight without a walk — 'just getting back to my roots,' he explained, 'throw it down the middle and let it eat.' His opponent Sandy Alcantara matched him frame for frame, three hits and one run across seven, the run arriving upon Eugenio Suárez's double in the sixth. Burke struck out the side in the eighth and Pagán closed for his fifteenth save. Burns stands four-and-ten against two with an earned-run figure of 2.47, third in the National, and Cincinnati has won two running.

HENDERSON RAPS FOUR AND THE STREAK DIES AT NINE: BIRDS 6, SUNSHINE NINE 5

Gunnar Henderson, whose season has been a long argument with his own reflection, went four-for-four at Tropicana Field — two singles, a double, and a two-run homer in the eighth, his twentieth — and Baltimore snapped Tampa Bay's winning streak at nine. Cowser added his tenth homer and Beavers a two-run single. The hosts made a proper fright of the ninth, three runs arriving upon Aranda's two-run single and Caminero's groundout, before Sanders recorded the final out with the tying run aboard. The Rays remain the American's class at four-and-seventy wins; Henderson, by his own telling, is simply playing 'with my hair on fire' again, and the Birds sit a single game from the final wild-card chair.

HOLMES IS PERFECT THROUGH SIXTEEN MEN IN HIS WRIGLEY UNVEILING: BRUINS 3, REDBIRDS 0

Clay Holmes, lately of both New York establishments, introduced himself to the Wrigley faithful by retiring the first sixteen St. Louis batters in order, the sinker boring holes in the afternoon until Fermín's soft single in the sixth dissolved the dream of history. He settled for six and two-thirds innings of one-hit ball, whereupon Seiya Suzuki supplied the entire offense with a three-run blast, his one-and-twentieth. Webb sealed the whitewash for his ninth save. The Bruins have won eight of ten, sit three behind Milwaukee in the Central, and hold the National's first wild-card chair by seven full games.

FOUR HOMERS ON THE ROAD, TWO FROM VARGAS: SOUTH SIDERS 9, MOTOR CITY NINE 5

The opening engagement of a week that may decide the American's Central went emphatically to Chicago. Miguel Vargas homered twice — his six-and-twentieth and seven-and-twentieth, drawing him level with his own teammate Murakami, who also cleared the wall — and Peters added a two-run shot, while Braden Montgomery drove in three upon a single and a triple. The evening was unkind to young Jobe, scored upon at last in his return and charged with six runs before the fourth was done; Urquidy, in his White Sox debut, collected the victory in relief. 'We know that this week means a lot for us,' said Vargas, and the ledger agrees: the lead over Detroit stands at three and a half.

COLE IS BETRAYED BY THE LEATHER, AND KIRK COLLECTS THE SPOILS: JAYS 3, PINSTRIPES 1

Gerrit Cole, facing Toronto for the first time in two years, delivered five spotless innings and was undone in the sixth by his own infantry: McMahon's low throw and a second error by García upon the same play let the tying run cross — the play upon which Guerrero was injured, as the news column relates — and Kirk promptly doubled home the lead before homering in the eighth for good measure. Bieber took the decision with six innings of one-run ball and Varland closed for his seven-and-twentieth save. Grisham's sixteenth homer was the whole of the New York account; the Pinstripes have dropped two running and remain six and a half behind the Rays.

LINDOR STIRS THE SLEEPING METROPOLITANS: METS 4, SENATORS 1

Washington led at Flushing upon House's single through the middle innings until Francisco Lindor, in the sixth, deposited his twelfth homer and woke the premises. Benge singled home the lead, and Young's eighth homer and Bichette's single fattened it in the seventh. Pintaro collected the first victory of his major-league life with two clean innings of relief, and Senga closed for his second save. The Metropolitans, whatever their station in the ledger, have won seven of ten; the Senators have surrendered the season's series.

PFAADT REPEATS HIMSELF, SEVEN BLANK FRAMES AND ALL: SNAKES 2, ATLANTAS 0

For the second consecutive outing Brandon Pfaadt delivered precisely seven scoreless innings upon two hits — this time with seven strikeouts and not a walk — and for the second consecutive outing his ledger brightened, now seven victories against a single defeat. His opposite Chris Sale struck out nine across six innings of one-run ball and was beaten regardless, the run scoring upon Moreno's groundout in the first; Tawa added a ninth-inning homer for insurance. Garcia closed for his fourth save. Arizona thus claims the opener at Atlanta and holds its wild-card seat, though the club departed the park anxious over Marte's knee, as the infirmary column relates.

THE BUCCANEERS BATTER THE CRIMSON HOSE: BUCS 8, BOSTONS 4

Pittsburgh built its victory upon the long ball and the timely double: Rafael Flores Jr. opened the account with his fourth homer and later singled home another, Valdez doubled home two, and Brandon Lowe's three-run blast in the fifth — his six-and-twentieth — settled the argument at seven-two. Young Chandler took the decision with five innings of two-run ball, and Montgomery recorded the final out for his fourth save after the Bostons crept within four. Duran scored twice in the losing cause. The Bucs have won two running; Boston, at five-and-sixty wins, keeps the second wild-card chair but has dropped six of ten.

CAMPUSANO DOUBLES TWICE AND THE FRIARS' STREAK REACHES SIX: MISSION NINE 7, FOREST CITY NINE 5

Cleveland struck first and often — Nathaniel Lowe homering and singling home another, Rocchio clearing the wall — but the Friars answered with five unanswered upon Bogaerts' two-run homer, Tatis' thirteenth, and Campusano's two-run double, the catcher adding a second double and Bogaerts a sacrifice fly in the seventh. King ground through six innings for the victory over Williams, and the rookie Rodriguez closed matters, as the news column celebrates. San Diego, winners of six straight, stand eight behind Los Angeles in the West but hold the National's second wild-card chair; the Cleveland nine have lost eight of ten.

CAGLIANONE DRIVES IN FIVE AND TROUT IS CUT DOWN AT THE PLATE: ROYAL BLUES 7, SERAPHS 6

A proper slugging match in Anaheim. Jac Caglianone drove in five runs by three separate instruments — a two-run single, a two-run homer, his two-and-twentieth, and the seventh-inning single that drew Kansas City level — and Massey, who had opened the scoring with a homer, singled home the decisive run moments later. The Seraphs' case was argued by Schanuel, whose bases-clearing double and later single produced four runs, and Josh Lowe, whose ninth homer briefly restored the lead; it was undone in part by Rave's right arm, which cut down Mike Trout at the plate upon the tying single in the fifth. Cruz closed for his fourth save.

MCNEIL FALLS A TRIPLE SHY OF THE CYCLE AS THE ELEPHANTS PLAY SPOILER: WHITE ELEPHANTS 8, TEXAS NINE 3

The Athletics, whose remaining schedule is thick with contending neighbors, have announced their intention to make mischief, and Jeff McNeil led the mischief-making: a double, a single, a solo homer, and in the ninth a two-run drive down the right-field line that hopped into the seats — a double when he wanted a triple, leaving the cycle a base short. Cortes had opened matters with a bases-clearing double against the wild Rocker, and after Burger's three-run homer drew Texas level, Butler and the deep men restored order. The defeat, Texas's fourth running, drops the Arlington men three behind Houston and half a game from the final wild-card chair.

FREELAND THROWS EIGHT AND THE MOUNTAIN MEN MAKE IT THREE: ROCKIES 5, JINTS 2

In the season's late-summer curiosities division: Colorado has won three running, the latest upon Kyle Freeland's eight innings of four-hit, one-run craftsmanship by the Bay. Johnston tripled home the first run, Veen cleared the wall with a man aboard, and the Jints' reply was confined to Eldridge's thirteenth homer and Devers' ninth-inning double, his eight-and-twentieth two-bagger of the year. Roupp absorbed his twelfth defeat. The two westernmost clubs in the National's cellar meet again this afternoon, bragging rights and draft arithmetic both in play.

The arithmetic desk directs the reader's attention to the American's final wild-card chair, presently occupied by Detroit at three score wins — with the Twins and Rangers half a game behind, the Jays and Birds a full game, and the Cleveland nine a game and a half: six claimants within a game and a half of one seat, and six weeks to sort them. Tonight's card runs to fifteen engagements, and the bill of fare is rich: at Dodger Stadium, a quarter past seven Eastern, the Brewers hand the ball to Misiorowski — proprietor of the major leagues' finest earned-run figure at 1.76 and two hundred four strikeouts besides — opposite Wrobleski, with the National's best record separated by a single game; the young man's stated plan for the great Ohtani is quoted upon the wires in full: 'I'm going to throw a heater. Good luck. Here it is.' Elsewhere, Schlittler and his American-best 2.21 takes the hill at Toronto at seven minutes past three, and Gray, the American's lone fourteen-game winner, pitches for Boston at Pittsburgh. Until the next dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
