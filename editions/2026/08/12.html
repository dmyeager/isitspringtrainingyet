THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 35 · Wednesday, the Twelfth of August, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

Is it spring training yet? No — but the championship season is upon us, a finer thing still.

The Game of the Day

SEVENTEEN HITS, ONE GRAND SALVO, AND A LOGJAM AT THE WILD-CARD TABLE: FRIARS 11, BREWERS 2

San Diego falls upon the owners of the game's best ledger with five runs before the second inning is old — Rengifo's bases-full blow the cannonade, Campusano three times a tormentor, and Tatis Jr. ascending to second place in the whole history of Friar slugging — whereby three clubs now sit level as a billiard table, contesting two October chairs

The Cream City nine arrived in San Diego owning baseball's handsomest account, four-and-seventy wins against six-and-forty defeats, and were sent to bed having absorbed an eleven-to-two thrashing — their second defeat running, and the Friars' fourth consecutive victory. The decisive business was transacted early: in the second inning Jake Cronenworth doubled home a run, and Luis Rengifo, a gentleman carrying but two home runs on the season's ledger, selected that moment for his third — a grand slam to left field, four runs across on a single swing, and a five-nought advantage before the visitors had rightly settled into their seats.

The procession scarcely slackened thereafter. Fernando Tatis Jr. struck his twelfth circuit in the fourth — a blow of historical freight, for it carried him past every Friar save one and installed him second upon the club's all-time home-run roll, behind only Manny Machado, his teammate still, who marked the occasion with three hits of his own. Luis Campusano went three-for-four with a homer and three runs driven in, Jackson Merrill doubled home another, and by the close the hosts had amassed seventeen hits while Walker Buehler — six innings, one run — pocketed the victory. The unfortunate Harrison, nine-and-three on the year, was charged with eight runs and did not survive the fifth.

The arithmetic is where the evening turns consequential. At four-and-sixty wins against seven-and-fifty defeats, San Diego now stands precisely level with Arizona and Philadelphia — three clubs at .529, crowding two wild-card chairs, with the Cubs comfortably above the scrum. Milwaukee's lead in the National's Central remains a plump four games, but the Friars have now taken consecutive series from first-place opposition, and they play like men who have read the schedule.

News Around the League

THE CROWN OF CIRCUITS: FOUR CLAIMANTS AT FIVE-AND-THIRTY, THE CLOSEST RACE IN FORTY YEARS

The wires devote their lead column to a spectacle not witnessed in four decades: a home-run race packed four deep. Junior Caminero of Tampa Bay and Matt Olson of Atlanta each struck his five-and-thirtieth last evening, drawing level with Houston's Yordan Alvarez — resident at that figure since late July — and Philadelphia's Kyle Schwarber. Four men, five-and-thirty circuits apiece, and Colorado's Hunter Goodman lurking a single blow behind at four-and-thirty. The connoisseur is advised to keep the almanac handy through September; the crown may be decided by a single evening's swing.

THE CATCHER PRIZE UNVEILED: RUTSCHMAN TAKES HIS BOW IN BOSTON FLANNELS

The debut these pages heralded yesterday is now in the books: Adley Rutschman, Boston's great prize of the Trade Deadline, was formally activated and caught his first game in Crimson employ at Toronto, collecting a single hit and drawing two walks in the five-to-three defeat chronicled upon the card below. The reinforcement could scarcely be more wanted — the Hub nine have now dropped four running — and the veteran Jake Rogers was designated for assignment to clear the perch. One debut does not arrest a slide, but Boston's followers will take the omen over the alternative.

THE INFIRMARY WARD: GREENE AND TREINEN CONSIGNED TO THE LONG LIST, TAILLON'S FEARS CONFIRMED

A somber morning's ledger for the medical men. Cincinnati has transferred Hunter Greene to the sixty-day list with soreness of the right elbow, and Los Angeles has done likewise with the reliever Blake Treinen, his complaint an inflammation of the same joint. In Toronto the examiners returned their verdict on Jameson Taillon, whose early exit from his Blue Jays debut these pages reported yesterday: tendinitis of the right forearm, and a fifteen-day sentence. Miami, for its part, has shelved Kyle Stowers with a strained left hamstring.

The brighter beds: Tampa Bay has dispatched Shane McClanahan on a rehabilitation errand to Durham, Pittsburgh has sent Oneil Cruz to Altoona on the same business, and Kansas City's Vinnie Pasquantino is mending at Omaha.

THE SHUTTLE DESK: SUMMONED AT NOON, PRODUCING BY NIGHTFALL

The day's roster traffic repaid its paperwork with unusual promptness. Texas selected Jarred Kelenic from Round Rock, and by evening he had singled home a run in Anaheim; Cincinnati summoned the first baseman Michael Toglia from Louisville, and within hours he stood in the middle of the eighth-inning uprising that turned the Chicago engagement; and Toronto recalled the reliever Chase Lee from Buffalo, who required but a few innings to collect his first victory of the year against Boston. Colorado, tidying its own shelves, recalled the outfielder Zac Veen and designated Edouard Julien for assignment. The express between the high minors and the show, the Herald observes, has seldom run so profitably on time.

THE ALMANAC: THE CORN IS OCCUPIED ALL WEEK AT DYERSVILLE

The Iowa corn has itself a full engagement book. The minor leaguers christened the Field of Dreams stage Tuesday evening — the wires pronounce the occasion properly magical, nostalgia hanging in the air alongside the humidity — and this very afternoon, at two o'clock Eastern, the High School All-American Game takes the same diamond, the first time the prospect showcase has been staged at Dyersville. The main attraction arrives upon the morrow: the Twins and Phillies contest the Field of Dreams game proper on Thursday evening. The regular campaign's final day, the almanac desk reminds the record, remains the twenty-seventh of September.

The Rest of the Card

THE PRODIGY McGONIGLE DRIVES IN THREE AND THE BENGALS KEEP ROARING: BENGALS 6, LAKE ERIE MEN 4

Young Kevin McGonigle, batting at a cool .288, delivered a two-run double in a three-run fifth and singled home another in the seventh, and Detroit collected its third straight victory — eight of its last ten — despite mustering but six hits. Chase DeLauter and Jo Adell homered in the Cleveland cause and Bibee absorbed his twelfth defeat; Jansen sealed the fourteenth save. The Bengals now breathe two and a half games behind the Pale Hose in the American's Central, while the Lake Erie men have dropped eight of ten and are running short of season.

EURY THE MAGNIFICENT OUTDUELS SKENES HIMSELF: FISH 2, CORSAIRS 0

Eury Pérez, who aspires aloud to the game's first rank of pitchers, presented his credentials in the most direct fashion: seven scoreless innings, three hits, seven strikeouts, with Paul Skenes — no small examiner — in the opposite corner. Esteury Ruiz homered in the third — his sixth — then stole his eight-and-twentieth base and came round on Edwards' single for the second tally, and Fairbanks notarized the eighteenth save. The Fish have won three running and sit two games above level water, hunting the wild-card scrum from two and a half back.

THE ONE-TWO PUNCH LANDS AGAIN: NORTH SIDERS 8, CAPITAL NINE 6

Pete Crow-Armstrong struck his seven-and-twentieth circuit and Seiya Suzuki, batting in his shadow, added his twentieth alongside a run-scoring double, while Alex Bregman crossed three times and homered besides. Washington answered gamely — Abrams' nine-and-twentieth brought his league-leading total to one-and-ninety runs driven in, and Crews and Young also cleared the wall — but the arithmetic favored Chicago, eight victories now in its last ten and holding the National's first wild-card chair by a comfortable six and a half games. Rolison improved to seven-and-one; Webb sealed the eighth save.

FOUR IN THE SEVENTH AND THE BOMBERS ESCAPE AGAIN: BRONX BOMBERS 4, PUGET SOUND MEN 1

For six innings the evening belonged to the pitchers, Woo dealing five scoreless for Seattle and Weathers conceding only Arozarena's fifteenth homer. Then came the seventh and the now-customary New York uprising: Ryan McMahon singled home two and Trent Grisham two more, all charged to the unhappy Rucker, and Bednar closed the seven-and-twentieth save. The manner is nerve-wracking, the manager confesses, but it is working — the Bombers hold the American's top wild-card seat at seven-and-sixty wins. Seattle has now lost five straight, and the backstop Raleigh, hitless again with his average sunk to .159, minced no words about it afterward.

THE ROOKIE BATEMAN PROVES A DUDE, AND THE CANADIANS TAKE ANOTHER: CANADIANS 5, HUB NINE 3

Brett Bateman, a rookie of very recent vintage, doubled home one run and singled home another, and Toronto — scrappy, stubborn, and suddenly warm — collected a second straight victory. Cease struck out seven in five innings without a decision, the fresh-summoned Chase Lee took the win (see the shuttle desk above), and Straw's insurance double preceded Miles' first save. Rafaela and Abreu homered in the Boston cause, and the debutant Rutschman reached three times (see the news column), but the Hub nine have now dropped four consecutive and feel every one of their nine games behind Tampa Bay.

THREE HITS SUFFICE WHERE SIX SCORELESS INNINGS LEAD: ATLANTAS 4, METROPOLITANS 0

The economy of it: Atlanta collected precisely three hits and won by four, for two of the three left the premises — Albies' twentieth and Olson's five-and-thirtieth, the latter drawing him level atop the game's home-run column (see the news desk). Martín Pérez supplied six scoreless innings for his eighth victory, extending a run of shutout work that has the wires talking rotation arithmetic in Atlanta, and three relievers finished the whitewash. McLean deserved kinder — six innings, one run — but the Metropolitans were blanked on four hits, and the Atlantas roll on at two-and-seventy wins.

THREE CIRCUITS IN THREE INNINGS AND THE BALTIMORES ARE OFF EARLY: BALTIMORES 5, NORTH STARS 2

The visitors wasted no ceremony upon Ober: Alonso's six-and-twentieth cleared the wall in the first with a man aboard — his seven-and-seventieth run driven in — Henderson's nineteenth followed in the second, likewise a two-run affair, and Encarnacion-Strand made it three homers in three innings in the third. Young rode the cushion to his ninth victory against three defeats, and Kittredge filed the sixth save. Minnesota, held to two, has lost seven of ten and slides in the wild-card queue.

ON POPE HAT NIGHT, PROVIDENCE WEARS CINCINNATI RED: REDLEGS 5, PALE HOSE 4, IN TEN INNINGS

Before a sellout of eight-and-thirty thousand crowned in papal headgear, Chicago built a four-nought lead on circuits by Doyle, Quero, and Vargas — his five-and-twentieth — with Antonacci's run-scoring double for good measure, and Burke of the home flannels struck out eight across seven fine innings. The visitors unraveled all of it: Suárez homered in the fifth, a three-run eighth arrived on De La Cruz's single and Stephenson's two-run blow, and in the tenth De La Cruz lifted the decisive sacrifice fly. A different Burke — Cincinnati's — took the victory, and Pagán, having won a fourteen-pitch war of attrition along the way, sealed his fourteenth save. The Pale Hose still lead the American's Central, but the cushion thins.

PALLANTE MATCHES THE ALL-STAR ZERO FOR ZERO, THEN HIS FRIENDS FINISH IT: MOUND CITY NINE 2, BROTHERLY LOVE NINE 0

Andre Pallante and Cristopher Sánchez — the latter owning the National's most victories at fifteen — traded six scoreless innings apiece, Sánchez striking out seven to no profit whatever. The decision fell upon the successors: Winn singled home Walker in the seventh off the same-day call-up McFarlane, Pereira added his fifth homer in the eighth, and O'Brien closed his thirtieth save, drawing level with San Diego's Miller atop the National's column. Philadelphia, blanked on five hits, thereby completes its descent into the three-way wild-card knot chronicled above.

THE HALOS ANSWER A HARD WEEK WITH A TIDY THIRD INNING: HALOS 3, LONE STAR NINE 2

All the home scoring arrived in one frame: Neto singled home a run, Schanuel lifted a sacrifice fly, and Grissom doubled home a third, undoing Pederson's two-and-twentieth homer from the first. Bradford pitched seven honest innings for Texas and was beaten anyway, though the fresh-arrived Kelenic singled home a run in the sixth (see the shuttle desk); Fermin took the decision and Natera Jr. the third save. The defeat stings in Arlington: with Houston also beaten, the Lone Star nine remain half a game out of first in the American's West, a rung they might have claimed outright.

KARROS TURNS ONE SWING AGAINST THE DESERT: MOUNTAIN MEN 3, SONORAN NINE 2

Arizona opened as if intent on a rout, Carroll and Marte homering in the game's first six batters, and then scored no more forever. Kyle Karros flattened the evening with a two-run circuit in the sixth — his tenth — and Moniak's sacrifice fly in the seventh completed the reversal. Sugano claimed his twelfth victory with six sturdy innings, Romano filed the eleventh save, and the Sonoran nine, thus upset by the National's rearmost club, awoke to find themselves sharing a wild-card berth with two housemates (see the day's principal dispatch).

WALLS WAITS A YEAR FOR ONE HOMER AND THIRTY MINUTES FOR THE NEXT: SUNSHINE NINE 12, MACKMEN 4

Taylor Walls had not homered in three hundred and eighty days; last night he did it twice inside half an hour, driving in three, and his fellows made the occasion general — Caminero's five-and-thirtieth (see the news column), Díaz's seventeenth, Mesa Jr.'s tenth, and the first circuit of young Carson Williams' major-league life, a three-run flourish in the eighth. Martinez pitched the whole nine innings for his twelfth victory against three defeats. Eight straight wins now for the Sunshine nine, three-and-seventy in sum, and the American's best account grows fatter by the evening; the Mackmen, for their part, have dropped eight of ten.

THE ROOKIE BATTERY CARRIES THE BAY: BAY NINE 4, HOUSTON NINE 1

Whisenhunt wriggled through five and two-thirds innings conceding but a single run, the rookie catcher Cavanaugh collected two hits, a run driven in, and his first stolen base, and Eldridge's twelfth homer supplied the exclamation as San Francisco held Houston to five hits. Peña committed two errors in a forgettable visitor's evening; Alvarez's sacrifice fly — his seven-and-eightieth run driven in, the American's most — was the whole of the Texan offense. Houston's hold on the American's West remains half a game, preserved only by Texas losing simultaneously down the coast.

SNELL STRIKES OUT TEN IN HIS RESURRECTION, AND MUNCY WALKS IT OFF ONCE MORE: ANGELENOS 5, MISSOURIANS 4, IN TEN INNINGS

Blake Snell, absent three months for the removal of loose bodies from his pitching elbow — his sharpening credited by the wires to a high-altitude rehabilitation start — returned with six innings of three-hit, ten-strikeout work before a crowd of nearly fifty thousand. The melodrama followed his exit: Kansas City struck thrice in the eighth against Phillips, Caglianone and Perez singling home runs before an error let in a third, and Betts — who had homered earlier, his fourteenth — leveled matters with a single in the bottom half. In the tenth, the bases full, Max Muncy singled home Ohtani for the second consecutive night of late-inning deliverance from his bat. Halvorsen took the victory, and the champions have quietly won two straight.

A full card of fifteen decorates this Wednesday, and the desk commends the following: at 2:15 the Brotherly Love nine seek their revenge in St. Louis behind Wheeler, ten-and-three with a 2.69 figure; at five past three the Sunshine nine send Rasmussen and his 2.75 against the Mackmen, nine straight in their sights; at ten past four the Brewers attempt to arrest their skid in San Diego with May opposing Ray; and the evening belongs to a pair of American League showdowns upon the national broadcast — the Puget Sound men at the Bronx at 7:05, and the Lone Star nine at Anaheim at ten past ten, half a game of first place riding on Texan shoulders. The sentimentalist is directed to Dyersville this afternoon for the High School All-Americans, and to the same corn upon the morrow, when the Twins and Phillies play the Field of Dreams game proper. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
