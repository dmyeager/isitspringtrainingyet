Volume I · No. 94 · Saturday, the Tenth of October, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

No contests this day; the pennant races rest.

0 days until the fifth and deciding game of the Division Series — the White Sox at the Guardians, eight o'clock this very evening by the lake (Saturday, the 10th of October, 2026).

News Around the League

THE DECIDER DESK: ONE QUARREL LEFT OF FOUR, AND IT ENDS TONIGHT BY THE LAKE — WILLIAMS KEEPS THE BALL HE NEVER SURRENDERED, BURKE TAKES IT WITH NO OPENER AHEAD OF HIM, AND THE WINNER PACKS FOR TAMPA BAY

The bracket has spent its riches down to a single argument. Of October's four Division Series, three are settled and their winners gone to their rest; only the Central's remains, and it concludes this very evening — the White Sox at the Guardians, eight o'clock Eastern at Progressive Field, the survivor to present itself at Tampa Bay's dome on Monday.26 Two games apiece, and the series' peculiar signature holds to the last: every victory in it has been taken in the other man's yard, Chicago seizing the first pair by the lake, Cleveland the next two on the South Side.

Stephen Vogt has answered the question his own Game 4 adventures raised: Gavin Williams — who closed Thursday's 9-5 verdict with the first relief errand of his professional life — keeps the start he was always scheduled to make, on his regular rest. "Gavin wants the ball," said the manager. "We want him to have the ball, and it's his game." The pitcher himself declines the taxonomy altogether: "It doesn't matter the role at this point," he offers, and reports the body sound. The Forest City nine hope Thursday's three-out cameo reads, in the final accounting, as a high-intensity side session rather than a withdrawal from tonight's funds.3

Chicago counters with Sean Burke, 11-7 with a 3.34 mark on the year, and this time no opener precedes him — the assignment is his from the first pitch. His Game 2 turn in bulk relief, one run on one hit across five and a third innings with six strikeouts and thirteen men retired in order at one stretch, remains the finest pitching the visitors have shown Cleveland all series, and Will Venable pronounces him "built for these types of moments."4 The bullpen ledgers counsel economy on both sides of the field: Erick Fedde's 61 pitches of Thursday likely rule him out of tonight's account, Hagen Smith has now worked three times in the series and on consecutive days, and Grant Taylor — charged with four runs of Thursday's sixth without recording an out — professes himself ready for anything at all. "We just want to win this series however possible," says Venable, "and wherever that is, we'll take it."4 The stakes have stood in this paper's columns all week and want no embroidery this morning: the Pale Hose chase a first pennant round since 2005, the Guardians the lifting of the game's longest-standing championship drought, and one of those long waits grows one day shorter tonight.

THE ROTATION DESK: YESTERDAY'S SURMISE BECOMES TODAY'S OFFICIAL RECORD — SKUBAL SUNDAY AND YAMAMOTO MONDAY FOR LOS ANGELES, WHILE MILWAUKEE KEEPS ITS OWN COUNSEL

What this paper carried yesterday as the wire's well-reasoned surmise is now the official record: Dave Roberts on Friday named Tarik Skubal for Sunday's opening game in Milwaukee and Yoshinobu Yamamoto for Monday's second, precisely the order the reasoning foretold — Yamamoto proceeding on his accustomed five days of rest, with a sixth game held open for his return should the quarrel stretch so far.5 Milwaukee, for its part, has signed nothing: Jacob Misiorowski appears set to answer Skubal in the opener, but the Brewers have made no announcement, and the Herald reports the probability as a probability and not a fixture.5

The ledger behind the Los Angeles order is briefly told. In the Division round the four starters — Skubal, Blake Snell, Yamamoto and Tyler Glasnow — allowed 4 earned runs in 21 1/3 innings among them, a 1.69 figure; Skubal's National League debut ran six innings of one-run work against Atlanta, and Yamamoto's Game 3 ran seven with 10 strikeouts.5 Behind the confirmed pair the projections place Snell third, on extended rest and still nursing the groin tightness that has trailed him since the middle of September — it showed in a ragged five-walk outing that lasted under four innings in Game 2 — and Glasnow fourth, in whose seven postseason starts the Boys in Blue have yet to taste defeat.5

The curtain rises Sunday at eight o'clock Eastern in Milwaukee, the second sitting follows Monday at five, and the argument removes to Los Angeles for Wednesday's third.2

THE REPOSE DESK: ELEVEN IDLE DAYS AGAINST THREE OF WORK — THE GULF COAST CLUB SCRIMMAGES AGAINST ITSELF THIS AFTERNOON WHILE OCTOBER CHOOSES ITS NEXT OPPONENT

The Rays, their sweep of New York concluded Wednesday, have made an art of the interval. Home Thursday and the rest of the day off; a full dress workout Friday at their dome; an intrasquad scrimmage this very afternoon, the club's hitters and pitchers obliged to make do with one another; a standard drill Sunday — and then Monday night, the first game of the pennant round, against whichever club survives tonight by the lake.6 Between the regular-season finale of September 27 and that first pitch the club will have counted eleven idle days against three of work. Kevin Cash accounts the rest a "privilege," and his charge to the room wants no translation: "We've got two more bites — two more bites on the bone!"6

The pitching plan waits politely upon tonight's result — "some pretty good ideas," the manager allows, with nothing final until the opponent is known — though Drew Rasmussen stands in line for the opener, as he stood for the Division round's, and Cash means to lean on the same five starters throughout.6 The repose has been kindest to the relief corps: Bryan Baker, the locksmith of the ninth of whom this paper wrote only yesterday, was used but sparingly in the sweep, as were Cam Booser and Kevin Kelly, and the Championship Series calendar — off days after the second and fifth games only — will ask considerably more of them than the Division round ever did.6

THE BALLOT DESK: TEN NAMES TO A LEAGUE AND TWO DAYS LEFT AT THE BOX — THE AARON HONOR NAMES ITS FINALISTS, FIVE OF THEM STILL AT THEIR DAY'S WORK THIS OCTOBER

The season's first ballot-box stands open. The Hank Aaron Award, the annual laurel for each league's most accomplished batsman, has its finalists for 2026: ten names to a league, selected by a committee of MLB.com's own journalists, with the winners to be chosen by a panel of the game's legends and past honorees — the fandom casting its own votes besides, at the league's site, until 11:59 Monday morning by the Eastern clock.7 The winners are to be announced November 11 at Las Vegas on the MLB Network's awards evening. The Herald, by its standing rule, reports a ballot as a ballot: nothing has yet been won, and the trophy keeps to its case until the panel speaks.

The rolls reward a reading. The American League's ten include Bobby Witt Jr., Riley Greene, Pete Alonso and Yordan Alvarez; the National's, Shohei Ohtani, Kyle Schwarber, Pete Crow-Armstrong and Fernando Tatis Jr.7 And five of the twenty are still in uniform this October: Junior Caminero and Yandy Díaz of Tampa Bay's waiting company, Miguel Vargas of the White Sox — at liberty to add to his argument this very evening — and Ohtani and Milwaukee's Jake Bauers, whose clubs open the pennant round against one another Sunday.7

THE PACIFIC DESK: NINE-AND-THIRTY CIRCUIT CLOUTS AND THE THREE JEWELS OF THE CENTRAL LEAGUE — SATO OF THE HANSHIN TIGERS WEARS JAPAN'S TRIPLE CROWN, AND THE POSTING WINDOW BECKONS

From the far shore of the Pacific comes word of a coronation. Teruaki Sato of the Hanshin Tigers has won the Japanese Central League's Triple Crown, batting .306 with 39 home runs and 106 runs driven across — a .991 on-base-plus-slugging besides — making him the ninth man in Nippon Professional Baseball's long history to lead his league in all three columns at once, and the first since Munetaka Murakami turned the trick for Yakult four years ago.89 The 27-year-old left-handed slugger, principally a third baseman, follows a 2025 campaign of 40 home runs and 102 runs batted in with the finest season of his life.9

What follows is the portion that concerns the thirty clubs of this hemisphere: the wire expects Sato posted for their inspection as soon as early November, and the insider Jon Heyman already names the Mets, the Yankees, the Phillies and the Dodgers among the likely bidders.8 The precedent travels well and sits near at hand — Murakami, the last man to wear this crown, crossed the ocean and now draws his pay from the White Sox, whose season comes to its winner-take-all hearing this very evening. The scouting reports will make their winter rounds; the Herald merely notes that the market for Japanese royalty has lately been a seller's.

The Friday ledger is a blank page — not a pitch thrown from one coast to the other — and the paper begrudges the quiet nothing, for the calendar now owes us riches on consecutive evenings: tonight at eight by the lake, the only fifth game October's first round produced; Sunday at eight, the National League's curtain in Milwaukee; Monday, the American League's at Tampa Bay's dome, with the senior circuit's second sitting at five o'clock the same day.2 Somewhere tonight a season ends in Cleveland — one or the other must — and a pennant argument gains its final party before the morning. The Herald will have the whole account in its next number. Until the next dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
