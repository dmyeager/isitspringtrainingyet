Reporting 1 contest from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

THE SECRET IS ANNOUNCED OVER THE LOUD-SPEAKER AND KEPT ANYWAY — ONE-AND-SEVENTY FASTBALLS FROM 'FASTBALL FREDDY,' THE SWIFTEST OF HIS NINE-YEAR LIFE; A GROUND BALL FINDS THE ONE CRACK IN THE LEAGUE'S SUREST THIRD BASEMAN; AND THE THROWBACK-CLAD RAYS SQUARE THE PENNANT ROUND: RAYS 3, GUARDIANS 1

Kevin Cash grumbles that even the public-address man was advertising 'Fastball Freddy' before the first pitch — then Peralta throws 71 four-seamers among his 99 pitches regardless, 29 of them at 97 miles the hour or better and one at 99.4, the hardest of his career, across six scoreless innings in the longest postseason outing of his life; Tampa Bay's three runs, every one unearned, are manufactured from a walk, two infield choppers, an alert dash to an unguarded base and a ball that skips through José Ramírez's legs; and Parker Messick's boyhood-ballpark homecoming ends in a tough-luck defeat as the series packs for Cleveland tied at a game apiece

The evening's one secret was given away before the first pitch, and it kept perfectly well anyway. "The announcers before the game and even our PA were calling him 'Fastball Freddy,'" Kevin Cash grumbled afterward, half in jest. "That told everybody what was coming."4 What came, Tuesday night at Tropicana Field, was 71 four-seam fastballs among Freddy Peralta's 99 pitches — a reliance he had not permitted himself in any start since June of 2019 — and the forewarned Guardians went 3-for-17 against the pitch and never once scratched him.4 The figures read like an affidavit: 29 offerings at 97 miles the hour or better from a man whose season average was 94.3; a 99.4 to Jo Adell in the fifth, the hardest pitch of his nine-year career; 51 of the 71 heaters spotted at the top third of the strike zone or above it.43 "It's already an elite fastball, but he was placing it at the top for strikes," said Steven Kwan. "And then it felt like when he wanted to, he just put it a ball above. When he's doing that, it's pretty hard to lay off."4 Six scoreless innings — the longest postseason errand of Peralta's life — on four singles, two walks and a hit batsman, closed with a nine-pitch siege of Petey Halpin that ended in a changeup, a strikeout, a scream off the mound and the dome chanting "Freddy! Freddy!" at the Trade Deadline acquisition. His last eight starts, summer and October together, have now each allowed five hits or fewer and no more than one run, with a Tampa Bay victory to show for every one — the longest such run by any traditional starter since 1900.4

The three runs behind him were all unearned and all of a piece — "a very Rays inning," their own manager certified.6 Junior Caminero opened the fourth with a walk; Victor Mesa Jr.'s double-play bouncer was undone on review, the relay having pulled Nathaniel Lowe's foot from the bag; and after a strikeout, Chandler Simpson — making his first start since the second game of the Division round — chopped a ball five feet in front of the plate and outran the clean play. Mesa, observing that third base stood unguarded where Ramírez had drifted toward short, simply never stopped running.6 Then Ben Williamson beat a 2-1 changeup into the ground toward the surest glove in the league, and the ball struck the lip where the dirt meets the artificial turf, hopped off the top of Ramírez's mitt and passed between his legs into left field as Mesa trotted home.65 Liam Hicks, dropped to eighth in the order against the left hand, lined the next man's first pitch — a slider left up — to right-center for two more. One ball out of the infield in the whole rally, and the game decided by it. "They've done that all year," said Stephen Vogt. "When they smell blood, they take it and run."3

Cleveland's grief wants a careful accounting, for the club otherwise played a clean, hard game. Ramírez makes the play in question as near to always as the language allows; this once the bounce defeated him. "Nobody's perfect, but I still feel bad," the third baseman said through the club's interpreter. "The ball bounced right on the edge between the dirt and the turf. That's what made it more difficult for me."5 Kwan, who was himself victimized by the surface in the opener, offered the visiting fielder's lament: "It's kind of inconsistent whether it's going to hop or skip. When you don't play on it every day, you're just not calibrated to it."5 The offense supplied its own share, going 1-for-6 with runners in scoring position and leaving nine men aboard; the lone reply was Chase DeLauter's double to right in the seventh, cashing Patrick Bailey's walk.5 And Messick — whose homecoming to the ballpark of his boyhood this paper set out at length yesterday morning — deserved a kinder ledger than the one he signed: five innings, six hits, two walks, three runs and not one of them earned, a tough-luck defeat in the house he once watched from the cheap seats.5

The victors, it should be recorded, played the evening in fancy dress. Down a game, the Rays swapped into their Devil Rays throwbacks around ten o'clock that morning on a suggestion from the club's chief executive Ken Babby — a costume in which they had gone 12-3 during the regular season — and the swagger arrived with the wardrobe.7 Caminero leapt to spear Kwan's fifth-inning liner and administered a finger wag after the fashion of Dikembe Mutombo; by the seventh the two were conferring amiably at third base about it. "He told me, 'Hey, if I hit it a little bit harder, the ball's past you,'" Caminero reported. "And I said, 'Yeah, try to hit it harder and I won't catch it.'"7 So the 32,125 went home in two hours and forty-two minutes, and the round removes to the lakefront tied at one — no small adjustment of the odds, the home-field club having historically taken such a series 55 times in 102, and 24 of 40 in the pennant rounds. The third game goes Thursday evening at six o'clock Eastern at Progressive Field, and the Herald surveys that sitting elsewhere in this number.32

News Around the League

THE LINEUP DESK: THE QUESTION OF RESTING OHTANI IS ANSWERED BY THE MAN HIMSELF — 'ADAMANT' HE CAN HELP, THE GAME'S GREATEST INSTRUMENT TAKES HIS PLACE IN TONIGHT'S ORDER, MECHANICS, SYMPTOMS AND ALL

The conversation this paper reported yesterday has been held, and the player won it. Shohei Ohtani will bat for Los Angeles in tonight's third game, Dave Roberts announced Tuesday at Dodger Stadium, the club having weighed resting him and found the man immovable. "He was very convicted that he is onto something mechanically," the manager said. "He was adamant that he can help us. He's well enough to help us win a baseball game. So that's all I needed to hear."8 Roberts likened the sitting-down he did not order to the one he once gave Freddie Freeman, whose sprained ankle cost him three games of the 2024 postseason before five World Series appearances that entered legend.8

The figures that prompted the question have not improved for being aired. Ohtani stands at .197 with a .629 on-base-plus-slugging over his last 35 games, has not homered since August 18 — a drought of 110 plate appearances, the longest of his career — and is 3-for-20 this October without an extra-base hit, dropped from second in the order to cleanup. Speaking to the press for the first time since the eve of the Division round, he declined the alibi of the right biceps and left knee that have troubled him since June: "The symptoms shouldn't be the reason why I can't hit," he said through his interpreter.8 The remedies have been uncharacteristic — on-field batting practice four days running, from a man who takes it in public a handful of times a year, and even a show of bunt against a left-handed reliever on Monday.8 The opposition declines to read the ledger literally. "We remember vividly at this time last year that he maybe had the best baseball game ever played the day he pitched against us," said Milwaukee's Pat Murphy. "You can't forget that, his capabilities, whatever."8 On that much, every scorer in the press box agrees.

THE PRECIPICE DESK: TONIGHT AT EIGHT THE TWICE-CROWNED CHAMPIONS MAKE THEIR STAND AT HOME, TWO GAMES IN ARREARS — SNELL, WHO FACED THE MINIMUM THROUGH EIGHT AGAINST THESE SAME BREWERS A YEAR AGO, DRAWS THE ASSIGNMENT AGAINST GASSER, A MAN WHO BEGAN THIS SEASON IN NASHVILLE

The National League's argument resumes tonight at eight o'clock Eastern at Dodger Stadium, and the arithmetic of the precipice is plainly printed: clubs down two games to none in a best-of-seven have recovered the series 16 times in 95 trials, and of the 42 that ever fell behind three games to none, 32 were swept and exactly one — Roberts' own 2004 Red Sox, as the manager hardly needs reminding — climbed all the way out.9 "You want to get that first one," Roberts allows.9

His chosen instrument is the left hand with the best claim on Milwaukee's respect. Blake Snell, limited to nine starts this season by shoulder trouble and surgery on the elbow, was "too fine" by his manager's reckoning in his lone October outing, walking five men in an abbreviated Division-round start; but his five starts at Dodger Stadium this year show a 2.19 earned-run average, and his last acquaintance with these Brewers — in this very round, a year ago — ran eight scoreless innings in which he struck out ten and faced the minimum.910 "0-2, 2-0, it wouldn't matter," Snell said. "I know what I need to do to win and dominate." His teammate Teoscar Hernández counts the remaining cards without blinking: "We've got Snell, we've got Glasnow, and then we've got Tarik again... I think we're going to be OK."10

Milwaukee's answer is the season's quietest success story: Robert Gasser, who opened the year at Triple-A Nashville and enters a pennant-round start on a run of 2-0 with a 1.73 earned-run average over his last seven appearances — a rise that began in late August when a comebacker felled Dustin May on the game's first pitch and Gasser walked out of the bullpen into a rotation he has not left since.9 With Milwaukee's fourth-game starter yet unnamed, every inning the sweeper-artist supplies tonight is worth two. The home crowd, the probabilities and the champions' pride all report for duty at the same hour; the Herald will believe the obituaries when it sees the body.9

THE LAKEFRONT DESK: THE AMERICAN ROUND, NOW A BEST-OF-FIVE WITH THREE GAMES BY THE LAKE, RECONVENES THURSDAY AT SIX — JAX IS NAMED FOR TAMPA BAY, WILLIAMS STANDS LIKELY FOR CLEVELAND, AND THE LOUDEST YARD OF THE AUTUMN AWAITS THEM BOTH

The American League's pennant round rests today and reconvenes Thursday at six o'clock Eastern at Progressive Field, reduced by the split at the dome to a best-of-five in which Cleveland holds the next three home dates.112 The statisticians commend the appointment: in best-of-seven series knotted at one, the third game's winner has gone on to take the series 71 times in 102.5

Tampa Bay has named its man — Griffin Jax, a starter for most of the season, who carries a 0.90 earned-run average over his last five appearances and spent only 32 pitches in his lone Division-round errand, two tidy innings against the Yankees.11 Cleveland has named nobody, Vogt preferring to see how his staff emerges from the first two games, but the ball figures to belong to Gavin Williams on regular rest — the same Williams whose Division round ran to a 1.54 earned-run average with 24 strikeouts in 11 2/3 innings, closing one elimination game and starting the next.11

The venue is reckoned a combatant in its own right. The lakefront crowd that sang its club back from two games dead against Chicago now receives a series tied, and even the enemy tips his cap in advance. "That's as good an atmosphere as you're going to see," said Kevin Cash. "They've got a great fan base that's going to be behind their team."11 The marquee names, meanwhile, owe the round a signature: Ramírez's series so far holds one solid game and one costly error, and Caminero still awaits the first postseason home run of his career. One of the two clubs will put the sport's longest waits another game behind it Thursday — Cleveland's drought of 77 seasons against a Tampa Bay franchise that has never once, in 28 years, carried the flag home.11

THE MANAGERIAL DESK: SEATTLE ASKS LEAVE TO COURT THE MAN WHO KEPT HOUSTON'S MACHINE RUNNING — ESPADA, HIS CONTRACT EXPIRING WITH THE MONTH, WILL HEAR THE MARINERS OUT

Word from the executive corridors: Joe Espada, these past three seasons the manager of the Astros and for six before that their bench coach, will interview for Seattle's vacant managership, the Mariners having sought and received Houston's permission by the account of the wire's sources. Neither club confirms the courtship; the man's contract expires at the month's end, which rather confirms it for them.12

The résumé travels well. Espada's nine Houston years took in the 2022 championship, two further pennants and seven division titles, and his own three seasons at the helm ran 256-229 with the West's flag in 2024 and again this summer — though this summer's edition won it at 81-81 and was swept from the Wild Card round by the White Sox, a decline that had already cost the general manager Dana Brown his post in August.12 Espada, for his part, has said plainly he would like to continue in Houston.

Seattle's specification reads like a reply to its own history: the president of baseball operations Jerry Dipoto — whose two previous selections, Scott Servais and the departed Dan Wilson, had never managed a game at any level — now declares prior big-league experience "preferred" and the need to be for "fresh thought."12 A man with three division titles in three years, suddenly at liberty across the division, answers the advertisement almost suspiciously well. The Herald files the courtship under October's quieter competitions and will report the banns if they are published.12

A single game stands on tonight's card, and it may be the month's heaviest: the National League's third sitting, at eight o'clock Eastern at Dodger Stadium, where the champions of the last two World's Series stand twice beaten, with Snell on the hill and Ohtani batting on his own insistence. Thursday the calendar doubles again — Tampa Bay at Cleveland at six o'clock, Milwaukee at Los Angeles at nine.2 The week's two evenings at the dome have supplied a matched pair of lessons: Monday the form sheet burned, and Tuesday it was lovingly restored, the best fastball of a man's life and a single error punished to the last unearned run. The game gives and takes away with the same hand, and October keeps every receipt. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
