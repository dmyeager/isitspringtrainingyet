The Game of the Day

TWO HUNDRED SIXTY-FOUR SUCH BLOWS HAD ALWAYS LEFT THE YARD, AND THE TWO HUNDRED SIXTY-FIFTH DIED IN HALPIN'S GLOVE — YET CAMINERO AND HIS FELLOWS WOULD NOT BE REFUSED, AND THE AMERICAN QUESTION STANDS LEVEL AT TWO GAMES APIECE: RAYS 7, GUARDIANS 3

Petey Halpin crashes into the center-field wall to rob Junior Caminero of a drive that, by the ledger of every like ball struck since the measuring instruments went up, had never once stayed in a ballpark — and Caminero answers two innings later with the go-ahead double anyway; Jonathan Aranda, hitless in ten tries entering the night, collects three hits and two runs batted in; Yandy Díaz closes the account with a two-run homer to the opposite field in the ninth; and Nick Martinez's 5 1/3 innings of one-run ball, preserved by two borrowed outs from Freddy Peralta, square the pennant round and guarantee its return to St. Petersburg on Monday

Begin with the cruelest out of the month. In the fourth inning Saturday night at Progressive Field, Junior Caminero struck a ball 110.7 miles the hour at the sweet-spot angle — and since the league's measuring instruments began their ledger in 2015, 264 balls had been hit so, and all 264 were home runs. The 265th hung in the frigid lakefront air nearly six full seconds while Petey Halpin ran a crooked, backpedaling errand to the deepest corner of center field, gathered it at the base of the wall, and struck the barrier with a thud the yard heard twice over. "For that to stay in the yard, it's got to do some funky stuff," said the robbed man's robber; "100%," said Caminero, asked if he thought it gone.7 Foster Griffin, who threw the pitch, called the play unbelievable and the ball a home run in any number of other parks.7

Cleveland's luck, having spent itself so extravagantly on one catch, ran dry thereafter. Caminero returned in the sixth and laced Daniel Espino's offering 110.2 miles the hour over the leaping José Ramírez into the left-field corner, cashing Jonathan Aranda for the lead, then stood atop second base in his October layers beating his chest at his own dugout.34 It was the awakening the Rays had waited a round for. Their celebrated top three — Díaz, Aranda and Caminero, whom the manager Kevin Cash needles in lean weeks as los tres pequeños, the three little ones — had come to Cleveland a combined 5-for-34 with one run batted in and 13 strikeouts; on Saturday they went 6-for-14 and drove in five. Aranda, hitless in the series at nightfall, finished 3-for-4; Díaz doubled, then sealed the business in the ninth with a two-run homer carried just over the right-field wall, opposite-field work of the barrel-chested old school.43 "It was just a matter of time for us three to get going," said Aranda through the club's interpreter.4

The pitching held up its end with room to spare. Nick Martinez, 36 years of age and master of three fastballs and a changeup he refused to abandon after spiking it early, allowed Steven Kwan's first-inning single and Chase DeLauter's run-scoring knock — DeLauter promptly cut down stretching for two by Victor Mesa Jr.'s sliding stop and throw — and then retired 11 men in succession, handing over 5 1/3 innings of one-run ball and collecting the first postseason victory of his long career. "He did a lot of Nick things out there," certified Cash.5 When Martinez departed with a man aboard in the sixth, the relief was Freddy Peralta — the Game 2 starter, lately of the Mets, whose between-starts throw-day the Rays simply relocated from the bullpen mound to the sixth inning of a pennant round. He recorded his two outs and kept the lead at one. "I'm just a reliever," he teased the writers afterward. "Why do you want to talk to me?" His starter answered for them: "He saved our game today."6

The seventh settled it, and unkindly for the home nine. Richie Palacios and Taylor Walls scored on Aranda's single to right, which skipped under DeLauter's glove for two bases more and an unearned third run when Chandler Simpson's sacrifice fly followed. "Put that one on me," said DeLauter, declining every excuse offered him. "You miss a ground ball and give up two runs — essentially give up three."11 Cleveland answered gamely — Travis Bazzana's first postseason homer and Brayan Rocchio's run-scoring single made it 5-3, and Kwan finished the evening with gauze packed in a lip he split diving into first base — but no nearer.8 So the round stands two games apiece, a best-of-three with the home dome recovered: the fifth game goes this very evening at five o'clock by the lake, Drew Rasmussen against the rookie Parker Messick, and the sixth is now guaranteed for Monday night at St. Petersburg.32 The scholars' comfort for Cleveland, who stood one swing from a three-games-to-one grip: in series knotted at two, the fifth game's winner has taken the argument in 46 cases of 69.8 The 33,355 who sat through 46 degrees of partly cloudy went home with the same arithmetic.1

News Around the League

THE PIVOT DESK: THE FIFTH GAME GOES AT FIVE O'CLOCK THIS EVENING, AND THE RAINOUT'S STRANGE GIFTS ARE NOW PLAIN — RASMUSSEN ON HIS ACCUSTOMED FIFTH DAY AGAINST MESSICK MOVED UP A GAME, WITH A PENNANT'S WORTH OF LEVERAGE ON THE TABLE

Friday's washout, which this paper mourned at the time, has proved an even-handed quartermaster. Drew Rasmussen takes the ball for Tampa Bay tonight on the five days' rest he kept nearly all season — his October account stands at 14 innings, two runs, two walks and 17 strikeouts across two starts, atop a 16-5, 2.67 regular campaign — while Cleveland, as this paper supposed yesterday it might, moves Parker Messick up from his appointed sixth game to the fifth, on normal rest, precisely because the postponement shoved the calendar a day.1081 The rookie left-hander's season now runs to 198 innings, the sixth-most by a first-year man in the last 20 years and far past his previous high of 138 1/3; his manager has allowed that some October command trouble may be the workload talking. The rookie himself files no grievance: "Honestly, my body feels great. To be able to take the ball in this game shows the confidence the team has in me."98

The stakes require no embroidery: the winner flies to St. Petersburg tonight holding three games of the four won and one victory from the World's Series; the loser bats for his life Monday. Cleveland's manager Stephen Vogt declares himself unshaken — "I'll put our guys up against any team in the world in a three-game series" — and holds in reserve a rested late guard of Hunter Gaddis, Erik Sabrowski and Cade Smith, none of whom worked in Saturday's seven-man bullpen procession.108 Tampa Bay, for its part, wanted exactly this: "That was one of the main objectives that we had here coming into Cleveland, just to force the series back home," said Jonathan Aranda. The forecast, for once, has nothing to say: clear and cold by the lake.10

THE WAITING DESK: SEVEN FULL DAYS OF IDLENESS FOR THE PENNANT-HOLDERS, THE LONGEST SIT OF ANY FLAG-WINNER SAVE THE '07 ROCKIES — AND THE LEDGER ON LONG RESTS READS EXACTLY EVEN, THOUGH THE RECENT PAGES LEAN AGAINST THE RESTED

Milwaukee's reward for sweeping its round by Thursday is a week of waiting: seven full days between the pennant and Friday's first game of the World's Series, the club's longest pause since spring training and the second-longest any flag-winner has endured since the championship rounds went to seven games in 1985. Only the 2007 Rockies sat longer — eight days, after winning 21 of 22 — and they were promptly swept by Boston, a precedent Milwaukee is free to decline.12

The full accounting is studiedly neutral. Since the postseason took its three-round shape in 1995, clubs entering the Series with the extra rest stand exactly 15-15; clubs that sat five full days or more, as the Brewers will, went 10-10. But the recent pages lean the other way — since 2009 the club that clinched its pennant first has gone 4-12 in the Series, and from 2009 through 2017 the less-rested club won nine straight.12 The sweep itself cuts both ways: of the 11 previous clubs that reached the Series by sweeping a seven-game round, only four finished the job — though the three most recent of them, the 2019 Nationals, 2022 Astros and 2025 Dodgers, all did.12 The Suds men may prefer the simplest reading: the first game goes Friday evening at their own yard, and nobody idle has lost anything yet.122

THE SHORTSTOP DESK: DOMBROWSKI SERVES NOTICE ON HIS $300 MILLION MAN — MEND THE GLOVE BY SPRING OR QUIT THE POSITION, PHILADELPHIA TELLS TURNER, AFTER A SEASON OF ONE-AND-TWENTY ERRORS

The bluntest sentence of the young offseason was pronounced in Philadelphia. Dave Dombrowski, the Quakers' president of baseball operations, summoned Trea Turner to Citizens Bank Park on Tuesday and told the shortstop — seven years and $190 million still owed on his $300 million engagement — that his defense must recover or his position will. "He knows he has to be better at shortstop," Dombrowski reported. "I told him it's really mandatory that it's like that. You cannot play shortstop like you did defensively in '26 going to '27."13

The indictment is in the ledger: Turner, 33, batted .242 with a .675 on-base-plus-slugging — perhaps the worst season of his career — and committed 21 errors at shortstop, the most in the league, before going 1-for-11 in the Wild Card defeat at Atlanta's hands. The remedies sketched are several: Turner to the outfield, which he does not prefer; Bryson Stott across the bag to shortstop; the prospect Aidan Miller, recovered from a year lost to his back, competing for a place by spring. Turner, to his credit, filed no appeal — "I've got a lot of work to do," he said. "Find answers. See what I did bad."13 The man's no-trade clause makes him all but immovable, so the parties are wedded to the repair — and with a work stoppage reckoned all but certain come December, the winter may afford him more drilling time than anyone would wish.13 The Herald, whose sympathies in such matters are on record, merely notes that a club which can owe one workman $190 million has settled the question of whether the till is empty.

THE AUCTION DESK: THE WINTER MARKET'S CROWN LOT IS ALREADY POSTED — SKUBAL, RENTED BY LOS ANGELES AT THE COST OF TWO PROSPECTS OF THE FIRST RANK, NOW STANDS TO COMMAND THE RICHEST PITCHING CONTRACT EVER LET

The Dodgers' early exit opens the bidding season, and the first lot is the grandest. Tarik Skubal heads a class of nine Los Angeles free agents — Kiké Hernández, Evan Phillips, Alex Vesia, Blake Treinen and the retiring Miguel Rojas among the others — and is reckoned the top player on the whole market. Los Angeles paid dearly for its three months of him at the summer deadline: the ready-made starter River Ryan, the outfield prospect Zyhir Hope and the right-hander Brady Smith, all to Detroit, for a rental now concluded.14

The walk-year ledger holds up its end. Skubal went 12-7 with a 2.72 earned-run average across 26 starts split between the Tigers and Dodgers, despite six weeks lost to a left elbow operation; last winter he took a record $32 million from Detroit in the arbitration room — an award this desk files among the honest victories of the workingman's side of the ledger — and he is now widely expected to surpass Yoshinobu Yamamoto's twelve-year, $325 million engagement as the largest guarantee ever extended a pitcher.14 Whether Los Angeles, which must also weigh the futures of its own young men, re-enters the bidding for the man it rented is the question Chavez Ravine will chew on all winter; the pitcher himself, asked after Thursday's elimination, preferred not to discuss it.14

The forward ledger is brief and loud: the fifth game of the American round this evening at five o'clock by the lake, the sixth Monday night at St. Petersburg, and a seventh Tuesday at the same address only if the argument insists; the World's Series opens Friday, the 23rd, at Milwaukee, where the hosts wait out the week in enforced leisure.2 By tomorrow's number one club will stand a single victory from the pennant, and the other will be rehearsing arguments for a Monday jury. The Herald will be at its post for all of it. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
