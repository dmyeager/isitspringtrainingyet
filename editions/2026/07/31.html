THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 23 · Friday, the One-and-Thirtieth of July, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 10 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

ONE HIT IN EIGHT INNINGS, THEN FOUR RUNS IN A SINGLE SWING: CLEMENS'S TWO-OUT GRAND SLAM IN THE NINTH STEALS THE VERDICT FROM KANSAS CITY, 4 TO 3, AT MINNEAPOLIS

Noah Cameron carries a one-hit shutout through eight and departs to applause; his relief corps fills the bases with two down, whereupon Kody Clemens — son of the Rocket, bearer of all four Minnesota runs batted in — deposits the third pitch he sees into the right-center seats, and a nine so lately hopeless goes home delirious

For eight innings the night belonged wholly to Noah Cameron. The Kansas City left-hander permitted the Twin Cities nine a single hit across those eight frames, fanning seven upon but seven-and-ninety pitches, while his mates staked him three: Jac Caglianone's two-run circuit in the third — his seventeenth — and John Rave's fifth-inning double, cashed by a Minnesota throwing error. Three to nothing it stood when Cameron yielded the ball, and the two-and-twenty thousand at the yard could be forgiven for reaching toward the exits.

Then the ninth, and the oldest lesson in the ledger: no verdict is rendered until the final man is retired. Lucas Erceg, summoned to close, could not find the third out — a single, a base on balls, and the sacks stood full with two down. In came the left-hander Strahm to face Kody Clemens, a man batting .244 whose surname wants no introduction in any city where the game is played. On Strahm's third offering Clemens swung, and the ball did not come back: a grand slam to right-center, his nineteenth home run, all four of Minnesota's runs driven home in one thunderclap, and a three-hit, four-run robbery sealed at 4 to 3. The wires from Minneapolis put it simply: there are memorable wins, and then there are moments.

The arithmetic beggars belief — Kansas City out-hit the victors eight to three and departed beaten — and the standings sharpen the sting. The Twin Cities men square their account at five-and-fifty apiece and stand but a single game shy of Cleveland's hold upon the American's final wild-card chair; the Missourians, at six-and-forty wins, sink twelve games adrift in the Central. Gómez took the win unnoticed; Clemens took the evening entire.

News Around the League

The Bazaar, With Monday's Bell Now Audible: Detroit Ships Rogers to Baltimore, the Mets Deal Minter North for an Agbayani, and the Skubal and Greene Markets Boil

Three days remain before the deadline tolls Monday, the third of August, at six o'clock, and the commerce quickens. Done and recorded: the Tigers have traded the catcher Jake Rogers to the Orioles for the right-hander Zane Barnhart — Baltimore's catching corps being a ward unto itself, with Rutschman (left wrist) and the rookie Basallo (right shoulder) both still upon the shelf. Reported by the wires' sources besides: the Mets send the left-handed fireman A.J. Minter to Minnesota, receiving in return a nineteen-year-old outfielder who happens to be the son of Benny Agbayani, playoff hero of the old Shea faithful — a quarter-century's circle neatly closed.

As for the greater game: the Skubal sweepstakes swells by the hour, one correspondent declaring that offers "should start flowing in any minute" now that Detroit, at one-and-fifty wins against eight-and-fifty defeats, has all but folded its October hand. The Reds' former All-Star Hunter Greene is likewise generating buzz, a source placing the odds of his movement at "50-50." And in Flushing the clock runs finest of all: unless he is dealt before ten past seven this very evening, Freddy Peralta takes the hill against Miami, says the interim manager Green — a deadline within the deadline.

The Infirmary Ward: McClanahan's Back Seizes at Tampa Bay, While Ohtani's Knee Grants Him an Evening in the Dugout

The Tampa men won their engagement (see the card, below) but hold their breath regardless: Shane McClanahan departed in the fourth inning with what the club styles mid-back tightness, his evening done after three frames. The consolation, such as it is: the complaint concerns his back and not the storied left arm, though the severity awaits the morning's judgment. Out west, Shohei Ohtani reported renewed soreness of the left knee whilst undergoing treatment and was held from the Dodgers' lineup for Thursday's finale — the club expects him back at his post this very evening, and his mates managed handsomely in his absence (see the card, below).

The Motor City Summons Its Crown Jewel: Max Clark, the Wires Report, Arrives This Very Evening

A ray of light for the beleaguered Bengals: the wires report the club is calling up Max Clark, its finest prospect and the thirteenth-ranked in all the land, for this evening's series opener at the Athletics' yard. The young center fielder, the third man taken in the draft of two summers past, stayed patient at Toledo whilst the parent club auditioned a procession of center fielders, and a dominant July has at last forced the door. Detroit's followers, lately fed a diet of blown leads and bazaar rumors concerning their ace, may finally have something to cheer.

The Counting-House: With the Labor Compact Expiring the First of December, "Competitive Balance" Becomes the Phrase Upon Every Tongue

A note from the game's counting-house, where the abacus clacks as loudly as any bat: the league and its players' brotherhood, having opened negotiations upon the twelfth of May, continue their labors toward a new agreement before the present compact expires the first of December. Competitive balance — the ancient quarrel over whether the richest nines purchase too great a share of October — is expected to sit at the very center of the table. The Herald ventures no prophecy on how such matters resolve, save to observe that the game has survived every counting-house war yet waged upon it.

The Almanac: Cornfields in a Fortnight, Williamsport Beyond

For those who mark their calendars in advance of their scorecards: the Field of Dreams engagement comes the thirteenth of August, the Twins and Phillies to play amid the Dyersville corn, and the Little League Classic follows the three-and-twentieth, Brewers against Braves at Williamsport, Pennsylvania. The regular campaign's final day remains the seven-and-twentieth of September — eight-and-fifty days hence, for those keeping the grimmer count.

The Rest of the Card

TWO HITS, SEVEN WALKS, AND NO CIGAR: THE TAMPA MEN EDGE TEXAS, 3 TO 2, ON MULLINS'S EIGHTH-INNING THUNDER

The Lone Star nine drew seven passes and struck but two blows all evening, and the arithmetic finally told. Texas nursed a 2-to-1 edge — Langford's double the honest half of it — until Jonny DeLuca squared matters with a sixth-inning circuit, and in the eighth Cedric Mullins struck his thirteenth home run off Peoples for the verdict. Cleavinger took the win and Baker his thirtieth save, joint sovereign of the American's closers. The Rays, at four-and-sixty wins the American's finest, stretch their Eastern lead to three-and-a-half games — though the evening cost them their starter (see the ward, above).

WHAT IS BETTER THAN ONE WALK-OFF? TWO IN SUCCESSION: THE PALE HOSE OUTLAST THE PINSTRIPES AGAIN, 2 TO 1, IN ELEVEN CHAPTERS

A night after their twelfth-inning resurrection, the South Siders did it again. Nothing crossed for nine innings — Weathers spun seven scoreless for New York while Burke fanned ten across six — and when Grisham's single put the visitors ahead in the tenth, Benintendi answered directly to square it. In the eleventh the sacks filled against Cruz, and Tristan Peters settled the matter without swinging: a bases-loaded pass, a walk-off walk, and a second consecutive extra-inning verdict over the New Yorkers. Domínguez won it. The Central leaders hold Cleveland two games at arm's length; the Pinstripes, dropped twice running, fall three-and-a-half behind Tampa Bay.

BREGMAN AND HAPP STRIKE IN THE TENTH, DOUBLE UPON DOUBLE: THE NORTH SIDE MEN TAKE THE RUBBER MATCH AT ST. LOUIS, 4 TO 2

Knotted at two after JJ Wetherholt's sixteenth home run answered Busch's seventh-inning single, the affair reached a tenth inning, where Alex Bregman lined his sixth double of the club's four-day residence at Busch Stadium to send Suzuki home, and Ian Happ promptly doubled Bregman across besides. Webb collected the win, Civale his first save of the campaign, and the Cubs claim the series. At two-and-sixty wins they hold the National's first wild-card chair, five-and-a-half back of Milwaukee; the Redbirds slip below the even mark.

ROBERT SPEAKS TWICE AND THE FLUSHING NINE LISTEN: METS 4, MARLINS 2, THROUGH AN HOUR'S RAIN

After an hour and six minutes' delay, Kyle Stowers and Jakob Marsee supplied the Gulf Stream nine's whole commerce by circuit blows, but Flushing had the patient answer: Lindor's single and an error-gifted run in the sixth, then Luis Robert Jr.'s two-run single in the eighth off Petersen for the decision. The rookie McLean gave six-and-a-third sturdy frames and Williams sealed his sixteenth save. The Mets take two straight; Miami falls to five-and-fifty apiece, their road miseries continuing.

DE LA CRUZ DOUBLES THRICE AND SCORES THE LAST RUN STANDING UP — WELL, TROTTING: THE REDLEGS WALK OFF PITTSBURGH, 3 TO 2

Elly De La Cruz struck three doubles, stole two bases, and in the ninth crossed with the winning run upon Nathaniel Lowe's force-out — a walk-off by the humblest instrument. Brandon Lowe's three-and-twentieth home run had squared it for the Corsairs in the sixth. Pagán took the win over Soto, and Matt McLain, activated from the injured list that very afternoon, scored the game's first run for good measure. Pittsburgh has now dropped three straight and sits at five-and-fifty apiece; the Queen City men bank a second victory in succession.

THREE SOLO SHOTS SPOTTED AND ALL OF THEM REPAID: THE TOMAHAWK NINE OVERHAUL WASHINGTON, 5 TO 4, ACUÑA STRIKING HIS FIRST CIRCUIT SINCE MAY

Daylen Lile homered twice and Luis García Jr. once, and the Capital nine led three to nothing before Jake Irvin — activated from the sixty-day shelf that morning — had allowed a hit. Then Atlanta's answer: Ronald Acuña Jr.'s solo blow in the fourth, his first since May; Yastrzemski's two-run stroke to square it; Albies's two-run single in the sixth for the lead. García Jr.'s seventh-inning triple drew Washington within one, but Iglesias barred the door for his three-and-twentieth save. The East leaders, at four-and-sixty wins, sit seven clear of Philadelphia; Irvin's return ledger reads five runs in five innings and the defeat.

BACK-TO-BACK IN THE EIGHTH, AND THE LEDGER TURNS: ABREU AND CONTRERAS CARRY THE CRIMSON HOSE PAST THE MACKMEN, 5 TO 4

Trailing four to three in the eighth, the Bostons required but two swings: Wilyer Abreu's sixteenth home run to square it and Willson Contreras's three-and-twentieth directly upon its heels, both off the unfortunate Medina. Sonny Gray, though touched for four runs across seven frames, ran his astonishing account to thirteen victories against two defeats — no man in the American owns more wins — and Chapman sealed his six-and-twentieth save. Boston has won two straight and holds the second wild-card chair; the Mackmen, at five-and-forty wins, fall for the second night running.

TATIS ROBS DEVERS AT THE WALL — AGAIN — BUT SUSAC AND RAY SETTLE THE LARGER QUESTION: THE ORANGE AND BLACK 4, SAN DIEGO 1

The first inning furnished the theater: Rafael Devers launched one bound for the right-field seats and Fernando Tatis Jr. went up the wall to take it back — a year after performing the identical larceny upon the identical victim — though Ramos trotted home on the sacrifice regardless. Daniel Susac did the heavier commerce, a two-run circuit in the second and a sacrifice fly in the fifth, while Robbie Ray scattered six frames of one-run ball for his tenth victory before two-and-forty thousand at the Mission nine's yard. Devers banked three hits in consolation. San Diego slips two games off the wild-card pace; the Frisco men have won two running.

RUSHING'S THREE-RUN THUNDERBOLT AND SASAKI'S STEADY HAND: THE ANGELENOS SUBDUE SEATTLE, 6 TO 2, AND STAND ALONE ATOP THE GAME

No Ohtani in the order (see the ward, above), and no matter: Mookie Betts doubled two home in the first, Dalton Rushing struck a three-run circuit in the fourth, and Teoscar Hernández added a solo blow in the sixth. Roki Sasaki fanned seven across five-and-a-third for the victory over Woo, before two-and-fifty thousand at Chavez Ravine. At nine-and-sixty wins against forty defeats the Dodgers own the game's finest ledger outright; the Puget Sound men, beaten twice running, drift to seven games below the even mark.

A full fifteen-game card awaits, and the bazaar shadows every line of it. The Pinstripes visit Wrigley Field at twenty past two, Warren opposite Imanaga, for a matinee between two nines of October ambition; at ten past six Skenes faces Hunter Greene at Cincinnati, every scout in creation watching the latter; at ten past seven the Central-leading Pale Hose call upon the East-leading Rays, Martinez — ten victories against two defeats — barring the door. By late lamplight Max Clark is expected to make his debut for Detroit at the Athletics' yard at forty minutes past nine, and Boston opens at Chavez Ravine at ten past ten. In Flushing, Peralta takes the hill at ten past seven — unless the wires say otherwise before first pitch. Three days to Monday's bell. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
