THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 22 · Thursday, the Thirtieth of July, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 16 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

SEVEN RUNS DOWN AND LEFT FOR DEAD, THE BIRDS RISE TWICE: BALTIMORE 10, DETROIT 9, IN TWELVE CHAPTERS, ON THE NIGHT OF SKUBAL'S THOUSANDTH STRIKEOUT

The great left-hander opens his perhaps-valedictory evening by fanning his thousandth man and departs with a seven-run cushion; Baltimore answers with five in the seventh, Mayo's tying triple in the eighth, and — after Torkelson's two-run thunderbolt squares it anew in the eleventh — Cowser's twelfth-inning single, the club's grandest resurrection in seventeen summers

The evening began as a coronation. Tarik Skubal, taking the ball at Detroit in what the wires solemnly style his potential farewell in the home flannels, threw a changeup past Taylor Ward to open the proceedings — the thousandth strikeout of his life, reached in fewer innings than any Tiger before him, Scherzer included, and faster than every left-hander in the game's history save four. The crowd rose; the pitcher doffed his cap; and his mates set about building him a fortress, Javier Báez singling home two in the third and the runs accumulating until the ledger read seven to nothing after six. "Some of these accomplishments just hit different," Skubal would say afterward, and through six innings nothing about the night suggested anything but a garland.

Then the Birds — eleven games adrift in the East, and flattened fourteen-to-nothing in this very yard but the night before — staged the franchise's grandest comeback in seventeen years. Five runs crossed in the seventh — Taveras, then Jackson Holliday with a two-run single, then Ward with another — and in the eighth Coby Mayo lashed a two-run triple, his first three-bagger of the year, and seven-nothing was seven apiece. The ninth and tenth passed barren. In the eleventh the Birds pushed two across upon Pozo's sacrifice fly and Holliday's third run driven home, whereupon Spencer Torkelson answered with a two-run blow, his twentieth, and the affair stood nine and nine. In the twelfth, Colton Cowser lined a single to center, Ward crossed, and Kittredge — the seventh Baltimore pitcher of the night — recorded the final out for his fourth save. Wolfram took the victory, Sommers the ruin, and the visitors had somehow scored ten runs upon nine hits while committing three errors, an arithmetic that defies every textbook in print.

The standings render their own cold verdict on the vanquished: Detroit, at one-and-fifty wins against eight-and-fifty defeats, sits six games back in the Central and four-and-a-half from the American's final wild-card chair — figures the front offices now canvassing for Skubal's services will read as an invitation (see the bazaar, below). The Birds, for their part, stand but two-and-a-half games from that same final chair, and after a night such as this may be forgiven for believing in resurrections.

News Around the League

The Bazaar, Four Days from the Bell: Texas Lands O'Hoppe and Silseth by Night, the Skubal Sweepstakes Swells to Half the League, and Miller Grows Dearer by the Hour

The deadline tolls Monday, the third of August, at six o'clock in the evening, and the first considerable commerce is done: the Rangers, their catching corps ravaged by injury — Jansen and Higashioka both upon the shelf — acquired the young catcher Logan O'Hoppe and the right-handed reliever Chase Silseth from the Angels for the minor-league infielder Angel Arredondo. "a right-handed power arm, leverage arm," said the Texas manager Schumaker of his new fireman, adding of O'Hoppe: "still young, a controllable catcher." Meanwhile the Skubal market swells by the day: the wires report the Dodgers, Yankees, Rays, Brewers, and Braves all ringing Detroit's offices, the Phillies dispatching scouts to his Wednesday start besides, and one prominent correspondent declares the industry "almost resigned" to the great left-hander landing in Los Angeles. As for San Diego's flame-throwing Mason Miller, the odds of his movement shrink with each Friar victory — five straight now — and the front office there is said to demand that no man in a suitor's organization be off limits. The Miami ace Alcantara's name circulates anew as well. Four days remain; the shouting has scarcely begun.

The Infirmary Ward: Buxton's Hip Returns Him to the Shelf After Nine Days at Liberty, While Kurtz Awaits the Morning's Magnets

Grim tidings from the Twin Cities: Byron Buxton, but nine days removed from his last activation, returns to the injured list with an impingement of the right hip — the same tormentor that has dogged him since May and cost him his All-Star engagement. He departed Tuesday's contest after two turns at bat, received an injection, and leaves behind a line of five-and-twenty home runs in but two-and-eighty games; the youngster Keaschall will mind center field in his absence. Out west, the Athletics hold their breath: Nick Kurtz, the reigning American Rookie of the Year, was struck upon the left forearm by a ninety-four-mile sinker in the first inning of Wednesday's affair and left the game with what the club styles a contusion, the magnets to render their verdict this very morning. The Elephants, who have already lost Rooker and Langeliers for the season, can ill afford a third such bill.

Cue the Trumpets: Díaz Steps Off the Shelf and Onto His Own Bobblehead, Slamming the Ninth Shut at Chavez Ravine

The theatrics could not have been better arranged by a playwright: Edwin Díaz was reinstated from the injured list Wednesday evening — the very night the Dodgers distributed his likeness in bobblehead form at the gates — and when the ninth inning arrived, the trumpets of his famous anthem sounded through the yard for the first time in ninety days. "I got lucky," the closer grinned beforehand, and then made his own luck: touching nine-and-ninety miles upon the gun, he weathered a two-double squall in the ninth and coaxed the final man into a line-out to Freeman's glove, sealing the fifth save of his campaign (see the card, below). The left-hander Barnes was designated for assignment to clear the room.

The Skipper Hears the Captain's Plea and Shrugs: "Players Like Being GMs," Says Mattingly, as Philadelphia's Skid Reaches Three

A day after Bryce Harper allowed that his club "need some help," the Philadelphia interim manager Don Mattingly declined to join the chorus. "I sense Bryce thinking about it. I don't really sense anybody else thinking about it," said the old Yankee captain, before delivering the epigram of the week: "Players like being GMs and making calls and spending other people's money." The front office chiefs Dombrowski and Preston Mattingly are nonetheless expected to seek reinforcements before Monday's bell. The urgency writes itself: the Philadelphias have now dropped three straight and stand three-and-nine since the All-Star pause, swept out of Miami entirely (see the card, below) after squandering a six-run lead in the finale.

The Rest of the Card

TWELVE INNINGS ON THE SOUTH SIDE, AND QUERO SPEAKS LAST: THE WHITE STOCKINGS OUTLAST THE BRONX BOMBERS, 6 TO 5, ON SIX HITS AND NO SMALL SORCERY

The Bombers struck four in the first — Spencer Jones's three-run blow the heavy stroke — and Schlittler, keeper of the American's finest earned-run figure, fanned eight across six-and-a-third. Yet the Chicagos kept chipping: Benintendi's double in the seventh, Antonacci's two-run circuit and Murakami's tying solo — his three-and-twentieth — in the eighth. When Volpe's sacrifice fly restored New York's lead in the eleventh, Murakami promptly scored upon a pickoff gone wrong; in the twelfth Edgar Quero lined home the verdict. Hicks won it, Yarbrough lost it, and the Central leaders — six hits, six runs — hold Cleveland at arm's length by a game and a half while the Yankees fall two-and-a-half behind Tampa Bay.

NOTHING TO NOTHING THROUGH NINE, EVERYTHING IN THE TENTH: HERRERA'S TWO-RUN DOUBLE WALKS OFF THE BRUINS, 3 TO 2, AT ST. LOUIS

Boyd spun seven spotless innings for Chicago and May answered with five one-hit frames, and so the affair reached the tenth a double blank. There Pete Crow-Armstrong struck his four-and-twentieth home run, a two-run stroke that seemed decisive — until the Mound City men stormed back against Thornton: a run forced home upon Walker's ground ball, and then Iván Herrera, three hits on the night, lining a two-run double to left to end it. Gastelum collected the win. The Cardinals thus arrest a three-game slide at the even mark of four-and-fifty apiece; the Cubs, six back of Milwaukee, hold the National's first wild-card chair yet.

SIX RUNS SQUANDERED AND A SWEEP ENDURED: THE FISH DEVOUR PHILADELPHIA'S LEAD, 8 TO 6, CONINE STRIKING THE DECISIVE BLOW

The Philadelphias built a six-nothing edifice by the fifth — Turner's fifteenth home run and Harper's triple among the timbers — and watched it burn: three Miami runs in the fifth, three more in the seventh upon Lopez's and Edwards's singles, and in the eighth the pinch-hitter Griffin Conine's two-run circuit off Bowlan to seal the reversal. Fairbanks banked his fifteenth save, and the Floridians — winners of three straight, every one against Philadelphia — complete the sweep at five-and-fifty wins against four-and-fifty defeats. The visitors' aggregate for the series: six runs, all of them Wednesday, and a captain pleading for reinforcements (see the bazaar, above).

THE MATINEE TO THE AMAZINS: BENGE'S SEVENTH-INNING DOUBLE TRUMPS OLSON'S TWO-RUN ANSWER, 3 TO 2

The twin bill's first act at Flushing went to the home nine: Alvarez doubled a run across and Ewing's sacrifice fly made it two in the fifth, and after Matt Olson squared matters with his eight-and-twentieth home run, the rookie Carson Benge lined a double to right in the seventh to send Ewing home with the verdict. Brazobán won, Williams sealed his fifteenth save, and the Atlantas — themselves buried beneath fourteen Metropolitan runs on Monday — were reminded that the spoilers of Flushing keep at their trade.

THE NIGHTCAP BELONGS TO THE ANCIENT LEFT-HANDER: SALE'S SIX SCORELESS AND NINE STRIKEOUTS CARRY ATLANTA, 1 TO 0, OVER TWO METROPOLITAN HITS

Vengeance arrived by lamplight. Chris Sale, seven-and-thirty years old and pitching as though the calendar were a rumor, threw six scoreless innings with nine strikeouts — "he just defies all the laws," marvel the wires — running his account to twelve victories against six defeats with an earned-run figure of 2.08, second in the National. Olson singled home Jarvis in the sixth for the evening's lone commerce, Iglesias sealed his two-and-twentieth save, and the Mets managed two hits in all. The split leaves the East leaders six-and-a-half clear of Philadelphia.

THE OHIO CUP GOES TO THE LAKE ERIE MEN: FOUR PATIENT RUNS IN THE FIFTH, MANZARDO'S THIRTEENTH, AND A 6-TO-1 VERDICT AT CINCINNATI

Cleveland assembled its fifth inning from the humblest materials — Bazzana's single, two bases-loaded passes to Manzardo and Arias, Bailey's sacrifice fly — and Kyle Manzardo added a solo circuit in the seventh for flourish. Sabrowski carried the decision over twelve Cleveland hits; Singer absorbed his tenth defeat. The Lake Erie men, having taken the rubber match of this Ohio Cup engagement, cling to the American's final wild-card chair with Houston a game in arrears — and sit but a game and a half behind the White Stockings in the Central.

DOBNAK'S LONG-DEFERRED REWARD: THE KANSAS CITY NINE BLANK MINNESOTA, 4 TO 0, TOLBERT SUPPLYING THUNDER FROM AN UNADVERTISED QUARTER

Randy Dobnak banked his first victory of the campaign as the Kansas City pitchers administered a five-hit whitewash. The speedster Tyler Tolbert did uncommon heavy work: a home run in the third — his third — a double, a stolen base, and two runs scored, while the venerable Perez legged out his first triple of the season and Caglianone singled home another. Ryan took the defeat, and the Twins — deprived of Buxton anew (see the ward, above) — slip a game and a half from the wild-card line.

THREE HITS SUFFICE WHEN TWO OF THEM CLEAR THE WALL: DÍAZ AND VILADE STRIKE FIRST-INNING CIRCUITS, AND THE SUNSHINE NINE BLANK TEXAS, 3 TO 0

The whole of the evening's scoring occupied the opening frame: Yandy Díaz — batting .301, second in the American — struck his fifteenth home run, and moments later Ryan Vilade followed with a two-run blow of his own. Seymour made three hits stand as a fortune, Baker closed for his nine-and-twentieth save, and Gore — betrayed by an offense that mustered four hits — absorbed the defeat. The Rays, at three-and-sixty wins the American's best, stretch their Eastern lead to two-and-a-half games; the Arlington men, their Western cushion shaved to a single game, consoled themselves with the night's trade (see the bazaar, above).

THE RUBBER MATCH WANTS NO EXTRA CHAPTERS: RODRIGUEZ REACHES TEN VICTORIES, MORENO CLEARS THE WALL, AND THE SNAKES SILENCE PITTSBURGH, 3 TO 0

After two nights of extra-inning bedlam, the third act was all brisk efficiency: Rodriguez ran his ledger to ten-and-three, scattering five Pittsburgh hits, while Gabriel Moreno's two-run homer in the fourth and Kepler's single in the sixth furnished the commerce. Garcia sealed his second save. The Snakes take the series and sit joint holders, with Philadelphia, of the National's last two wild-card chairs; the Bucs drop two games off that pace, their second straight defeat.

A BIRTHDAY BELATEDLY HONORED: YESAVAGE, ONE DAY PAST THREE-AND-TWENTY, STIFLES THE SENATORS AS THE MAPLE LEAF NINE RALLY LATE, 5 TO 2

The young right-hander Yesavage, having turned three-and-twenty but a day prior, flashed what the wires call ace stuff, and his elders furnished the runs when it mattered: Clement's tying double in the sixth, Varsho's go-ahead single in the seventh, and a three-run eighth built upon Okamoto's two-run double — the slugger from Japan now owning eight-and-sixty runs driven in. Varland closed for his three-and-twentieth save. The Senators, so puissant abroad, remain curiously helpless in their own yard, where the ledger now reads three-and-twenty wins against four-and-thirty defeats.

DOWN THREE BEFORE AN OUT WAS RECORDED, THE 'STROS GRIND IT BACK: 7 TO 4 OVER THE HALOS, AND A THIRD STRAIGHT VICTORY

Anaheim struck thrice in the first — Grissom's two-run double the chief instrument — and Houston answered with patience: solo circuits from Yainer Diaz and Peña, then Cam Smith's two-run double in the sixth followed by his dash home upon a wild pitch, and single runs late for cushion. Wesneski earned his first victory of the year, Abreu his seventh save. The 'Stros have won three running — eight of their last ten — and breathe upon Texas's neck a single game back in the West; the Halos have dropped three straight.

TEN INNINGS AT THE ELEPHANTS' YARD, AND MONASTERIO SETTLES IT: BOSTON 4, THE ATHLETICS 2

A taut affair of traded solo blows — Heim and Butler for the home nine, Jahmai Jones for the visitors, with Sogard's single squaring it at two — stretched into a tenth inning, where Andruw Monasterio struck a two-run homer, his sixth, to decide it. The closer Chapman vultured the victory and Weissert banked his first save. The Fenway men, seven winners in their last ten and owners of a handsome road ledger at one-and-thirty wins against two-and-twenty defeats, hold the second wild-card chair; the Elephants' evening was darkened in its very first inning by Kurtz's injury (see the ward, above).

SIXTEEN RUNS AND SEVENTEEN HITS BY THE BAY: SUSAC'S FIRST TWO CIRCUITS OF THE YEAR ARRIVE IN ONE EVENING AS THE CREAM CITY NINE ARE ROUTED, 16 TO 3

The Bay nine visited a thorough indignity upon the league's proudest ledger: seven runs in the sixth, three more in the seventh, three in the eighth, the catcher Susac striking his first and second home runs of the season with four runs driven in, Ramos and Lee adding three apiece, and Arraez — the National's batting sovereign at .331 — chipping in two. Webb collected the decision; Pannone was the party charged; Milwaukee kicked the ball thrice besides. The defeat costs the Brewers their share of the game's best record — the Dodgers now hold it alone — though their Central lead over Chicago stands six games yet.

A FIFTH STRAIGHT FOR THE FRIARS, AND MILLER GROWS COSTLIER STILL: SAN DIEGO 3, COLORADO 1

One three-run burst in the fourth — Merrill's single, Rengifo's ground ball, Sheets's sacrifice fly — proved the whole of the necessary commerce, and Canning ran his record to two victories against nine defeats. Mason Miller sealed his eight-and-twentieth save, most in the National, a figure his front office no doubt recites to every suitor on the wire (see the bazaar, above). The Friars' fifth consecutive victory draws them within a game and a half of the wild-card line; the Rox have dropped four running.

PAGES OPENS THE SCORING AND DÍAZ CLOSES THE BOOK: THE BOYS IN BLUE TAKE THE OPENER FROM SEATTLE, 4 TO 2, AND STAND ALONE ATOP THE GAME

Andy Pages struck his eighteenth home run in the first and singled home another besides; Ohtani doubled a run across and Freeman singled home a third; and Lauer carried the decision into the late innings, whereupon the trumpets announced Edwin Díaz's return from the shelf (see the news, above) and the ninth was duly sealed despite two Seattle doubles. The Seattle nine got late runs from Young and Naylor and nothing more. At eight-and-sixty wins against forty defeats, the Dodgers own the game's finest record outright; the mariners of the Sound slip two-and-a-half back in the West.

Ten engagements on this day's card. The matinees come thick: the Rangers — O'Hoppe now among their number — seek redress at Tampa Bay at ten past twelve; the Bombers and White Stockings settle their series on the South Side at ten past two; the Bruins and Cardinals reconvene at St. Louis at a quarter past two. By evening the Senators open at Atlanta at a quarter past seven upon the national broadcast, Gray — the American's winningest man at twelve-and-two — takes the hill for Boston at the Elephants' yard at forty minutes past nine, and Sasaki faces Woo at Chavez Ravine at ten past ten. Four days now remain before Monday's deadline bell, and every wire hums with the name Skubal. Until the next dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
