Is it spring training yet? No — but the championship season is upon us, a finer thing still.

THE MORNING HORSEHIDE HERALD

"Every Score Set Down, No Deed Unsung"

~ Being a Faithful Daily Chronicle of the National Pastime ~

Volume I · No. 18 · Sunday, the Six-and-Twentieth of July, Two Thousand and Twenty-Six

Reporting 15 contests from the day prior.

The Game of the Day

ONE HIT IS ALL BOSTON GETS: CEASE GOES THE FULL NINE AT FENWAY, STRIKES OUT TWELVE, AND THE LAST-PLACE JAYS TOPPLE THE LEAGUE'S WINNINGEST MAN, 6 TO 0

One hundred and twenty pitches, a lone double surrendered, and not a run to show against him — while Springer's three-run blast felled Gray, hitherto twelve-and-one, and a Fenway house of near seven-and-thirty thousand went home having witnessed a masterpiece for the visitors

Andruw Monasterio's sixteenth double of the season would scarcely merit a line of type on an ordinary evening; last night at Fenway Park it was the entire Boston offense, first article and last. Dylan Cease allowed that one hit and nothing else across nine full innings — twelve strikeouts, three passes, one hundred and twenty pitches, the final one thrown with his arms stretched toward the sky — and the Maple Leaf nine, last in the American East at eight-and-forty wins against seven-and-fifty defeats, administered a 6-to-0 silencing to a club that had won nine of its previous ten.

The evening's other astonishment was the man opposite. Sonny Gray arrived with a ledger of twelve wins against a single defeat, the winningest pitcher in the game, and departed after five innings having yielded ten hits and five runs. Toronto did the bulk of its work in the third: Vladimir Guerrero Jr. singled home the first run, George Springer followed with a three-run thunderclap to left — his eleventh, with Guerrero and the record-setting rookie Kazuma Okamoto aboard — and Ernie Clement doubled home a fifth for good measure, his four-and-twentieth two-bagger of the year. Guerrero finished three-for-four and added a ninth-inning single that cashed the sixth run.

Mark the paradox the box score presents: Cease, now the American League's strikeout sovereign at one hundred sixty-seven with an earned-run average of 2.46, pitches like a laureate for a club thirteen games and a half from the summit — and the wires this morning speak openly of his deepening Cy Young case. Boston's grief is more immediate: the eight-of-ten surge is checked, and the Fenway men's hold upon the second wild-card chair stands at a bare half-game over Cleveland, which also lost (see the card below). Two hours and five-and-twenty minutes was all Cease required. The scorecard will be framed somewhere in Ontario.

News Around the League

The Bazaar Opens for Business: Mead to Boston, Early to Washington, Eight Days Before the Bell

The first musket of deadline season has been discharged. Boston, long in want of a right-handed bat, has acquired the young infielder Curtis Mead from Washington, sending the rookie left-hander Connelly Early south in exchange — this mere hours after Mead had crossed the plate in Washington livery in Saturday's victory over Arizona (see the card below). The deadline of the third of August now stands but eight days off, and the rumor columns boil accordingly: the appraisers report suitors gathering about San Diego's closer Mason Miller, with the playoff-minded White Sox said to be weighing an all-in bid; the Rays, holders of the American League's best record, are described as aggressive buyers; the Skubal riddle in Detroit remains unsolved; and the Athletics, per the wires, have agreed to terms with the former Cy Young laureate Dallas Keuchel upon a minor-league pact, though the club has not yet confirmed the report.

The Infirmary Ward: A Grim Verdict on Soto's Calf, Ohtani's Bullpen Postponed, and Bellinger to the Examiners

The photographer's table delivered its verdict in Flushing, and it was the one the Metropolitans dreaded: Juan Soto's left calf bears a strain of the second grade, and he has been placed upon the ten-day invalid list — his second such banishment of the season — with a significant absence expected. For a club now three-and-forty and two-and-sixty, the nightmare campaign deepens. In the same city, Shohei Ohtani postponed the bullpen session he was to throw Saturday, a lingering discomfort in the left knee the culprit, though he remains in the Dodger order as the batsman; the pitching project waits another day. And in Philadelphia, Cody Bellinger is to be examined for an ailing hamstring — the club hopeful no invalid-list stay is required — a worriment noted the very evening the Yankees won a third straight without Aaron Judge (still awaiting the mending of a fractured rib) and Giancarlo Stanton (proceeding slowly through his running progression).

The Record Book Opens Twice: Perez Stands Alone Among Royalty, and Díaz Reaches a Thousand

Ink two fresh lines in the ledger of deeds. In Detroit, Salvador Perez — the beloved backstop of the Kansas City nine, serving yesterday as the batsman — struck the three hundred eighteenth home run of his career, passing the immortal George Brett for the most ever hit in Royals raiment. He had tied Brett at three hundred seventeen but three days prior; now the crown is his alone, and the blow itself squared a game his mates would win in the ninth (see the card below). And at Tampa Bay, Yandy Díaz rapped the thousandth hit of his career amid the Rays' blanking of Cleveland — a milestone reached, fittingly, on an evening his club played like the pennant-holder it presently is.

Cooperstown Crowns Its Immortals This Very Afternoon: Beltrán, Jones, and Kent

The village on Otsego's shore keeps its appointment today: Carlos Beltrán, Andruw Jones, and Jeff Kent are to be enshrined in the National Baseball Hall of Fame this afternoon. Two outfielders counted among the finest of their generation — the one a switch-hitting October colossus, the other the deftest center-field glove of his age — and the mightiest power-hitting second baseman the game has recorded, gathered at last onto bronze. The Herald counsels every reader within a day's ride of Cooperstown to make the journey; immortality is conferred but once a year.

The Capital's Young Jewels: Abrams Reaches Five-and-Twenty as the Suitors Circle

CJ Abrams struck his five-and-twentieth home run of the campaign in Saturday's win over Arizona, went three-for-four with three runs driven in, and now sits atop the National League's run-batted-in table at nine-and-seventy — this while his young comrade James Wood carries eight-and-twenty homers of his own. The pair, aged five-and-twenty and three-and-twenty, are the twin engines of a Washington nine that has climbed within two games of the wild-card line — and yet the rumor columns report the Nationals willing to entertain offers on their shortstop, with the Yankees, Red Sox, and Brewers among the interested parties. Whether the Capital nine mean to sell their jewels or merely appraise them, the next eight days will tell.

The Rest of the Card

BEAVERS AGAIN, AND ALWAYS IN THE NINTH: THE ROOKIE'S THIRD WALK-OFF BLOW SINKS ATLANTA, 3 TO 2

One night after losing a ten-inning heartbreaker on this same ground, the Baltimores took their revenge in regulation: Dylan Beavers singled home Jackson Holliday in the last of the ninth, the third walk-off hit of a career not yet a year old. Christian Encarnacion-Strand had opened the scoring by cashing Pete Alonso in the third, and Gunnar Henderson added his eighteenth homer; Atlanta drew level on Mauricio Dubón's groundout and Ozzie Albies's eighth-inning circuit blow, but no further. Cano took the win, Fuentes his first defeat of the year, and the Braves' three-game string is cut — though their hold on the National East remains five games and a half, Philadelphia having obliged by losing as well.

PEREZ FOR HISTORY, LOFTIN FOR THE VERDICT: THE ROYAL BLUES STEAL ONE AT COMERICA, 3 TO 2

Salvador Perez's record-breaking three hundred eighteenth career homer squared the account at two in the seventh, and Nick Loftin's ninth-inning blast — a modest fifth of the season, struck at a most immodest hour — carried the Royal blues past the Bengals. Hao-Yu Lee had done Detroit's scoring singlehanded with a run-scoring double and a solo homer; Lynch took the decision and Lange sealed his tenth save. The Detroits, three days ago winners of three straight, have now surrendered the gains.

A BURRITO, A RECORD BLAST, AND A THIRD STRAIGHT VERDICT: WELLS'S FOUR-HUNDRED-THIRTY-FOOT WONDER LIFTS THE BOMBERS, 3 TO 1

The catcher Austin Wells — fortified, the wires solemnly attest, by a pre-game burrito — launched the longest home run of his career, four hundred thirty feet to the opposite field, to square matters in the third; Cody Bellinger singled him home in the sixth and Jasson Domínguez added a seventh-inning solo blow. Weathers gave five and two-thirds sturdy innings with seven strikeouts, and Bednar banked his twentieth save before four-and-forty thousand in Philadelphia. The Bronx men have won three running and sit but two behind Tampa Bay; the Phils, losers of four straight, cling to the National's first wild-card chair by a single game.

MARTINEZ AGAIN, AND THE GULF COAST CLUB ROLLS ON: CLEVELAND BLANKED, 3 TO 0, AS THE SLIDE REACHES FOUR

Martinez gave six spotless innings without a walk for his tenth win against two defeats, Junior Caminero doubled home the first run, Cedric Mullins lifted a sacrifice fly, and Hunter Feduccia squeezed home a third with a deft bunt. Baker closed the account for his seven-and-twentieth save, second-most in the league. At one-and-sixty wins the Tampa men own the American's best record; the Lake Erie men, losers of four running and now but a half-game clear of the wild-card chasers behind them, managed four hits and no comfort — Bibee falling to four wins against ten defeats.

THE DUEL THAT WASN'T: IMANAGA SPOTLESS, CROW-ARMSTRONG FOUR-FOR-FIVE, AND THE CUBBIES BURY SKENES BENEATH ELEVEN RUNS

The wires had billed Imanaga against Skenes as a renewal of Friday's ten-inning quarrel; the Cubbies declined the premise. Pete Crow-Armstrong went four-for-five with three runs scored, a double, and his three-and-twentieth homer; Miguel Amaya and Michael Busch delivered two-run singles in a five-run sixth; and Imanaga threw six shutout innings for the 11-to-0 laugher. Skenes struck out eleven — a season's high, wasted utterly — while yielding five runs in five and a third, his bid for a fourth straight win foiled and his ledger evened at nine and nine. The sellout Smoky City grandstand, the correspondents noted, sounded suspiciously like Wrigley Field without the ivy.

GRIFFIN JOINS THE TWELVE-WIN CLUB AS ABRAMS AND LILE STRIKE IN A FOUR-RUN THIRD: SENATORS 5, SNAKES 3

Arizona's four-game string met its end at the Capital. Down two in the third, Washington answered with four — CJ Abrams's two-run double and Daylen Lile's two-run triple the heavy blows — and Abrams added his five-and-twentieth homer in the fifth for cushion. Griffin labored through six and two-thirds for his twelfth win against two defeats, drawing level with the National League's winningest arms, and the rookie Beeter sealed his ninth save. Gabriel Moreno drove in two of the Snakes' three; their grip on the league's final wild-card chair holds at a game, with St. Louis a game and a half back.

EOVALDI'S SEVEN STERN INNINGS, AND THE TEXAS NINE WIDEN THE WEST'S GAP: MARINERS FELLED, 7 TO 1

Texas scored thrice before Seattle recorded three outs — Ezequiel Duran's run-scoring triple and Evan Carter's double the instruments — added two more in the second upon Wyatt Langford's double and Duran's single, and let Nathan Eovaldi do the rest: seven innings, three hits, eight strikeouts, his tenth win. Langford and Joc Pederson collected three hits apiece; Alejandro Osuna's two-run double in the seventh completed the freight. The mariners of the Sound have now dropped four straight and sit two and a half back in the West, while the Texas nine's lead over surging Houston stands at two.

BROWN'S TWO-HIT SERMON MAKES IT FIVE RUNNING FOR HOUSTON: THE WHITE STOCKINGS SUBDUED, 4 TO 1

Hunter Brown threw seven shutout innings of two-hit ball, walking none and striking out seven, and Isaac Paredes drove home three — his sixth-inning single, compounded by a fielding error in right, clearing the sacks in one stroke. Hader closed for his twelfth save. The Houston nine have won five straight and stand but two games off the West's summit; the White Stockings, beaten twice running, see their Central lead over stumbling Cleveland hold at a game and a half only by the grace of the Guardians' own miseries.

THE NURSERY CARRIES THE CREAM CITY: ROOKIES PRATT AND LARA DRIVE HOME THREE APIECE, ROCKIES REPELLED, 8 TO 5

Milwaukee's first-year men did the whole of the heavy lifting: Cooper Pratt went three-for-three with his second homer and three runs driven in, and Luis Lara matched the three with a sacrifice fly and a two-run double, adding a stolen dash home upon a pickoff gone astray. Gasser gave six solid innings and Megill sealed his seventeenth save — though not before the Mountain men, twice given up for dead, put three across in the ninth on singles by Jake McCarthy and Kyle Karros. At five-and-sixty wins the Suds men remain six clear of the Cubs atop the National Central.

PALLANTE PERMITS TWO HITS, THE SIXTH INNING SUPPLIES SIX RUNS, AND THE REDBIRDS BLANK CINCINNATI, 7 TO 0

Andre Pallante gave seven innings of two-hit, eight-strikeout craft for his eleventh win, and his mates broke the thing open with six in the sixth — Iván Herrera's two-run double, Blaze Jordan's two-run single, and a run squeezed home on Nathan Church's bunt among the plunder. Hunter Greene bore the brunt, seven runs charged in five and a third. The Redbirds thus draw within a game and a half of the wild-card line; the Cincinnatis, so lately winners of three straight, were reminded how quickly July gives and takes.

TUCKER STRIKES AGAIN, THE AMAZINS STUMBLE OVER THEMSELVES, AND THE TROLLEY DODGERS TAKE A FOURTH RUNNING, 4 TO 3

Kyle Tucker homered for a second consecutive evening, Andy Pages scored once upon a passed ball and singled home another, and Yoshinobu Yamamoto ground through six innings of one-run labor for his eleventh win — his fifteenth quality start of the season, the wires note. The Amazins made it close with Tyrone Taylor's two-run double in the eighth, and Stewart recorded his first save amid the tension. At seven-and-sixty wins against eight-and-thirty defeats the champions own the game's finest ledger; their hosts, losers of three straight and twenty games below evenness, learned of graver news besides (see the Infirmary Ward above).

ELEVEN STRAIGHT DEFEATS FOR THE FISH: FRANCE DRIVES HOME THREE, AND THE FRIARS FEAST, 7 TO 2

Miami's July ordeal stretched to an eleventh consecutive loss, the longest active suffering in the game. Ty France delivered a two-run single in the third and another run-scoring knock in the eighth, Jase Bowen added a two-run single behind him, and Sears took the decision with three and a third hitless relief innings. The Friars have won two running to draw level with the very Fish they beat — both clubs now two-and-fifty and three-and-fifty — though only one of the pair is trending toward daylight.

DEVERS AND LEE SUPPLY SEVEN RUNS BY THE FOURTH: THE BAY NINE MAUL THE HALOS, 9 TO 2

A night after winning at the last gasp, the Bay nine dispensed with suspense altogether: Jung Hoo Lee struck a three-run homer in a five-run third, and Rafael Devers — three-for-four with four runs driven in — capped a four-run fourth with his two-and-twentieth, a three-run line drive to left-center. Robbie Ray gave six shutout innings for his ninth win. The Halos' consolations were a run scraped from a double play and a bases-loaded walk; the Anaheim men have now dropped three straight, while San Francisco banks consecutive victories in a season that has offered few.

FOURTEEN AND TWO-THIRDS INNINGS OF FAMINE, THEN RODEN: THE MINNESOTANS EDGE THE WHITE ELEPHANTS, 2 TO 0

The Twin Cities nine had gone fourteen innings and two-thirds without a run when Alan Roden lined a two-run single in the sixth, cashing Josh Bell and Royce Lewis — and that, in a game of this economy, was the whole of the argument. Rogers took the win with two clean relief innings and Gómez sealed his thirteenth save. The Minnesotans thus stay within two games of the wild-card scrum; the White Elephants, blanked a night after blanking Minnesota themselves, head home wondering where the runs went.

A full Sunday bill of fifteen awaits, but the day's first pilgrimage is to Cooperstown, where Beltrán, Jones, and Kent take their bronze this afternoon. On the diamonds: Cleveland carries its four-game stagger into Tampa Bay at a quarter past noon, Messick against Rasmussen; the terrifying Misiorowski — ten wins, an earned-run average of 1.57, and one hundred seventy-three strikeouts — receives Colorado at Milwaukee; and the evening's jewel sends Warren against Sánchez, he of twelve wins, when the Yankees and Phils conclude their quarrel at twenty past seven. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
