News Around the League

THE HIT KING PARDONED: FROM THE PRESIDENTIAL AEROPLANE, A 'COMPLETE AND TOTAL' CLEMENCY FOR PETE ROSE, SEVEN-AND-THIRTY YEARS AFTER THE BAN AND TWO YEARS BEYOND THE GRAVE — THE TAX CASE IS STRUCK FROM THE FEDERAL LEDGER, BUT COOPERSTOWN KEEPS ITS OWN BOOKS

The winter's business paused Thursday for a dispatch from an altitude the wire rarely files from. President Trump, flying home from Asia aboard Air Force One, announced upon his social channels that he had granted a posthumous pardon to Pete Rose, the game's all-time leader in base hits, better than two years after the man's death. "I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND," the President wrote. The gesture was first promised in March of 2025, to be signed "over the next few weeks"; the pen took roughly twenty months to find the paper. What precisely the pardon covers was not specified — asked for particulars, a White House official pointed to the tax charges.3

Those charges are the one entry a president's pen can reach. On April 20, 1990, Rose pleaded guilty to two counts of filing false income-tax returns, admitting he had failed to report $354,968 across four years; that July, Judge S. Arthur Spiegel of Cincinnati sentenced him to five months in federal prison, a $50,000 fine and 1,000 hours of community service. Rose repaid the revenue men $366,042, reported to a minimum-security facility in Illinois in August of 1990, and was released the following January. That is the case now wiped from the books.3

The game's own sentence was never the President's to commute. Rose accepted a permanent banishment in August 1989, after the investigator John M. Dowd concluded he had bet numerous times on his own Cincinnati club while playing for and managing it from 1985 to 1987 — and that ledger was already settled by a different hand: Commissioner Manfred ruled in May 2025 that permanent ineligibility ends at death, a stroke that restored seventeen men, Rose and Shoeless Joe Jackson the most notable of the company. The road to bronze thus runs on Cooperstown's clock alone, and the earliest either man could be inducted is 2028. The Herald observes only this: it has spent the week in a Brooklyn gallery where the game's ancient quarrel with the wagering man is still being litigated at public expense, and the reader may weigh the week's two gambling dispatches in whatever order he pleases.3

THE EXCHANGE DESK: THE ROTATION PRINTED WEDNESDAY IS CROSSED OUT BY THURSDAY NIGHT — CHICAGO SENDS CASTILLO AND MARTIN TO THE MOUNTAIN MEN FOR TWO PROSPECTS, DENVER ASSUMES THE WHOLE OF THE VETERAN'S WAGE, AND GETZ PROMISES MORE BUSINESS IN THE COMING WEEKS

When these columns set down the South Side rotation account on Wednesday — Burke, Martin, Castillo, the young Schultz, Taylor — the ink was scarcely dry before the ledger crossed two names out. On Thursday the White Sox introduced their new man Dustin May at a press conference, the steward Chris Getz spoke of getting back on the telephone the moment it ended, and by nightfall he had: Luis Castillo and Davis Martin, both right-handers, were traded to Colorado for the left-hander Sean Sullivan and Douglas Veliz, a 17-year-old catcher out of the Dominican Summer League.54

What the Mountain men have bought is experience, at full freight. Castillo, who turns 34 next month, is a three-time All-Star — twice for the Reds, once for Seattle — with a career ledger of 88-95 and a 3.75 earned-run average across 272 games and ten seasons, an above-average figure in his rookie year of 2017 and every season from 2019 through 2025 before a rough spell followed his deadline trade to Chicago; Colorado takes on the whole of his $24.15 million wage for 2027. Martin, 30 in January, went 9-8 with a 3.95 this past season after opening it 8-1 with a 2.00 through 11 starts, remains under club control through 2030, and is reunited with the new Rockies pitching coach Ethan Katz, who held the same post in Chicago. The need could hardly be plainer: starting pitching is Denver's declared first priority, the staff having lately owned the game's highest earned-run average both in sum (5.59) and from its rotation (6.28), and with Freeland, Sugano and Quintana all exploring free agency, Ryan Feltner had stood as the most experienced starter on the premises. It is the second time the DePodesta-Byrnes front office has bought pitching from this same shop, after the summer's Doyle trade.4

Chicago's side of the ledger is the future and the purse. Sullivan, 24, a second-round selection out of Wake Forest in 2023, made his debut in June and took his lumps — 0-3 with an 8.87 in six starts before shoulder and elbow trouble ended his summer — then steadied himself in the Fall League at 2-1 with a 3.07; the move is a homecoming of sorts, the big lefty having begun his collegiate career at Northwestern. Veliz, signed out of Venezuela for $50,000, batted .300 in 29 games on the island circuit. The savings on Castillo's wage, the club allows, will be reinvested in another arm, and Getz put the winter's intent plainly: "We're going to look for opportunities in the coming weeks. We're going to continue the pursuit." The parting note belongs to Martin, who leaves as the longest-tenured White Sox man by service time — proof, if November needed any, that the hot stove honors no seniority.54

THE SKUBAL REGISTER: THE FOURTH AUDIENCE IS HELD AND A DARK HORSE DECLARES ITSELF — SAN DIEGO SITS WITH THE PRIZE ARM AND IS STYLED 'ULTRA-AGGRESSIVE' WITH NEW MONEY BEHIND PRELLER, WHILE THE WIRE'S SOURCES STILL MAKE QUEENS THE FAVORITE

The register these columns keep on the winter's grandest courtship duly records its fourth audience: the Mission nine sat with Tarik Skubal on Wednesday, completing the round these pages forecast — the Mets on Monday, the Dodgers and the Maple Leaf nine on Tuesday, San Diego to follow — with two of the six sittings booked still belonging to hosts unnamed. The insider Jon Heyman supplies the morning's color: the club's new ownership will be "uber-committed" behind the general manager A.J. Preller, the wire styles the pursuit "ultra-aggressive," and while Heyman stops short of calling San Diego a frontrunner, he allows the club "a shot to upset" the Mets, Toronto and the Dodgers. The complication is arithmetical — the Mission nine must rebuild very nearly an entire rotation besides, and keep an interest in the bat of Seiya Suzuki into the bargain.76

The handicapping, for all that, holds its shape. ESPN's sources continue to reckon the Mets the favorite as the pursuit quickens, and the counsel on timing stands as yesterday's columns had it: no decision is imminent, and the weekend may well pass without one. The figures under discussion remain historic — the largest guarantee ever written for a pitcher is reckoned to be in danger — and so the register stays open, with the collective agreement's clock ticking audibly beneath every sitting.86

THE INK DESK: THE METROPOLITANS SHOP IN JAPAN FOR LEFT-HANDED HELP — DARWINZON HERNANDEZ, LATELY OF THE SOFTBANK HAWKS, COMES BACK ACROSS THE PACIFIC ON A THREE-YEAR WAGER OF TWELVE MILLIONS

The Metropolitans made the day's quietest purchase and perhaps its shrewdest. By the account of a source, the Mets have agreed with the left-handed reliever Darwinzon Hernández on a contract of three years and $12 million — the club not yet confirming — to bring the Venezuelan back across the Pacific. Hernández, who turns 30 in December, has spent the last four seasons with the Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks of Japan, pitching to a 2.46 earned-run average across 131 and two-thirds innings, this past year a 1.48 with 52 strikeouts in 42 and two-thirds. His last major-league work came for Boston in 2022, the close of a four-season Fenway tenure that read 5.06 with 133 strikeouts against 73 walks in 85 and a third innings — power stuff forever at war with the strike zone, and whether the Japanese seasons have ended that war is the question the wager turns on.9

The reasoning was stated on the record weeks ago. "We're at a point in the industry where we've got to be able to get left-handed hitters out," said the president of baseball operations David Stearns at the Las Vegas meetings — and the arithmetic backs him, left-handed batters having taken 49 percent of all big-league plate appearances last season, the highest share since at least 1961. The Flushing bullpen, meanwhile, was stripped of its left hands at the summer deadline, Minter sent to Minnesota and Raley to Philadelphia; Hernández arrives as the likeliest southpaw in the 2027 relief corps, and he and the closer Devin Williams now stand as the only Mets relievers on guaranteed money. Nor is the eastern shopping done: a video audience is planned with Kaima Taira, the Seibu right-hander posted off a league-best 1.36 — the same Taira the White Sox eye, as Wednesday's columns noted.96

THE EXCHANGE DESK, CONTINUED: HALF A DOZEN CLUBS AND MORE RING ON LOWE WHILE BOTH OLD EMPLOYERS KEEP THE DOOR AJAR, BUBIC'S MENDED ARM DRAWS A CROWD, THE NORTH SIDERS SCOUT SATO AND DURAN, AND SEATTLE GOES SHOPPING FOR ITS GARDEN

The market around Brandon Lowe has filled quickly. Three days after the second baseman declined his $23.15 million tender — with a fresh silver bat under his arm, as these columns recorded Wednesday — the insider Jon Paul Morosi reports "very strong interest" from more than half a dozen clubs in a man coming off a .260 season of 35 home runs and 96 driven in. Both former employers linger at the door: the Pittsburgh steward Ben Cherington says his club remains in touch and open to a reunion, and the Tampa men, who sold Lowe to the Smoky City only last winter, are reckoned open to taking him back. Toronto is linked in multiple reports, the Yankees have checked in, and whoever signs him pays the draft-pick toll, Pittsburgh collecting a premium selection for its trouble.6

The left-hander Kris Bubic draws a comparable crowd from the pitching aisle. The surgeon's catalogue is lengthy — the Tommy John operation in 2023, a rotator-cuff strain in 2025, elbow soreness this past season — but so is the ledger beside it: a 2.90 earned-run average across the last three years, an All-Star turn as a Kansas City starter in 2025, and October work from the Dodgers' bullpen after a deadline trade. The Athletic reports interest from contenders and non-contenders alike; Morosi expects him signed as a starter and names the Royals, Tigers, Cubs, Birds, Toronto and the Cardinals as sensible shorter-term fits, with Baltimore's want sharpened by Rogers' free agency and the Capital nine making their own inquiries.6

On the North Side the winter's shopping list runs long — Imanaga, Boyd, Gausman, Holmes, Peterson and Rea are all at liberty from the rotation, and neither Happ nor Suzuki is reckoned likely to return to the garden. The Athletic's Patrick Mooney reports the Cubs have "extensively scouted" the Japanese slugger Teruaki Sato — the same Sato that Toronto covets, as Thursday's columns noted — and the club has evaluated Boston's Jarren Duran as a change-of-scenery addition besides, a possibility these pages mapped Wednesday that grows likelier now that the Bauers purchase has crowded the Fenway outfield.6

And in Seattle, the rotation shop having been declared shut in yesterday's lead, the buying turns to the grass. The club seeks a corner outfielder, bullpen help and a versatile infielder, the steward Dipoto calling another right-handed bat "ideal"; the linked names include Happ — four consecutive Gold Gloves in left, and old ties to the new Seattle manager Jason Kanzler, lately the Cubs' farm director — and Suzuki, with Donovan reckoned for left field now that Arozarena has gone to market, and face-to-face sittings with several free-agent relievers already held.6

THE WINTER ISLANDS: THE SPRING'S ITALIAN SENSATION TAKES THE EAGLES' HELM — CERVELLI IS NAMED MANAGER OF THE AGUILAS CIBAEÑAS, HOURS AFTER A WALK-OFF LOSS TO THE SELFSAME LICEY THAT COURTS GUERRERO

The island chapter gains a new character. The Águilas Cibaeñas of Santiago announced Thursday morning the hiring of Francisco Cervelli as manager, the former big-league catcher replacing the veteran skipper Luis "Pipe" Urueta — and the timing told the whole story, the announcement coming hours after a walk-off loss to the Tigres del Licey at the Quisqueya, a two-run drive over the wall by the Arizona farmhand Ángel Ortiz, that left the Eagles last in the league at 9 and 16. It is Cervelli's first command in winter-league play, and his brief is nothing less than the island's proudest rivalry: the Águilas hold 22 championships since the league's founding in 1951, second only to Licey's 24, and have not raised a banner since the winter of 2020-21.10

The man arrives with the year's best managing story already in his pocket. Cervelli caught thirteen big-league seasons — a ring with the 2009 Yankees, then the Pirates, Braves and Marlins — served as catching instructor on the 2022 Padres club that reached the league championship series, and took the Italian national post fourteen months before this spring's World Classic, honoring the country of his father while the land of his own birth, Venezuela, went on to take the trophy. His Azzurri went 5 and 1 — a sweep of their Houston pool with upsets of the United States and Mexico, a quarterfinal win over Puerto Rico, and a semifinal exit against the eventual champions. The roster he inherits is no winter hobby outfit, counting the Yankees' Jasson Domínguez, the San Diego right-hander Randy Vásquez and the Arizona reliever Justin Martínez among its hands. And the reader of Thursday's columns will note the standings' finest subplot: the club Cervelli must now chase is the same Licey whose blue Vladimir Guerrero Jr. waits on Toronto's blessing to wear.10

A presidential pen scratching at a 36-year-old tax case, a rotation unmade the day after this paper printed it, and a new skipper for the winter islands — the Friday columns range farther afield than November usually permits, and every mile of it is set down above. The forward ledger was opened before a word was written, per the house custom, and tells its patient story: the camps' reporting dates remain unposted, so the count runs to the first rung bearing a date — the spring exhibition openers of Friday, the 19th of February, 91 mornings from this one, the ledger already printing the matchups themselves, the remade Rockies at the Desert nine among them — with Opening Night to follow on Wednesday, the 24th of March, 124 mornings off.2 The nearer columns hold their stations: the collective agreement has 11 mornings left to live, Cooperstown's committee of sixteen reports in 16, the Winter Meetings convene at San Diego with the Rule 5 drafting on December 9, 19 mornings from this one, and the writers' envelope waits for the 19th of January, 60 mornings off. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
