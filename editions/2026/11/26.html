News Around the League

THE LABOR QUESTION: FIVE MORNINGS REMAIN — THE OWNERS SPEND THE EVE OF THE FEAST IN A FEDERAL COURTHOUSE, ASKING A JUDGE TO PRONOUNCE THEIR LOCKOUT LAWFUL BEFORE IT EXISTS, AND THE UNION CALLS THE SUIT COMPLETELY FRIVOLOUS

The owners went to law on the eve of the feast. On Wednesday — the last day the courthouse sits before the holiday weekend — Major League Baseball filed a class-action complaint in the federal court for the Southern District of New York, asking a judge to declare in advance that the lockout the whole game expects on the first of December would be lawful under the antitrust statutes, and would remain lawful even should the players' association renounce its standing as the men's bargaining agent. The league styles the filing purely defensive. "Today's filing is a defensive measure simply intended to keep the parties at the bargaining table to work towards a new collective bargaining agreement and a full 2027 season," said the spokesman Glen Caplin, who accused the union of "pretending to 'disclaim'" its status in order to sue under the antitrust laws — a maneuver the league calls the union's "nuclear option," and which it asked the court to "proactively disarm."3

The mechanics, for the reader who keeps the docket beside the box scores: the complaint seeks three declarations — that a lockout is protected by the labor exemption to the antitrust laws and would remain so after any disclaimer; that even unexempted it would be a reasonable restraint, temporary and designed to drive the parties to terms; and that the federal courts lack jurisdiction to enjoin it at all, Congress having long ago taken them "out of the labor injunction business." The playbook the league says it fears is a real one with a long pedigree: the disclaimer of interest — quicker and less formal than decertification, wanting no election of the men, only a letter from the leadership — has been run by the union's interim executive director Bruce Meyer and its outside counsel Jeffrey Kessler against the other leagues for three decades, football in 1989 and 2011, basketball in 1987, 1995 and 2011, hockey in 2012. By the league's own account the union could disclaim as early as Monday or Tuesday next — on the agreement's very deathbed — and the suit, which seeks no damages and does not interrupt the bargaining, was filed where the league means the fight to stay, in Manhattan, and not in "some unrelated location cherry-picked by the Union."3

The union required one paragraph. "The lawsuit filed today by MLB is completely frivolous," its statement read. "We will respond appropriately, but MLB's decision to run to court in the middle of negotiations rather than bargain in good faith is incredibly disappointing." The league answers that it is the union that has refused to so much as consider the cap-and-floor design, holding its antitrust threat as coercion; the Herald, whose sympathies on matters of the purse are of long record, notes only the novelty of the posture — the employer asking a judge to certify the padlock before the door is shut, and suing the workmen, in effect, for a thing they have not yet done. Both parties, it should be said plainly, remain at the table. The agreement expires at one minute before midnight on the first of December, 5 mornings from this one, and the week's furious ledger, set down below, says what the market thinks of the deadline.3

THE INK DESK: THE ROTATION SHELVES CLEAR BY THE ARMFUL — GAUSMAN, SIX-AND-THIRTY COME JANUARY, TAKES FIVE-AND-TWENTY MILLIONS TO DETROIT, CLEVELAND KEEPS ITS RESTORED WANDERER GRIFFIN FOR THREE YEARS AND EIGHT-AND-FORTY, AND DENVER SIGNS TWO GROUND-BALL LEFT-HANDERS BEFORE SUPPER

"We're going to continue to add arms," the Detroit president of baseball operations Scott Harris said this week, and these columns barely finish recording one purchase before the next wants ink. On Wednesday the Detroits agreed with Kevin Gausman on one year and $25 million, pending a physical and unconfirmed by the club. The right-hander turns 36 on the 6th of January, and what Detroit is buying is the rarest commodity in the modern game — innings on schedule: 175 or better in eight of his last ten full seasons (and one out shy in 2022), five consecutive seasons without a visit to the injured list, six seasons of 3.0 wins above replacement or more. The 2026 ledger between Toronto and the Cubs reads 9-12 with a 4.51, but the underwriting is sounder than the surface — a 3.74 fielding-independent figure against a career 3.61, with 9.3 strikeouts per nine and nearly four strikeouts for every walk, his best such marks since 2023, and 14 quality starts besides. The reunion is personal: his 14-win, 5.2-WAR season for the 2021 Giants came under a general manager named Scott Harris, and his history on the Detroit grounds runs seven quality starts in seven visits at a 1.93. He joins Valdez atop a young company of Melton, Jobe, Ryan, Olson and the newly arrived Gil — and the connoisseur of the wire's mortality will note that Toronto's "mutual interest" in a Gausman reunion was reported the very day Detroit signed him.414

Cleveland, whose purchase of a year of Bubic's mended arm these columns recorded only yesterday, spent Wednesday keeping its own: the Forest City nine are re-signing Foster Griffin for three years and $48 million, by a source's account, the club not confirming. The file is the season's best serial. A first-round choice of Kansas City in 2014, Griffin made his big-league debut on the 27th of July, 2020, and his elbow gave out in that very outing; seven appearances were his whole account through 2022, and he spent 2023 through 2025 pitching in Japan. He returned on a one-year wager with Washington and, at 31, made the All-Star team — a 3.06 across 129 and a third innings and 22 starts — was sold to Cleveland at the August deadline, posted an ugly 5.19 there that one eight-run wreck against Baltimore largely explains (3.73 across his other seven starts), and pitched October creditably, a 3.48 in three appearances with the Game 1 win of the league championship series against the Rays among them. The president of baseball operations Chris Antonetti called him, at the deadline, "one of the best starters in baseball"; the seven-pitch left-hander now joins Bubic, the strikeout king Williams and young Messick — and here too the wire's shelf-life is instructive, for Wednesday's rumor columns had Toronto eyeing him, and by nightfall he was off every list.514

And in Denver the winter that these columns observed Monday at least produces transactions now produces them in pairs. The Mountain men agreed with David Peterson on two years and $32 million and with Patrick Sandoval on three and $42 million, by multiple sources, neither announced — this on the heels of last week's Castillo-and-Martin freight from the White Sox, so that a staff which ran the game's worst starting figure, a 6.28, through a fourth consecutive season of 100 losses, has conjured some four-fifths of a rotation inside a fortnight. Peterson is the hometown entry — raised at Aurora, a first-rounder out of Oregon by the Mets in 2017, an All-Star in 2025, and lately the owner of a redemptive half-season: demoted to the Queens bullpen in May at a 6.09, traded to the Cubs at June's end, and thereafter 3.70 across 16 appearances with three earned runs or fewer in 12 of his final 13 starts. The trade of both men is the same trade — Peterson's career ground-ball rate stands at 50.7 percent and his fastball at a modest 92.3, Sandoval at his best living likewise on weak contact, his 2025 lost entire to elbow surgery — which is to say the Rockies have stopped shopping for strikeouts the altitude will not sell them, and started buying the ball on the ground.6

THE INK DESK, SMALLER TYPE: ROBERT COMES BACK TO BALTIMORE AT TWELVE MILLIONS ON A WAGER OF HEALTH, BOHM TAKES THE THIRD-BASE HOLE IN KANSAS CITY, SÁNCHEZ CARRIES HIS WALKS AND HIS NEW RING TO MIAMI, AND THREE BULLPENS DO THEIR MARKETING BEFORE THE HOLIDAY

The Baltimores, who declined Luis Robert Jr.'s $20 million option at October's end, re-signed the man Wednesday evening for one year and $12 million, by a source's account, the club not confirming. He is 29, and he wore Baltimore flannels for precisely three games — a lumbar herniation having cost him nearly three months of a season begun with the Mets after January's trade from the White Sox, a .223 average and 10 home runs his whole Queens account, before waivers carried him to the Chesapeake in August and a hamstring ended his year at 2-for-11. What $12 million buys is the memory of 2023 — .264 with 38 home runs, 36 doubles and an .857 on-base-plus-slugging, an All-Star and Silver Slugger season, in a career that opened with a Gold Glove and the runner-up's share of the Rookie of the Year vote — and another right-handed bat for an outfield where the left-swinging Beavers and Cowser had only O'Neill for balance. The club that crowned Peralta on Tuesday, as yesterday's columns recorded, is assembling its winter at speed.7

In Kansas City the hole the club dug itself a week ago is filled: Alec Bohm, late of Philadelphia, takes two years and $20 million to play the third base the Garcia freight to Seattle emptied — the six-player exchange that brought Miller to town, as these columns have recorded. Bohm is 30, an All-Star of 2024, and a run-producer by trade: 88 driven in this past season though he started but twice in the order's top third, against a career best of 97. The slash line sagged to .249/.315/.383, his faintest since 2021, but the contact instruments call him unlucky — an expected average of .265 against the printed .249 — and he closed at .298 after the All-Star break. He hits right-handed, which suits a club whose first baseman Pasquantino swings left; the platoon suggests itself.8

Miami's purchase is a connoisseur's: Gary Sánchez, one year and $6.5 million. The catcher is 33 until Wednesday next, twelve seasons deep, and arrives wearing a World Series ring from Milwaukee, where he hit .209 but walked at a 16.7 percent rate — third in the game among men with 250 trips to the plate, behind only Kurtz and Trout — and slugged 12 home runs behind Contreras, a 112 OPS+ in the sum. The power remains the resume: 201 career home runs, one for every 15.68 at-bats, tenth among active men of 3,000 plate appearances, wedged between Olson and Soto on that roll. He backs the sophomore Mack on a club that ranked 23rd in home runs, and he will take his swings as the designated hitter when a left-hander is up.9

The bullpens, too, did their marketing before the feast. The San Diego nine made their first notable stroke of the whole winter, three years and a reported $21 million for the left-hander JoJo Romero — seven seasons at a 3.51, a 2.07 for the 2025 Cardinals, a ring with Milwaukee this autumn and eight postseason innings of one run with 11 strikeouts, the lone run being Cronenworth's go-ahead blow in Game 3 of the division series — struck, the reader may relish, by a man of the very club that now signs him; whether the purchase portends Morejon's departure the wire cannot say. The Twin Cities nine agreed to two years with Kevin Ginkel, a career Diamondback of 341 appearances at 3.91, whose 2026 ran 72 strikeouts against 17 walks in 67 and two-thirds innings. And the Washingtons spent one year and $5 million on the tallest lottery ticket at liberty: A.J. Puk, 6-foot-7, who has not thrown a big-league pitch since April of 2025 — an elbow mended by internal brace, then a shoulder that swallowed 2026 whole — but whose 2022-24, a 3.38 with 242 strikeouts across 194 and a third innings, was the work of one of the game's best left-handed relievers.101112

THE PACIFIC DESK: THE LAUREL CATCHES UP TO THE FORTUNE — SATO IS NAMED THE CENTRAL LEAGUE'S MOST VALUABLE FOR A SECOND YEAR RUNNING, BY 213 FIRST-PLACE VOTES, SOME EIGHTEEN HOURS AFTER SIGNING HIS CHICAGO PAPER

The laurel has caught up to the fortune. At the Japanese game's awards ceremony Wednesday evening in Japan, Teruaki Sato was formally named the Central League's Most Valuable Player for the second consecutive season — 213 first-place votes, his Hanshin teammate Haruto Takahashi, the newly minted Sawamura laureate, a distant second at 34 — roughly eighteen hours after agreeing to the six-year, $120 million Chicago contract that led yesterday's edition of these columns. The season so honored is the one Chicago bought: the Central League's Triple Crown at .306 with 39 home runs and 106 driven in, 40 doubles and 68 walks beside a 1.017 on-base-plus-slugging across 143 games, following the 40-homer campaign of 2025 that won him the laurel the first time. The Herald admires the tidy order of operations — the fortune Tuesday, the crown Wednesday — and records the plain sum of it: the North Side establishment has purchased the reigning everything of the Japanese game.13

THE EXCHANGE DESK: WHAT THE FEAST FINDS STILL ON THE TABLE — ONE 'BIG THREE' OF TORONTO, PHILADELPHIA AND SAN DIEGO CIRCLES BOTH SUZUKI AND AROZARENA, MOREJON'S PRICE MAY SET A LEFT-HANDED RECORD, AND KING IS STILL TIPPED TO SIGN INSIDE THE WEEK

With Sato sold, the grandest bats at liberty are Seiya Suzuki and Randy Arozarena, and the wire's Jon Paul Morosi finds the same three establishments circling both sluggers: the Torontos, the Philadelphias and the San Diego nine. The fits are not mysterious. Toronto hit the fewest home runs in the majors this past season and Suzuki has struck 58 across the last two — Sportsnet's Ben Nicholson-Smith reckons an offer is probably coming if not already made, and the man would stand a third Japanese star beside Okamoto and the newly signed Taira — while Arozarena's 53 over the same span answers the identical want. Philadelphia seeks a right-handed bat to flank Harper and Schwarber, the need sharpened Wednesday when Bohm signed in Kansas City, as the smaller type above records; and San Diego could set either man in left beside Merrill and Tatis.14

The arms column is shorter and dearer. Adrian Morejon remains the best reliever at liberty, the Yankees and the Torontos both engaged by Morosi's account, a San Diego return not expected — the Romero purchase above reading naturally as cover — and the eventual contract could approach a record for a left-handed reliever in both length and yearly wage; the Bronx men weigh one large commitment against spreading the money across their other wants, a deliberation these columns recorded Tuesday. Michael King stands where yesterday's edition left him, the best starter unsigned and tipped by Morosi to find a home before the week is out, with Toronto — "busy and motivated" for pitching even after Taira, a six-man rotation contemplated to keep the newcomer's once-a-week habits — holding a radar of King, Mahle, Holmes and Martinez. The reader may take the measure of a Wednesday rumor's life expectancy from the fate of the list's other two names: Griffin re-signed in Cleveland and Gausman signed in Detroit before the report's ink was dry. And in Baltimore, by The Athletic's account, the next starter may arrive by trade, the club's glut of infielders and shelf of pitching prospects reckoned capital enough for a significant strike.14

THE ANGELENO DESK: OHTANI, ASKED WHY HE STILL INSISTS ON DOING TWO MEN'S WORK, ANSWERS THAT IT IS THE POST FROM WHICH HE HELPS MOST — AND VOWS TO REPAY THE YEAR'S FRUSTRATION WITH INTEREST

Speaking with the Japanese press by video on Wednesday, Shohei Ohtani settled the question the autumn's odder interviews had tried to raise: he remains a two-way man. "That's the position where I can help the team the most and make the best use of my strengths," he said, a man who once found the hitter-only life lonely answering as if the question puzzled him — and of the winter ahead: "I want to make this a good period to make up for this year's frustration next year." The frustration has a precise shape. The Angelenos' bid for a third consecutive championship died in a four-game sweep by Milwaukee in the league championship series, Ohtani's three-run clout in Game 4 arriving in a 6-4 loss, the bat having come good only at the end — "a little too late," by his own audit. "Of course I feel frustrated," he allowed.15

The season the infirmary interrupted had opened like his finest: 8-2 with a 1.79 earned-run average across 14 starts before a left knee and a right biceps, among other complaints, ended the pitching after the 3rd of July, while the bat ran to .275 with 30 home runs and 81 driven in across 135 games around a September spell on the shelf. He is 32, and he has not strung together a full two-way season since 2023 — the shoulder of the 2024 World Series, then the Classic-shortened winter, having each taken their toll on the ramp. The next ramp is itself unsettled: he and the club weigh several plans for Opening Day readiness, and the labor agreement's expiration on the first of December adds its own variable, a pitcher who throws too much in a stoppage buying nothing but fatigue. The aim he named is the only one that establishment recognizes: "The real battle is in October, so whether I can get there in the right spot is the battle ahead."15

A Thanksgiving paper, and the fullest eve-of-feast ledger of the winter: the owners went to law to have their padlock blessed in advance, nine clubs signed ten men between them, and the eastern league pinned its laurel on the man Chicago bought — the whole of it set down above. The forward ledger was read before any word was set, by the house custom, and stands as it has all week: the camps' reporting dates remain unposted, so the count runs to the first rung bearing a date — the spring exhibition openers of Friday, the 19th of February, 85 mornings from this one, with Opening Night to follow on Wednesday, the 24th of March, 118 mornings off.2 The nearer columns: the republic sits to its feast this very morning, and the market, as the reader has seen, did its shopping before the bird went in; the collective agreement has 5 mornings left to live; Cooperstown's committee of sixteen reports in 10; the Winter Meetings convene at San Diego with the Rule 5 drafting on December 9, 13 mornings from this one; and the writers' envelope waits for the 19th of January, 54 mornings off. Until the morrow's dispatch, keep your scorecards near and your enthusiasms nearer.
